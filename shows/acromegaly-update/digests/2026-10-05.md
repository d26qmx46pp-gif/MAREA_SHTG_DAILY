## Part A — Acromegaly research

**MRI-based radiomics prediction of somatostatin receptor ligand response in acromegaly: an automated pipeline study**
Ruiz S, Mateu-Estivill R, Izquierdo-Gómez A, López-Máñez M, et al. — *Journal of the Endocrine Society*
[PMID 42751035](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42751035/) · [DOI 10.1210/jendso/bvag198](https://doi.org/10.1210/jendso/bvag198)
The team built a fully automated pipeline that finds and outlines pituitary tumors on MRI and then extracts imaging features to predict who will respond to somatostatin receptor ligands (first-line injectable drugs). Using 267 scans across three datasets, segmentation reached a Dice score of about 0.80. In 49 treated patients, the best model, a simple three-feature logistic regression, classified 39 correctly (about 80%) with an AUC near 0.80. It is a small pilot without external validation, but it points toward choosing drugs from baseline MRI.

**Familial acromegaly with a novel PRKAR1A splice-acceptor variant and marked intrafamilial phenotypic variability**
Jha V, Barry S, Chatterjee D, Kaur V, et al. — *JCEM Case Reports*
[PMID 42787346](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42787346/) · [DOI 10.1210/jcemcr/luag244](https://doi.org/10.1210/jcemcr/luag244)
A 33-year-old man with long-standing acromegaly also had skin and heart myxomas and testicular Sertoli cell tumors, features of Carney complex. He reached biochemical remission after transsphenoidal surgery. His sister had childhood-onset acromegaly and persistent mildly elevated IGF-1, while their mother, who carries the same novel PRKAR1A variant, has had no endocrine disease to age 62. The authors argue for genetic testing in early-onset or familial acromegaly.

**Proteomic profile of bone in patients with acromegaly: new insights from a pilot exploratory study**
Demarchis L, Chiloiro S, Mattogno PP, Cicchinelli M, et al. — *Journal of Endocrinological Investigation*
[PMID 42329359](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42329359/) · [DOI 10.1007/s40618-026-02936-x](https://doi.org/10.1007/s40618-026-02936-x)
Mass spectrometry on bone samples from five acromegaly patients and five with non-secreting pituitary tumors found 18 differentially abundant proteins (six up, twelve down). Profilin-1 and a periostin isoform correlated positively with GH and IGF-1, and caveolin-1 correlated negatively. With only ten samples this is hypothesis-generating, but it offers clues to why bone in acromegaly is fragile despite normal-looking density.

**Lens densitometry alterations in acromegaly: Quantitative insights from Scheimpflug imaging and hormonal correlation**
Karataş ME, Karataş G, Çakır SD — *Indian Journal of Ophthalmology*
[PMID 42826047](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42826047/) · [DOI 10.4103/IJO.IJO_2445_25](https://doi.org/10.4103/IJO.IJO_2445_25)
In 40 patients versus 40 healthy volunteers, lens density measured by Scheimpflug imaging was significantly higher in acromegaly in all measured zones, and moderately correlated with GH and IGF-1. It suggests chronic GH excess may affect the lens, though this is a cross-sectional study with no clinical outcome data.

**Resolved GH excess and arginine vasopressin deficiency following pituitary apoplexy**
Mani B, Stevenson CB, Swauger S — *JCEM Case Reports*
[PMID 42798838](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42798838/) · [DOI 10.1210/jcemcr/luag250](https://doi.org/10.1210/jcemcr/luag250)
A 10-year-old boy with rapid growth and very high IGF-1 had GH excess that failed to suppress on testing. A hemorrhagic pituitary lesion was found; surgery was avoided, and over 13 months the hematoma retracted, his vasopressin deficiency resolved and IGF-1 and growth velocity normalized. The report shows GH excess can spontaneously remit after apoplexy and that tall stature in a prepubertal child warrants a GH evaluation.

## Part B — Rare Disease Industry News

**UniQure tumbles with latest data for Huntington's gene therapy**
BioPharma Dive (Jacob Bell), September 29, 2026 — https://www.biopharmadive.com/news/uniqure-huntingtons-four-year-data-gene-therapy-results/831611/
Four-year data for uniQure's one-time Huntington's gene therapy AMT-130 showed a 44% slowing of progression on a combined cognition and motor scale versus external controls, which was not statistically significant. A daily-activities measure showed a 61% slowing. Those numbers are lower than the three-year results (80% and 67%), raising durability questions, and the stock fell about 40%. The company has filed for accelerated approval, with a decision possibly about eight months out. See also: STAT News (Adam Feuerstein), https://www.statnews.com/2026/09/29/uniqure-huntingtons-gene-therapy-slows-disease-progression-four-years/
*Why it matters for rare disease developers:* Durability of one-time gene therapies, judged against external controls, is now a central regulatory and investor question.

**Novo Nordisk's hemophilia A drug denecimig: FDA extends review over manufacturing remediation**
STAT News (Pharmalittle), October 5, 2026 — https://www.statnews.com/pharmalot/2026/10/05/vaxcyte-pneumococcal-vaccine-rising-pharma-mergers-acquisitions/
Per the newsletter, the FDA extended its review of Novo's experimental hemophilia A drug because of ongoing remediation work at a manufacturing facility. No new decision date has been set. The item states no clinical data concerns were raised and that currently marketed Novo medicines are unaffected. Only a brief excerpt was accessible, so details are limited.
*Why it matters for rare disease developers:* Manufacturing readiness, not just efficacy, can delay rare disease approvals.

## What to watch

The thread across both parts is how well evidence holds up: small acromegaly studies are probing whether baseline MRI can pick the right drug, while uniQure shows how a waning effect can reshape a regulatory story. Watch for the FDA's handling of uniQure's accelerated approval filing and the new review date for Novo's denecimig.
