# Acromegaly Update — Monday July 6

**Bottom line:** It's a thin week for acromegaly-specific research — just one mechanistic study on tumor biology — but rare disease dealmaking was busy, headlined by Ipsen's ~$800M bet on a kidney-transplant antiviral and an FDA age expansion for Vertex's CRISPR therapy Casgevy.

## Part A — Acromegaly research

**GH responsiveness to corticotropin-releasing hormone identifies corticotroph-like somatotroph adenomas in acromegaly**
Itoh H, Mukai K, Mitsui Y, et al. (Osaka University Graduate School of Medicine)
*Pituitary*, published online July 1, 2026
[PMID: 42384227](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42384227/) | [DOI: 10.1007/s11102-026-01714-5](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11102-026-01714-5)

This retrospective study looked at 22 acromegaly patients whose growth hormone (GH) levels spike in response to corticotropin-releasing hormone (CRH) — a stress hormone normally tied to the adrenal axis, not GH — compared with 43 patients who don't show that response. The "CRH responders" turned out to have larger tumors, more vision problems, and pituitary tumor cells that looked biologically more like ACTH-producing (corticotroph) tumors than classic GH-producing ones, despite similar baseline GH and IGF-1 levels. The tumors also expressed more of the CRH receptor subtype (CRHR1) associated with this crossover behavior. The practical takeaway: a simple CRH stimulation test may help identify a biologically distinct, more aggressive subset of acromegaly tumors before surgery, giving clinicians an early read on tumor behavior beyond standard hormone testing. It's a single-center, moderately sized retrospective series, so the finding needs validation in larger and more diverse cohorts before it changes practice.

*Note: only one acromegaly paper cleared the top-tier journal bar this week (search covered NEJM, Lancet, JAMA, JCEM, Pituitary, Eur J Endocrinol, and similar); it's a quiet week for the disease-specific literature.*

## Part B — Rare Disease Industry News

**FDA expands Casgevy to children as young as 2 with sickle cell disease and beta thalassemia**
BioPharma Dive, July 1, 2026 — [biopharmadive.com/news/bridgebio-united-thymmune-otsuka-fda-casgevy](https://www.biopharmadive.com/news/bridgebio-united-thymmune-otsuka-fda-casgevy/824197/)
The FDA expanded approval of Vertex Pharmaceuticals' CRISPR-based gene-editing therapy Casgevy to cover children 2 and older with sickle cell disease or transfusion-dependent beta thalassemia, making it the first gene therapy cleared for those blood disorders at that age. Casgevy sales have lagged expectations because the treatment requires an intensive, multi-step process (stem cell collection, gene editing, chemotherapy conditioning, and reinfusion), and this approval widens the eligible patient pool.
*Why it matters: it's a concrete signal that regulators are willing to extend one-time gene-editing cures down into pediatric populations, which could reshape uptake economics for other one-and-done rare disease gene therapies still working through the FDA.*

**Otsuka's Voyxact hits confirmatory Phase 3 goal in rare kidney disease IgA nephropathy**
BioPharma Dive, July 1, 2026 — [biopharmadive.com/news/bridgebio-united-thymmune-otsuka-fda-casgevy](https://www.biopharmadive.com/news/bridgebio-united-thymmune-otsuka-fda-casgevy/824197/)
Otsuka reported that Voyxact (which blocks the APRIL protein implicated in IgA nephropathy) stabilized and, over two years, showed evidence of improving kidney function versus placebo — completing the confirmatory dataset needed to convert its late-2025 accelerated approval into traditional approval. Analysts expect the two-year data to encourage broader physician uptake of APRIL-targeting drugs in this rare autoimmune kidney disease.
*Why it matters: converting accelerated approvals to full approvals with real outcomes data (versus a surrogate marker) is exactly the kind of evidence rare disease drugmakers need to defend both regulatory standing and payer coverage.*

**Ipsen to buy Swiss biotech Memo Therapeutics in deal worth up to ~$800M**
Fierce Biotech, July 1, 2026 — [fiercebiotech.com/biotech/ipsen-gets-memo-penning-eu700m-deal-buy-rare-disease-biotech-clinical-stage-asset](https://www.fiercebiotech.com/biotech/ipsen-gets-memo-penning-eu700m-deal-buy-rare-disease-biotech-clinical-stage-asset) (see also: [BioPharma Dive](https://www.biopharmadive.com/news/ipsen-memo-acquisition-rare-disease-virus-deal/824207/))
Ipsen is paying €200M ($228M) upfront, with total deal value up to €700M (~$798M) in milestones, for Memo Therapeutics' potravitug — a first-in-class antibody targeting BK polyomavirus, an infection with no approved targeted treatment that can trigger transplant failure in kidney transplant recipients. Phase 2 data showed sustained viral suppression and resolution of biopsy-proven nephropathy; a pivotal Phase 2/3 trial is expected to start later this year. It's Ipsen's second biotech acquisition in three days, following a $450M upfront deal for a blood cancer asset.
*Why it matters: it's a reminder that "rare disease" dealmaking increasingly includes complication-of-transplant indications, not just classic genetic disorders — and that larger pharma still sees clinical-stage, single-asset biotechs as an efficient way to fill out rare disease pipelines.*

**United Therapeutics acquires Thymmune Therapeutics for congenital athymia cell therapy**
BioPharma Dive, July 2, 2026 — [biopharmadive.com/news/bridgebio-united-thymmune-otsuka-fda-casgevy](https://www.biopharmadive.com/news/bridgebio-united-thymmune-otsuka-fda-casgevy/824197/)
United Therapeutics is paying $140M upfront (with up to $160M more in milestones through 2031) for Thymmune Therapeutics, a startup out of Harvard geneticist George Church's lab. Thymmune's lead program, THY-100, converts induced pluripotent stem cells into thymic cells meant to restore immune function in patients born with congenital athymia — a rare, often-fatal immunodeficiency in which the thymus gland fails to develop.
*Why it matters: congenital athymia is about as ultra-rare as it gets, and the deal shows continued appetite for cell-therapy approaches to single-gland, single-function genetic conditions that have essentially no other treatment path.*

**BridgeBio raises up to $1B in preferred equity to fund rare disease drug launches**
BioPharma Dive, July 1, 2026 — [biopharmadive.com/news/bridgebio-united-thymmune-otsuka-fda-casgevy](https://www.biopharmadive.com/news/bridgebio-united-thymmune-otsuka-fda-casgevy/824197/)
Sixth Street Partners and HealthCare Royalty agreed to provide BridgeBio Pharma up to $1B in preferred equity (Sixth Street funding $800M, HealthCare Royalty $133.9M) to support its commercial launches, at a conversion price representing more than a 100% premium to BridgeBio's average share price. An analyst flagged the 7% preferred dividend as a real cost, but still cheaper than raising equivalent capital through equity.
*Why it matters: it's a sign rare disease companies with approved or near-approved drugs are increasingly turning to structured/preferred financing rather than diluting shareholders outright to fund the expensive job of commercializing low-prevalence-disease drugs.*

## What to watch

The acromegaly literature was quiet this week, but the CRH-responsiveness paper points to a broader theme echoed on the industry side: rare disease drug development is getting more precise about identifying biologically distinct patient subgroups — whether that's a corticotroph-like acromegaly tumor or an APRIL-driven kidney disease — rather than treating each condition as monolithic. On the industry side, watch whether the Casgevy pediatric expansion and Otsuka's confirmatory IgAN data translate into faster payer uptake, and whether Ipsen's and United Therapeutics' appetite for single-asset, clinical-stage rare disease biotechs continues into Q3.
