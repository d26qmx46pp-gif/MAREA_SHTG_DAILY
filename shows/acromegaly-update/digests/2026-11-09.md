# Acromegaly Update — Monday November 9

**Bottom line:** A quiet week for top-tier acromegaly papers, with a useful surgical-outcome predictor and a new treatment-landscape review, while rare disease industry news was dominated by setbacks: a failed Becker muscular dystrophy trial, deep Ultragenyx layoffs, and a new safety signal for Zolgensma.

## Part A — Acromegaly research

*No top-tier hits this week — showing next-best tier (journals outside the filtered list).*

**A clinicopathological prediction model for remission after transsphenoidal surgery for acromegaly: a retrospective single-center cohort study**
Vergeer RA, et al. Medicine (Baltimore), Nov 6, 2026. [PMID 42994872](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42994872) · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1097/MD.0000000000051358)
In 100 patients from Groningen who had primary surgery between 2000 and 2019, maximum tumor diameter was the single best predictor of remission. Larger tumor volume, higher Knosp grade, cavernous sinus invasion and higher random GH at diagnosis all tracked with lower remission. Histopathology and proliferation markers did not predict outcome. Diameter alone predicted early and long-term remission with sensitivity around 79% and specificity of roughly 72–78%. Single-center and retrospective.

**New horizons in pharmacotherapy for Cushing's disease and acromegaly**
Fleseriu M, Melmed S, Labadzhyan A. Endocr Relat Cancer, Nov 2026. [PMID 42878776](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42878776) · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1530/ERC-26-0317)
A review of newly approved and investigational drugs. For acromegaly it covers newer octreotide formulations, selective oral somatostatin receptor agonists, combination approaches and pegvisomant. The authors point to delayed diagnosis, treatment resistance, side effects, recurrence and monitoring burden as remaining challenges.

**Pediatric gigantism due to ectopic GHRH secretion from a metastatic bronchial neuroendocrine tumor**
Vidal-Ostos De Lara F, et al. JCEM Case Reports, Nov 6, 2026. [PMID 42996503](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42996503) · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1210/jcemcr/luag304)
A girl diagnosed at age 9 with a bronchial carcinoid and metastases developed gigantism and acromegalic features from GHRH secretion. Treatment spanned surgery, somatostatin analogs, a dopamine agonist, pegvisomant, pasireotide and lutetium-177 DOTATATE. Pasireotide improved biochemical control, and after 23 years she is well with stable disease. A single case.

**Primary hypertrophic osteoarthropathy type 2 presenting as an acromegaly mimic with enteropathy**
Kumar S, et al. JCEM Case Reports, Nov 6, 2026. [PMID 42996629](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42996629) · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1210/jcemcr/luag342)
A 39-year-old man with coarse facies, cutis verticis gyrata and clubbing had a normal GH suppression test and low-normal IGF-1, excluding acromegaly. Sequencing found a homozygous SLCO2A1 variant, confirming a genetic mimic with chronic enteropathy. Lesson: clubbing in an acromegaloid patient should prompt SLCO2A1 testing before pituitary work-up.

## Part B — Rare Disease Industry News

**Servier's sevasemten fails mid-stage Becker muscular dystrophy trial**
BioPharma Dive (Delilah Alvarado), Nov 6, 2026 — https://www.biopharmadive.com/news/lilly-hims-alnylam-boehringer-ultragenyx-sevasemten-ani-egetis/873843/
Servier reported that sevasemten, acquired from Edgewise Therapeutics about five months ago, did not produce a statistically significant benefit on a motor function test in Becker muscular dystrophy. Servier said the results were not aligned with some observations from earlier studies. It paid Edgewise $1.55 billion upfront in June, with up to $1.1 billion more tied to regulatory and commercial goals.
*Why it matters for rare disease developers:* A reminder that large upfront payments on early rare disease data carry real risk.

**Ultragenyx to cut about 1,000 jobs after Angelman gene therapy failure**
BioPharma Dive (Delilah Alvarado), Nov 6, 2026 — same roundup link above
Ultragenyx will cut about 20% of its workforce, halt all studies of its Angelman syndrome gene therapy (which missed in Phase 3 in September) and shelve a Wilson's disease candidate. It expects 2027 R&D costs to fall at least 50% versus 2025.
*Why it matters for rare disease developers:* Even established rare disease companies are retrenching sharply after late-stage misses.

**ANI Pharmaceuticals offers $560M for Egetis Therapeutics**
BioPharma Dive (Delilah Alvarado), Nov 6, 2026 — same roundup link above
ANI offered 11.25 Swedish krona per share, a 27% premium to the two-month average. Egetis sells Emcitate, the only approved U.S. and European treatment for peripheral thyrotoxicosis in Allan-Herndon-Dudley syndrome; ANI projects up to $300 million in peak annual sales.
*Why it matters for rare disease developers:* Mid-size buyers are paying premiums for commercial-stage ultra-rare assets.
*See also:* the same roundup reports Alnylam's chief commercial officer Tolga Tanguler is leaving, with Alnylam's shares down about 60% this year on concerns over Amvuttra's growth trajectory.

**Novartis cites previously unknown tumor cases possibly tied to Zolgensma**
STAT News / Pharmalot (Ed Silverman, Jason Mast), Nov 5, 2026 — https://www.statnews.com/pharmalot/2026/11/05/novartis-sma-zolgensma-tumor-case-reports/
Novartis disclosed tumor reports in children previously treated with its SMA gene therapy; its statement cites nine reports since approval, two of which showed evidence of a possible link (the article also references seven new cases, so figures are as reported). Regulators are examining the cases. Only the free portion of the paywalled article was reviewed, and it describes this as a potential safety signal for now.
*Why it matters for rare disease developers:* Long-term safety surveillance of gene therapies is under scrutiny.

**Alethio raises $90M Series A for rare blood cancers**
BioPharma Dive (Gwendolyn Wu), Nov 5, 2026 — https://www.biopharmadive.com/news/alethio-therapeutics-myeloproliferative-neoplasms-series-a/873775/
UK-based Alethio's round was co-led by Sofinnova Partners and RA Capital. Lead program ATX-011 is an antibody targeting G6b for myeloproliferative neoplasms; clinical testing is expected in the first half of 2028, so claims rest on preclinical work.
*Why it matters for rare disease developers:* Venture money is still available for early-stage rare disease platforms.

## What to watch
Acromegaly care keeps moving toward oral and individualized therapy, while the wider rare disease sector this week showed how unforgiving late-stage data and safety signals can be. Watch for regulatory responses on Zolgensma and how acquirers price rare disease assets after the Servier result.
