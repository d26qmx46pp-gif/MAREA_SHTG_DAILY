# Acromegaly Update — Monday November 23

**Subject:** Acromegaly Update — Monday November 23

**Bottom line:** A solid acromegaly week led by four-year extension data showing durable control on once-daily oral paltusotine and a Swedish registry study flagging excess cardiovascular morbidity in acromegaly-associated diabetes, while rare disease industry news brought two FDA approvals (Beren's Lembaz for Niemann-Pick type C, Sanofi's Infilbray for neurological Gaucher disease), a second rejection for Capricor's Duchenne cell therapy, and two sizable genetic-medicine financings.

## Part A — Acromegaly research

**Safety and efficacy of once-daily oral paltusotine in acromegaly: ACROBAT Advance open-label extension up to 4 years**
Gadelha MR, Gordon MB, Doknic M, Mezősi E, et al. — *J Clin Endocrinol Metab*
PMID: [42359578](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42359578/) · DOI: [10.1210/clinem/dgag254](https://doi.org/10.1210/clinem/dgag254)
Interim results from the ongoing open-label extension of the Phase 2 ACROBAT Edge and Evolve studies, in 43 patients (88% of those eligible) who had previously been on injected somatostatin receptor ligand regimens. Median IGF-I was 1.15× ULN at parent-study baseline and 1.01× ULN at week 207 (n = 20 still reporting), with stable GH, symptoms and tumor size; dose went up to 60 mg once a tablet formulation became available in year 3, and cabergoline or pegvisomant add-ons were allowed. Eight patients (18.6%) discontinued, two (4.7%) for adverse events, with no unexpected safety findings. This is the longest-duration dataset yet for the first approved oral nonpeptide SST2 agonist — reassuring on durability, though the open-label design and shrinking n at year 4 limit conclusions.

**Cardiovascular morbidity and mortality in acromegaly-associated diabetes: a Swedish nationwide study**
Milioto A, Ragnarsson O, Ferone D, Adiels M, et al. — *J Clin Endocrinol Metab*
PMID: [42573188](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42573188/) · DOI: [10.1210/clinem/dgag322](https://doi.org/10.1210/clinem/dgag322)
A nationwide matched-cohort study comparing 348 patients with acromegaly-associated diabetes against 3,480 type 2 diabetes controls matched 10:1 on sex, age, calendar year and diabetes duration. Baseline cardiovascular disease was more common in the acromegaly group (61% vs 43%), including hypertension, heart failure, arrhythmia and stroke. During follow-up, cardiovascular morbidity was higher (HR 1.55; 95% CI 1.24–1.94), driven notably by venous thromboembolism (especially pulmonary embolism) and arrhythmia, but neither overall mortality (HR 1.18; 95% CI 0.96–1.46) nor cardiovascular mortality (HR 1.12) was significantly increased. The message: diabetes in acromegaly is not "just" type 2 diabetes in a lean patient and warrants closer cardiovascular and thrombotic surveillance.

**A patient-clinician call for global harmonization of access to second-line medical therapies for acromegaly in the era of personalized medicine**
Puig-Domingo M, Way R, Ciriza R, Giustina A — *Pituitary* (narrative review / position paper)
PMID: [43050034](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/43050034/) · DOI: [10.1007/s11102-026-01794-3](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11102-026-01794-3)
In the EU and UK, the labels for pegvisomant and pasireotide LAR require prior inadequate control on a somatostatin analogue (or intolerance, for pegvisomant), unlike US labeling. The authors — clinicians plus patient representatives — review regulatory documents, pivotal trials, consensus statements and biomarker-guided trials, and argue that validated predictors now identify patients unlikely to respond to first-generation SRLs. They propose revisiting labels to allow earlier, individualized second-line therapy for selected patients, while acknowledging that predictors for pasireotide response are not yet confirmed. Useful policy context as oral and next-generation agents widen the treatment menu.

**Page turnover technique for resection of the medial wall of the cavernous sinus during endoscopic endonasal surgery for acromegaly**
Wang H, Zou Z, Zhang Y, Wang D, et al. — *Pituitary*
PMID: [43049956](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/43049956/) · DOI: [10.1007/s11102-026-01781-8](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11102-026-01781-8)
A surgical technique report in 12 consecutive patients with large, mostly highly invasive (Knosp ≥3A in 91.7%) GH-secreting adenomas, where the carotid artery limits conventional access to the cavernous sinus medial wall. Gross-total or near-total resection was achieved in 16.7% and 58.3% respectively, with endocrine remission in 33.3%. Intraoperative CSF leaks were common (66.7%, mostly low grade) but there were no postoperative fistulas, carotid injuries or cranial nerve deficits, and pathology confirmed medial-wall invasion in 90% of specimens. Small and retrospective — a feasibility signal for a hard-to-treat subgroup, not proof of improved remission.

**Oral and maxillofacial manifestations of acromegaly and their association with disease activity: a chart review**
Pélissier-Léon C, Ferrière A, Tabarin A, Trupin J, et al. — *Pituitary*
PMID: [43037755](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/43037755/) · DOI: [10.1007/s11102-026-01761-y](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11102-026-01761-y)
A two-center French chart review of 64 patients referred to oral and maxillofacial surgery. Macroglossia was present in about 80%, more than half had class III malocclusion with prognathism, 39% had transverse mandibular excess and 39% periodontal disease, and nearly half had interdental diastema; temporomandibular disorders were relatively uncommon (18.7%). The authors stress that jaw growth is transverse as well as sagittal, complicating orthodontic and orthognathic management. A practical reminder of the dental and facial burden patients report most.

*Also published (not featured):* a JCEM letter from Boguszewski and van der Lely on reassessing cancer risk in acromegaly (PMID 43037161), and several next-tier case reports and imaging studies.

## Part B — Rare Disease Industry News

**Beren clinches long-awaited FDA nod for cyclodextrin drug in Niemann-Pick disease**
Fierce Pharma, Nov 18, 2026 — https://www.fiercepharma.com/pharma/beren-clinches-long-awaited-fda-nod-niemann-pick-disease
The FDA approved Beren Therapeutics' adrabetadex, branded Lembaz, for adults and children with Niemann-Pick disease type C whose neurological symptoms began before age 6. The approval rests on an overall survival analysis pooling three trials, showing a 55% reduction in mortality risk in infantile-onset NPC versus an external control arm. It becomes the third approved NPC therapy after IntraBio's Aqneursa and Zevra's Miplyffa (both 2024), and the only one approved on survival data; Beren expects commercial availability by early December. The approval follows a review that had been extended after a major amendment, and a long history including a failed 2018 Phase 2/3 trial and acquisition out of Mallinckrodt's bankruptcy.
*Why it matters for rare disease developers:* A survival-vs-external-control package carried an approval in an ultra-rare disease — a meaningful precedent for natural-history-based evidence.
See also: [Fierce Biotech on the earlier review extension](https://www.fiercebiotech.com/biotech/fda-extends-berens-rare-disease-review-after-info-request-further-stretching-saga).

**Sanofi gains FDA clearance for Gaucher disease pill**
BioPharma Dive (Delilah Alvarado), Nov 19, 2026 — https://www.biopharmadive.com/news/sanofi-fda-infilbray-venglustat-approve-gaucher/875167/
Sanofi's once-daily oral venglustat, to be sold as Infilbray, was approved in the U.S. for adults and children weighing at least 15 kg with Type 3 Gaucher disease — described as the first approved treatment for the neurological symptoms of this lysosomal storage disorder. In the Phase 3 trial supporting approval, a year of treatment was associated with statistically significant improvements in neurological symptoms. The label carries warnings on suicidal thoughts and behaviors, lens clouding in children, and possible fetal harm. The drug came to Sanofi with its 2011 Genzyme purchase and had previously failed in Parkinson's, a rare kidney condition, GM2 gangliosidosis and Fabry disease; pricing was not disclosed and analysts expect modest sales.
*Why it matters for rare disease developers:* A long-shot asset found its niche after multiple failures — a reminder that indication selection can rescue a molecule.

**FDA again turns back Capricor Duchenne cell therapy**
BioPharma Dive (Gwendolyn Wu), Nov 21, 2026 — https://www.biopharmadive.com/news/capricor-fda-duchenne-cell-therapy-deramiocel/875384/
The FDA issued a complete response letter for Capricor's deramiocel in Duchenne muscular dystrophy, citing "clinical deficiencies" and data not "sufficient to establish substantial evidence of effectiveness." In a separate statement, the agency accused Capricor of "serious data integrity violations" in the key study, including breaches of blinding protocols and after-the-fact changes to the statistical analysis plan. Capricor's CEO said the company stands behind its data. This is the second rejection, after a July 2025 CRL and a negative advisory committee vote; the FDA had extended review to Nov. 22 after Capricor narrowed the proposed indication to upper-limb function.
*Why it matters for rare disease developers:* Even amid political pressure for rare disease flexibility, FDA is drawing a hard line on trial conduct and analysis integrity.
See also: [STAT, Nov 20](https://www.statnews.com/2026/11/20/fda-rejects-capricor-duchenne-therapy/).

**Marrow sets out to challenge genetic medicines for severe blood diseases**
BioPharma Dive (Gwendolyn Wu), Nov 17, 2026 — https://www.biopharmadive.com/news/marrow-therapeutics-sickle-cell-beta-thalassemia-drug/874789/
Marrow Therapeutics emerged from stealth with $173 million in combined Series A and B financing from 11 investors including Scion Life Sciences, OrbiMed and venBio. Its lead, MAR-101, is an siRNA conjugated to a transferrin-receptor-targeting antibody designed to reactivate fetal hemoglobin in bone marrow cells for sickle cell disease and beta thalassemia. Unlike Casgevy and Lyfgenia, it would be given by simple, potentially repeat injection without stem-cell harvesting or chemotherapy conditioning. No clinical timeline has been disclosed.
*Why it matters for rare disease developers:* Investors are funding "off-the-shelf" alternatives to one-time ex vivo gene therapies that have struggled commercially.

**Versant, Lilly put $100 million behind new startup aimed at solving one of gene therapy's thorniest issues**
STAT (Jason Mast), Nov 16, 2026 — https://www.statnews.com/2026/11/16/versant-lilly-put-100-million-behind-new-startup-aimed-at-solving-one-of-gene-therapys-thorniest-issues/
Versant Ventures, Eli Lilly and other investors put $100 million into new startup Seawolf Therapeutics, which aims to deliver genes to patients without using viral vectors. Sanofi has also signed a partnership with the company. Details beyond the opening of the article were paywalled and are not summarized here.
*Why it matters for rare disease developers:* Non-viral delivery could ease the safety, redosing and manufacturing limits of AAV that weigh on many rare disease gene therapy programs.

## What to watch

The common thread this week is evidence standards. In acromegaly, long-term open-label data on oral paltusotine and a push to loosen EU/UK step-therapy labels both point toward more individualized, earlier use of newer agents — and payers and regulators will want hard comparative data to follow. Across rare disease, FDA approved Lembaz on survival versus an external control while rejecting Capricor over data integrity: flexibility on study design is available, but not on trial conduct. Watch Lembaz's December launch, Capricor's next steps, and whether non-viral and conjugate platforms like Seawolf and Marrow reach the clinic in 2027.
