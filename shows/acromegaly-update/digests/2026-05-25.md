# Acromegaly Update — Monday May 25

**Bottom line:** A network meta-analysis confirms that injectable somatostatin receptor ligands remain comparably effective first-line pharmacotherapy for acromegaly while biochemical discordance after surgery complicates post-operative management — and in the rare disease industry, FDA leadership chaos is rattling investor confidence in companies targeting small patient populations.

## Part A — Acromegaly Research

*Five papers from top-tier acromegaly journals this week (May 18–23, 2026); all met the bar. Top four selected below.*

---

**Efficacy and safety of pharmacologic therapies in acromegaly: a systematic literature review and network meta-analysis**
Roberto Salvatori, Raffaella M Colzani, Noemi Hummel, et al. | *Journal of Clinical Endocrinology & Metabolism* | May 19, 2026
PMID: [41773305](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41773305/) | DOI: [10.1210/clinem/dgag085](https://doi.org/10.1210/clinem/dgag085)

A Bayesian network meta-analysis (NMA) of 22 records from 18 randomized controlled trials — the most comprehensive comparative evidence synthesis for acromegaly pharmacotherapy to date. Head-to-head trials are scarce in acromegaly, so this NMA fills a critical evidence gap by allowing indirect comparisons across all available drugs. The central finding: biochemical control rates were comparable among long-acting injectable somatostatin receptor ligands (SRLs) — lanreotide, octreotide, and pasireotide — making head-to-head superiority among them unlikely to emerge in future trials. The analysis provides the highest-quality evidence to date that initial choice among injectable SRLs can be guided by patient tolerability profiles and convenience rather than expected efficacy differences. This paper is likely to inform updated clinical guidelines.

---

**Prevalence and patterns of biochemical discordance in patients with surgically treated acromegaly**
Anna Pennlund, Thomas Olsson Bontell, Thomas Skoglund, et al. | *Pituitary* | May 23, 2026
PMID: [42177388](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42177388/) | DOI: [10.1007/s11102-026-01694-6](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11102-026-01694-6)

In this Swedish retrospective cohort of 82 surgically treated patients from Sahlgrenska University Hospital (1994–2019), one in five patients (19.5%) showed biochemical discordance after surgery — defined as one hormone controlled while the other is not. The dominant pattern was elevated IGF-1 with a normalized nadir GH on oral glucose tolerance test, occurring in 14 of the 16 discordant cases. Notably, the discordant group had significantly higher IGF-1 levels at diagnosis, suggesting greater pre-operative disease burden predisposes to this confusing post-operative picture. The study highlights a real clinical challenge: when GH and IGF-1 disagree, clinicians face genuine uncertainty about whether disease is controlled. The findings support treating pre-surgical IGF-1 elevation as a flag for closer post-operative monitoring.

---

**Hormonal staining patterns and clinical outcomes in acromegaly: a 15-year single-center study**
Sinem Başak Tan Öksüz, Esra Eraslan Aydemir, Aylin Okçu Heper, et al. | *Pituitary* | May 18, 2026
PMID: [42149337](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42149337/) | DOI: [10.1007/s11102-026-01688-4](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11102-026-01688-4)

Among 90 patients with acromegaly who underwent transsphenoidal surgery between 2010 and 2025, 52.2% had GH-only adenomas and a substantial portion co-expressed prolactin (GH/PRL). This 15-year single-center study compares pathological features and treatment outcomes across immunohistochemical subtypes. The clinical relevance of PRL co-expression in somatotroph adenomas remains debated, and this paper adds real-world outcomes data to that discussion. The study finds that GH/PRL co-expression does not simply translate into worse prognosis, adding nuance to how pathology reports should be interpreted in guiding post-operative management strategies.

---

**Calcaneal quantitative ultrasound parameters strongly correlate with DXA measurements in patients with acromegaly**
Claudia Campana, Andrea Casabella, Anna Arecco, et al. | *Pituitary* | May 18, 2026
PMID: [42149373](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42149373/) | DOI: [10.1007/s11102-026-01696-4](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11102-026-01696-4)

Acromegaly increases skeletal fragility, yet DXA — the gold standard for bone assessment — has recognized limitations in this condition. This observational cohort of 56 acromegaly patients and 59 matched healthy controls tests whether calcaneal quantitative ultrasound (QUS) could serve as a practical, lower-cost, radiation-free alternative for bone assessment. The study finds that QUS parameters correlated strongly with DXA measurements, suggesting QUS may have a role in monitoring bone health in acromegaly, particularly in settings where DXA is less accessible or for serial follow-up. A useful addition to the toolkit for managing acromegaly-related comorbidities.

## Part B — Rare Disease Industry News

*Lookback window: May 18–25, 2026. Two stories met the bar this week.*

---

**FDA Leadership Chaos Is Rattling Rare Disease Biotech Investors**
STAT News | May 20, 2026 | [statnews.com](https://www.statnews.com/2026/05/20/rare-disease-biotech-ceos-discuss-fda-uncertainty-stat-summit/)

At the STAT Breakthrough Summit West in San Francisco on May 20, rare disease biotech executives and academic experts described the ouster of key FDA officials as generating near-constant investor anxiety for companies pursuing small-population drugs. Mahzi Therapeutics CEO Yael Weiss described receiving daily investor emails asking how FDA personnel chaos will affect her company's approval trajectory — "It's a roller coaster," she said. Former BIO chairman Ted Love also participated in the discussion, which addressed not only personnel turnover but also evolving FDA flexibility on clinical trial design for rare disease programs. The panel debated how much design flexibility the agency should allow when patient populations are too small to power conventional randomized trials. The STAT report notes this comes amid a broader leadership vacuum at the agency following multiple departures of senior FDA officials.

*Why it matters for rare disease developers:* FDA consistency and personnel stability matter disproportionately in rare disease, where single-drug applications often depend on close, iterative dialogue with specific reviewers; instability at the top of the agency translates directly into timeline and financing uncertainty for small biotechs.

**AI Tool Identifies Existing Drug as Treatment for Newborn's Ultra-Rare Genetic Disorder**
STAT News | May 19, 2026 | [statnews.com](https://www.statnews.com/2026/05/19/ai-helped-find-treatment-newborn-ultra-rare-disease/)

Doctors at Mayo Clinic used an NIH-funded artificial intelligence platform called the Biomedical Data Translator to identify Klonopin (clonazepam) as a treatment for Jorie Kraus, an infant born with an ultra-rare disorder caused by a chromosome 10 gene deletion that produced severe neurodevelopmental and motor deficits. After rapid genome sequencing and AI-assisted compound matching across biomedical databases, the team prescribed the widely available seizure drug — and the response was dramatic: a child who had spent 73 days in the NICU and two years hitting developmental plateaus suddenly gained motor function. The story was shared publicly by Jorie's parents at STAT's Breakthrough Summit West, and the physicians noted a second patient with a similar mutation showed comparable benefit after receiving the same treatment. Jorie's parents have formed a nonprofit, the Jorie Effect, to support Mayo Clinic's rare disease precision medicine program.

*Why it matters for rare disease developers:* This case illustrates both the potential and the current limitations of AI-driven drug repurposing for rare and undiagnosed conditions — the AI can surface candidates, but the infrastructure for rapid genomic sequencing, lab confirmation, and expanded access programs remains inconsistently available, representing a gap that rare disease drug developers and diagnostics companies are increasingly trying to fill.

## What to Watch

On the acromegaly side, the NMA confirms a relative equilibrium among injectable SRLs — the next wave of differentiation will likely come from oral options like paltusotine, which remains in late-stage development. The biochemical discordance data underscores that high pre-surgical IGF-1 should prompt closer long-term monitoring even after apparently successful surgery. Meanwhile, FDA regulatory uncertainty — the defining theme of rare disease industry news this week — has direct relevance to the acromegaly pipeline: any new NDA for an acromegaly therapy would face the same reviewer-continuity questions that are making CEOs nervous across the rare disease space. Watch for how the agency resolves its staffing situation in the coming months, and for whether upcoming advisory committees signal a shift in FDA's evidentiary standards for small patient populations.
