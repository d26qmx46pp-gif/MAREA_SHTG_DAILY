# Acromegaly Update — Monday June 8, 2026

**Bottom line:** A landmark JCEM special issue lands this week with five major reviews and original studies that collectively reset the clinical playbook for acromegaly — from emerging oral therapies to the underappreciated mortality burden — while in the rare disease industry, Alnylam bets $2 billion on AI-designed RNA medicines, a controversial FDA voucher program faces calls for reform, and a new market forecast projects orphan drug sales above $400 billion by 2032.

## Part A — Acromegaly Research

*Note: This week's papers come from a dedicated JCEM special issue on acromegaly (all published June 4, 2026). One paper from this issue (PMID 42056759, "Acromegaly diagnosis") was covered in the prior episode and is excluded here.*

---

**What is the future of acromegaly therapy?**
Schilbach K, Strasburger C. *The Journal of Clinical Endocrinology & Metabolism*, 2026 Jun 4. [PMID 41964414](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41964414/) | DOI: 10.1210/clinem/dgag144

This narrative review synthesizes multinational late-stage clinical trial data through October 2025 to map where acromegaly pharmacotherapy is headed. The authors note that oral somatostatin receptor ligands — specifically paltusotine — are now available in the United States, representing a meaningful shift away from monthly injections toward more convenient long-term management. Non-oral innovations in the pipeline include next-generation GH receptor antagonists and combination regimens that pair different drug classes to address patients who fail single-agent biochemical control. The review is candid about gaps: even with growing options, a substantial share of patients remain in suboptimal biochemical or symptomatic states. For researchers and clinicians focused on emerging therapies, this is the most forward-looking piece in this week's issue.

---

**Current treatment landscape of acromegaly**
Castinetti F, Ioachimescu A. *The Journal of Clinical Endocrinology & Metabolism*, 2026 Jun 4. [PMID 41965092](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41965092/) | DOI: 10.1210/clinem/dgag152

This companion review surveys the full contemporary treatment toolkit from transsphenoidal surgery through medical therapy and radiotherapy. Long-acting somatostatin receptor ligands (octreotide, lanreotide) remain the cornerstone of medical management, with pasireotide offering more favorable biochemical outcomes in certain subgroups at the cost of higher rates of hyperglycemia. Pegvisomant, the GH receptor antagonist, provides an important rescue option but requires subcutaneous dosing and careful IGF-1 monitoring. The authors emphasize that combination strategies — pairing somatostatin receptor ligands with pegvisomant — are increasingly used to achieve normalization in patients refractory to monotherapy, and that treatment burden remains a major quality-of-life concern even when biochemical goals are met.

**Current controversies in acromegaly care**
Spencer-Segal J, Nachtigall L. *The Journal of Clinical Endocrinology & Metabolism*, 2026 Jun 4. [PMID 41981973](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41981973/) | DOI: 10.1210/clinem/dgag156

This perspective-style paper confronts practical clinical debates that guidelines have not fully resolved. Diagnostic delay in acromegaly remains stubbornly common and is associated with increased morbidity — the authors note that artificial intelligence tools may shorten the time from symptom onset to diagnosis, though they flag ethical and privacy considerations that remain unresolved. Additional controversies addressed include optimal biochemical monitoring intervals, management of women with active acromegaly contemplating pregnancy (where evidence is sparse), predictors of somatostatin receptor ligand response, and whether alternative dosing regimens such as extended injection intervals can maintain disease control while reducing burden. The paper is particularly useful as a practical reference for clinicians navigating gray-zone clinical scenarios.

---

**Acromegaly complications: an update**
Giustina A, Fleseriu M. *The Journal of Clinical Endocrinology & Metabolism*, 2026 Jun 4. [PMID 42014041](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42014041/) | DOI: 10.1210/clinem/dgag114

Comorbidities — not just GH/IGF-1 elevation itself — drive much of the morbidity and reduced survival in acromegaly. This review focuses on the most clinically relevant complication clusters: cardiorespiratory disease (including hypertension, cardiomyopathy, sleep apnea), metabolic disorders (diabetes, impaired glucose tolerance), musculoskeletal manifestations (arthropathy, vertebral fractures), and neoplastic risk. A key message is that achieving biochemical control is necessary but not sufficient — many complications persist or progress even after GH normalization, requiring targeted surveillance and individualized management. The authors advocate for structured complication screening at diagnosis and at defined intervals throughout the disease course.

**Changing understanding of acromegaly epidemiology and early mortality risk**
Esposito D, Johannsson G. *The Journal of Clinical Endocrinology & Metabolism*, 2026 Jun 4. [PMID 41961594](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41961594/) | DOI: 10.1210/clinem/dgag150

Acromegaly epidemiology is shifting. Reported incidence and prevalence have risen over recent decades — not because the disease is more common, but because improved diagnostic tools, greater physician awareness, and earlier detection have expanded the ascertained patient pool. This paper synthesizes data showing that mortality rates in acromegaly, while historically elevated (particularly from cardiovascular and respiratory causes), have improved substantially in contemporary cohorts with adequate biochemical control. Importantly, excess early mortality persists in patients with prolonged diagnostic delay or persistently elevated GH/IGF-1, reinforcing the clinical imperative for faster diagnosis and sustained treatment targets. Regional variability in outcomes data complicates cross-study comparisons and points to the need for better standardized registries.

## Part B — Rare Disease Industry News

**Alnylam Strikes $2 Billion AI Deal with Inceptive Nucleics to Power RNAi Drug Discovery**
*STAT News* · June 3, 2026 · [Source](https://www.statnews.com/2026/06/03/alnylam-partner-with-inceptive-nucleics-ai-foundation-models/)

Alnylam Pharmaceuticals announced a three-year strategic collaboration with Inceptive Nucleics, a startup founded by Jakob Uszkoreit — co-author of the seminal "Attention is all you need" paper that underpins modern large language models — worth up to $2 billion, with $30 million upfront in cash and equity. Inceptive is building "AI foundation models of life," reusable deep learning systems trained on biological sequence data that can, in principle, be applied across many drug discovery tasks without task-specific retraining. The deal will aim to co-discover new RNA interference (RNAi) therapeutics using Inceptive's models applied to Alnylam's molecular platform. Alnylam's 2025 revenues were $3.7 billion, driven by rare disease products including patisiran and vutrisiran for transthyretin amyloidosis and inclisiran for hypercholesterolemia.

*Why it matters for rare disease developers:* Alnylam is the leading commercial example of RNA-based therapies for rare diseases; its bet on AI foundation models signals a broader industry shift toward using large-scale ML to accelerate and de-risk RNAi pipeline expansion.

**Pharma and Patient Groups Urge FDA to Pause Makary's Commissioner's Voucher Program**
*STAT News* · June 4, 2026 · [Source](https://www.statnews.com/2026/06/04/fda-commissioners-voucher-program-town-hall-pharma-patients-urge-pause/)

At a town hall on the FDA's White Oak campus, seventeen speakers representing drug companies, patient advocacy organizations, and academic groups called on the FDA to pause — and then formally re-establish through standard notice-and-comment rulemaking — the Commissioner's National Priority Voucher program launched by former FDA commissioner Marty Makary approximately one year ago. The program offers dramatically shortened review timelines (as little as one to two months) to drugs that the agency judges as aligning with "national priorities," but critics have flagged the vague criteria and raised concerns about susceptibility to political interference. Most speakers preferred a pause over abolition, asking that the program's scope and eligibility criteria be codified through transparent regulatory procedures before resuming.

*Why it matters for rare disease developers:* Priority review pathways are disproportionately important to small rare disease companies, whose drugs often have the most to gain from faster FDA timelines; regulatory uncertainty around this program affects how developers plan pivotal trial timelines and NDA filings.

**Orphan Drug Market Projected to Exceed $400 Billion by 2032 Despite FDA Turbulence**
*BioPharma Dive / Evaluate* · June 1, 2026 · [Source](https://www.biopharmadive.com/spons/2026-orphan-drugs-report-a-safer-passage-amid-stormy-waters/820746/)

A new market analysis from Evaluate forecasts global orphan drug sales exceeding $400 billion by 2032 — accounting for over 21 percent of all prescription pharmaceutical revenue, up from roughly 15 percent in 2022. Despite an FDA environment marked by volatility around cell and gene therapy approvals, a wave of rejections for rare disease drugs in recent years, and uncertainty around new regulatory pathways, orphan drug commercialization has remained durable. Johnson & Johnson is projected to lead by orphan drug revenue with nearly $31 billion in 2032 sales from rare disease products. The report notes that orphan designation remains strategically valuable and that the pipeline of rare disease candidates continues to grow, supported by advances in gene therapy, RNA-based medicines, and targeted small molecules.

*Why it matters for rare disease developers:* Even amid FDA headwinds, the overall rare disease market trajectory is strongly upward — providing a continued commercial incentive for pipeline investment that smaller developers can cite in investor and partnership discussions.

## What to Watch

The JCEM special issue this week is a rare moment of coordinated scientific synthesis for acromegaly, compressing years of accumulated clinical evidence into a single readable reference set. Practitioners should note the emerging story on paltusotine and other oral agents, which could eventually change long-term adherence dynamics for patients who struggle with injectable regimens. Concurrently, the FDA regulatory environment for rare disease drugs is in active flux: the push to formalize — or pause — the Commissioner's Voucher program reflects broader industry anxiety about the durability of any fast-track pathway that lacks statutory backing. For rare disease companies investing in AI-enabled platforms (as Alnylam is now doing with RNAi), the market forecast confirming $400 billion in orphan drug sales by 2032 provides a commercial floor that should sustain deal-making momentum even through regulatory uncertainty.
