# Acromegaly Update — Monday September 21

**Bottom line:** This week's acromegaly literature centers on long-term disease burden — cardiovascular morbidity, musculoskeletal disease, and precision-medicine treatment selection — while the rare disease industry notched a landmark ultra-rare gene therapy approval, a $1.65B radiopharma merger, and a $123M bet on an oral thyroid eye disease pill.

## Part A — Acromegaly research

**Cardiovascular Morbidity in Acromegaly during a Long-term Follow-Up – a Nationwide Cohort Study**
Antti Partanen, Elina Peltola, Heini Huhtala, et al. *European Journal of Endocrinology*, Sept 18, 2026.
PMID: [42758639](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42758639/) · DOI: [10.1093/ejendo/lvag180](https://doi.org/10.1093/ejendo/lvag180)
This Finnish nationwide study followed 565 acromegaly patients and 5,629 matched controls from 10 years before diagnosis through more than 30 years after, using hospital-visit data to track cardiovascular disease. It's one of the largest and longest-running looks yet at when heart problems actually show up relative to an acromegaly diagnosis, rather than just confirming that they occur. The researchers used Cox regression to calculate hazard ratios for cardiovascular hospital visits after diagnosis, and separately examined how treatment choices, hypopituitarism, and biochemical control influenced that risk. The scale and duration of follow-up make this a reference-quality dataset for counseling patients about long-term cardiac surveillance.

**Approach to the Patient: Precision Medicine-Guided Evaluation and Treatment of Acromegaly**
Manel Puig-Domingo, Montserrat Marques-Pamies, Miguel Sampedro, et al. *Journal of Clinical Endocrinology & Metabolism*, Sept 16, 2026.
PMID: [42314069](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42314069/) · DOI: [10.1007/s11154-024-09935-4](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11154-024-09935-4)
This review argues that acromegaly's traditional one-size-fits-all treatment ladder — starting everyone on first-generation somatostatin receptor ligands and escalating by trial and error — leaves too many patients hormonally uncontrolled for too long. The authors describe cluster analyses identifying three biologically distinct patient groups: younger patients with invasive, treatment-resistant macroadenomas; older patients with smaller, more responsive tumors; and an intermediate group often needing combination therapy. The practical pitch is to use tumor and patient characteristics up front to pick therapy, rather than waiting out months of an ineffective first-line drug.

**Impact of Pituitary Adenoma Histological Subtype on Remission and Disease Control in Acromegaly**
Alexandra Monnot, Mirela-Diana Ilie, Camille Sergeant, et al. *Journal of Clinical Endocrinology & Metabolism*, Sept 16, 2026.
PMID: [42046892](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42046892/) · DOI: [10.1210/clinem/dgag174](https://doi.org/10.1210/clinem/dgag174)
A single-center retrospective study of 128 surgery-naive patients (surgical only, no prior medical therapy) found that densely granulated, sparsely granulated, and mixed GH-PRL adenomas behave differently after surgery. Sparsely granulated tumors showed up in younger patients and, per prior literature this group builds on, tend to be harder to control. The takeaway for clinicians: histology at diagnosis could help set realistic expectations for surgical remission and flag which patients are more likely to need adjuvant medical therapy.

**Acromegaly and Cardiovascular Disease: Mechanisms, Clinical Impact, and Evolving Management**
Marta Araujo-Castro, Pedro Marques, Luis Miguel Cardoso, et al. *Endocrine Reviews*, Sept 16, 2026.
PMID: [42029663](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42029663/) · DOI: [10.1159/000545875](https://doi.org/10.1159/000545875)
A comprehensive review in one of endocrinology's highest-impact journals, this piece works as a companion to the Finnish cohort study above — it walks through how chronic GH/IGF-1 excess drives structural heart changes, arrhythmias, vascular dysfunction, hypertension, diabetes, and dyslipidemia, and reviews current approaches to catching and managing these complications early. Useful as a reference for why cardiovascular surveillance matters so much in this population.

**Dietary Interventions and Vitamin D3 Replacement in Pituitary Tumors: A Systematic Review**
L.C. Te Nijenhuis-Noort, R. van de Groef, J. Refardt, et al. *European Journal of Endocrinology*, Sept 20, 2026.
PMID: [42764013](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42764013/) · DOI: [10.1093/ejendo/lvag182](https://doi.org/10.1093/ejendo/lvag182)
This systematic review pooled interventional and observational studies on dietary strategies — across acromegaly, Cushing's disease, prolactinomas, and hypopituitarism — aimed at improving metabolic and bone outcomes. The evidence base is thin and heterogeneous (the authors used ROBINS-I and Newcastle-Ottawa risk-of-bias tools to grade it), but it's a useful signal that nutrition is getting more formal attention as an adjunct to medical and surgical management of pituitary tumor comorbidities, rather than an afterthought.

## Part B — Rare Disease Industry News

**FDA approves Ultragenyx's Fayuvi, the first gene therapy for Sanfilippo syndrome type A**
Fierce Pharma, Sept 17, 2026. [Read the article](https://www.fiercepharma.com/pharma/fda-approves-ultragenyx-gene-therapy-fayuvi-rare-neurodegenerative-disorder)
The FDA approved Fayuvi (formerly UX111) as the first treatment specifically for MPS IIIA, an ultra-rare, fatal pediatric neurodegenerative disorder sometimes called "childhood Alzheimer's" that affects an estimated 3,000–5,000 patients worldwide. The one-time AAV gene therapy delivers a working copy of the SGSH gene; in the pivotal single-arm trial, treated infants and toddlers showed a 23.2-point cognitive score benefit over natural history data, and eight of seventeen reached a developmental milestone that no untreated child in the comparison group reached. Wholesale acquisition cost is $3.95 million. It's Ultragenyx's second FDA approval in under a month, following its glycogen storage disease gene therapy, and comes as the company also absorbs a Phase 3 failure in Angelman syndrome.
*Why it matters for rare disease developers:* It's a concrete signal that FDA's rare disease review posture is thawing after a stretch of high-profile rejections and public pressure campaigns — good news for other ultra-rare gene therapy sponsors watching the agency's risk tolerance.

**ITM Isotope Technologies and Telix Pharmaceuticals merge in deal worth at least $1.65B**
STAT News, Sept 20, 2026. [Read the article](https://www.statnews.com/2026/09/20/radiopharma-companies-itm-telix-merger/)
Telix Pharmaceuticals will acquire privately held ITM Isotope Technologies Munich, with Telix potentially paying up to $700 million more in milestones if ITM's lead radiopharmaceutical — a rival to Novartis's Lutathera for neuroendocrine tumors, a rare cancer type — hits regulatory and sales targets. The tie-up creates a second well-capitalized player in a radiopharmaceutical field Novartis has dominated since launching its own isotope therapies in 2018 and 2022. STAT reported the deal followed a "surprise FDA rejection" for one of the merging companies, though full details are behind STAT's paywall.
*Why it matters for rare disease developers:* Consolidation in radiopharma suggests investors see durable demand for targeted isotope therapies in rare and orphan cancer indications, and that scale (manufacturing, isotope supply) is becoming a competitive necessity.

**Sling Therapeutics raises $123M Series C, advances oral thyroid eye disease pill to Phase 3**
BioPharma Dive, Sept 16, 2026. [Read the article](https://www.biopharmadive.com/news/sling-linsitinib-series-c-financing-thyroid-eye-disease/830452/)
Sling Therapeutics closed a $123 million Series C — led by Forbion, with TPG Life Sciences Innovations and Sectoral Asset Management — to fund a confirmatory Phase 3 trial ("Orbit") of linsitinib, an oral small molecule for thyroid eye disease (TED). If successful, it would be the first oral alternative to approved injectable/infused antibodies like Amgen's Tepezza and Viridian's Lumvoa. An earlier late-stage trial showed a 52% response rate at 24 weeks with no drug-related hearing loss or tinnitus, side effects associated with the approved biologics. Since launching in 2022 on licensed-in Astellas chemistry, Sling has now raised $200 million total.
*Why it matters for rare disease developers:* A large Series C for a Phase 3-ready oral asset shows investors are still willing to write big checks for differentiated mechanisms in crowded-but-underserved orphan/rare autoimmune indications, even amid a broader biotech funding slowdown.

## What to watch

The acromegaly literature this week is really one story told twice — from a hormonal-excess mechanism angle and a 30-year outcomes angle — that chronic GH/IGF-1 excess exacts a slow, compounding cardiovascular toll, reinforcing why early biochemical control (the subject of the precision-medicine review above) matters clinically, not just biochemically. On the industry side, the Ultragenyx approval is a reminder that regulatory momentum in ultra-rare disease can shift quickly once the political and patient-advocacy pressure builds, which is worth watching for any acromegaly-adjacent pituitary or endocrine rare disease programs currently stuck in FDA review. Keep an eye on whether the Telix-ITM radiopharma deal draws competitive responses from other players eyeing neuroendocrine and other rare tumor types.
