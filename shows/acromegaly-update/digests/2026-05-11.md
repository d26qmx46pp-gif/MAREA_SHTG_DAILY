# Acromegaly Update — Monday May 11

**Bottom line:** Acromegaly research this week centered on the long-term pharmacology of established therapies — fresh discussion of the ACROSTUDY pegvisomant dataset, a methodology-focused systematic review of pasireotide LAR cost-effectiveness, a real-world Mexican comparison of octreotide LAR vs lanreotide autogel using the SAGIT score, and progress on an oral octreotide formulation — while the broader rare disease industry absorbed a $4.1B Angelini-Catalyst takeover, an FDA reversal on the rare blood cancer therapy Ebvallo, a Phase 3 win for Cytokinetics' Myqorzo in non-obstructive hypertrophic cardiomyopathy, and a setback for Entrada's next-generation Duchenne candidate.

## Part A — Acromegaly research

Only one paper cleared the original top-tier journal filter this week (Pituitary), so the slate has been broadened to include the strongest acromegaly-relevant work from the next tier. Selection prioritizes direct therapeutic relevance to acromegaly management.

**Letter to the editor: Long-term safety and efficacy of pegvisomant monotherapy for acromegaly: final data from the full ACROSTUDY cohort.**
Fedai NK, Unsal IO, Cakal E. *Pituitary*, May 6, 2026. [PMID 42089931](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42089931/) · [doi:10.1007/s11102-026-01687-5](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11102-026-01687-5).
A correspondence engaging with the final ACROSTUDY analysis of pegvisomant monotherapy, the longest-running real-world dataset for the GH-receptor antagonist. The authors flag practical questions raised by the cohort — including how IGF-1 normalization rates compare across baseline tumor size and pre-treatment regimens, and how the long-term hepatic and pituitary-MRI safety signals should shape monitoring intervals in routine practice. The letter is short on new numbers but useful as a clinical-practice sanity check on how ACROSTUDY's pooled long-term findings translate to dose-titration decisions.

**Cost-effectiveness of pasireotide long-acting release in acromegaly: a systematic literature review and methodology assessment.**
Gilis-Januszewska A, Bronikowska M, Binowski G, et al. *Journal of Medical Economics*, 2026. [PMID 41518621](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41518621/) · [doi:10.1080/13696998.2025.2609506](https://doi.org/10.1080/13696998.2025.2609506).
Pasireotide LAR sits as a second-line option for patients unresponsive to or unsuitable for first-generation somatostatin receptor ligands, but payers have struggled to size its value. This systematic review pulls together the published cost-effectiveness models, scores their methodological quality, and lays out which design choices — comparator selection, time horizon, handling of hyperglycemia, and the way biochemical control is mapped to long-term outcomes — drive the wide spread in published incremental cost-effectiveness ratios. Useful framing for endocrinologists involved in formulary discussions and for trialists thinking about which outcomes future studies need to capture.

**[Efficacy of octreotide LAR and lanreotide autogel in acromegaly assessed by SAGIT].**
Núñez-Martínez FJ, Chávez-Lárraga AJ, Hernández-Salazar E, et al. *Revista Médica del Instituto Mexicano del Seguro Social*, May 6, 2026. [PMID 42090715](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42090715/) · [doi:10.5281/zenodo.18715331](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.18715331).
A retrospective observational comparison of the two workhorse first-generation somatostatin receptor ligands in a Mexican IMSS cohort, scored with the SAGIT instrument rather than GH/IGF-1 alone. SAGIT explicitly incorporates signs and symptoms, associated comorbidities, GH/IGF-1, tumor profile, and treatment burden, so it is closer to the patient experience than biochemical-control rates. The study addresses a clinically important question — whether the two long-acting SRLs differ on more than just biochemistry — in a population where access patterns mean such head-to-head real-world data are scarce.

**Genome architecture in endocrine diseases: X-Linked Acrogigantism (X-LAG) syndrome.**
Daly AF, Beckers A, Pétrossians P. *Annales d'Endocrinologie*, May 7, 2026. [PMID 42103581](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42103581/) · [doi:10.1016/j.ando.2026.102554](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ando.2026.102554).
X-LAG is the severe pediatric-onset end of the GH-excess spectrum, driven by duplications involving GPR101 on Xq26.3 and presenting in infancy with rapid linear growth and combined GH/IGF-1/prolactin elevation. This review from the Liège group — the lab that originally characterized the syndrome — focuses on what is now understood about the topologically associating domains around GPR101 and how disruption of the local chromatin architecture appears to drive the somatotroph phenotype. Relevant for the acromegaly community because X-LAG sits on a continuum with sporadic somatotropinomas and informs how clinicians think about gigantism cases with unusual molecular signatures.

**Effect of octreotide–deoxycholate hydrophobic ion pairing solid dispersions on intestinal permeability to enhance octreotide absorption via increased lipophilicity and ASBT-mediated transport.**
Zhang X, Xiao F, Zou M, et al. *International Journal of Pharmaceutics*, May 5, 2026. [PMID 41905700](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41905700/) · [doi:10.1016/j.ijpharm.2026.126817](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ijpharm.2026.126817).
The FDA-approved oral octreotide capsule (Mycapssa) achieves roughly 0.7% systemic bioavailability — adequate clinically but a long-standing target for improvement. This formulation paper reports hydrophobic ion-pairing of octreotide with sodium deoxycholate and demonstrates increased lipophilicity plus engagement of the apical sodium-dependent bile acid transporter (ASBT) to improve intestinal permeability in preclinical models. The work is mechanism-of-delivery rather than clinical, but it is the most active near-term lever for making oral somatostatin-analog therapy a practical maintenance option in acromegaly.

## Part B — Rare Disease Industry News

A busy week for rare disease developers, anchored by a multi-billion-dollar takeout and a meaningful FDA course-correction.

**Angelini Pharma buys Catalyst Pharmaceuticals and its rare disease drugs for $4.1B.**
Andrew Joseph, *STAT News*, May 7, 2026. [Source](https://www.statnews.com/2026/05/07/angelini-pharma-acquires-catalyst-pharmaceuticals-rare-disease-neurology-firdapse/).
Italian privately-held Angelini Pharma agreed to buy Florida-based Catalyst Pharmaceuticals for roughly $4.1 billion in cash, at $31.50 per share — a 28% premium to the 30-day average before the deal leaked on April 22. Catalyst markets three approved products, anchored by Firdapse for Lambert-Eaton myasthenic syndrome, and the deal gives Angelini its first meaningful U.S. footprint and deepens its neurology franchise. The price tag also reinforces the trend of European mid-cap pharmas paying up to enter U.S. rare disease neurology.
Why it matters for rare disease developers: another data point that strategic buyers will pay premium multiples for derisked rare disease commercial assets, particularly in neurology — supportive for valuations of similarly profiled small-cap peers.

**FDA to reconsider rare cancer drug Ebvallo after surprise rejection.**
Adam Feuerstein, *STAT News*, May 7, 2026. [Source](https://www.statnews.com/2026/05/07/fda-reconsider-rare-disease-drug-ebvallo/).
Pierre Fabre Pharmaceuticals and Atara Biotherapeutics said the FDA, in a late-April meeting, agreed that their completed single-arm trial of Ebvallo (tabelecleucel) in EBV-positive post-transplant lymphoproliferative disease is in fact sufficient to support review and possible approval — directly reversing the trial-adequacy concerns the agency had cited when it rejected the drug in January. The original review was run by CBER under Vinay Prasad, who left the agency at the end of April. STAT frames the original rejection as part of a pattern in which senior FDA officials had raised the bar for rare disease drugs, and the reversal as a meaningful softening of that stance.
Why it matters for rare disease developers: removes a chunk of recent overhang on small-trial, single-arm rare disease packages and signals that the post-Prasad CBER may be more willing to credit feasibility-of-trial arguments that companies have been raising for two years.

**Cytokinetics drug Myqorzo meets twin efficacy goals in study of genetic heart disease.**
Adam Feuerstein, *STAT News*, May 5, 2026. [Source](https://www.statnews.com/2026/05/05/cytokinetics-myqorzo-non-obstructive-hypertrophic-cardiomyopathy-trial/).
Cytokinetics reported that aficamten (sold as Myqorzo) significantly improved heart-failure symptoms and cardiovascular fitness in patients with non-obstructive hypertrophic cardiomyopathy in the Phase 3 ACACIA study, hitting both co-primary endpoints with statistical significance. Myqorzo is already approved in the more severe obstructive form of HCM; a positive non-obstructive label expansion would meaningfully enlarge the eligible HCM population and is the basis for sell-side peak sales forecasts in the $5B range.
Why it matters for rare disease developers: validates a second cardiac myosin inhibitor franchise across the HCM spectrum and tightens the competitive squeeze on Bristol Myers Squibb's mavacamten (Camzyos), with implications for any developer pursuing rare cardiomyopathies.

**Next-gen Duchenne drug from Entrada disappoints.**
Jason Mast, *STAT News*, May 7, 2026. [Source](https://www.statnews.com/2026/05/07/entrada-duchenne-exon-skipping-study-disappointment/).
Entrada Therapeutics' ENTR-601-44, the company's lead next-generation exon-skipping candidate for Duchenne muscular dystrophy patients with mutations amenable to exon 44 skipping, missed expectations in an early clinical study. The next-gen exon-skippers are designed to deliver markedly higher dystrophin levels than Sarepta's first-generation Exondys — a low bar that competitors had been clearing in preclinical and early human work — so a soft readout for Entrada raises questions about its position in an increasingly crowded field that also includes Avidity, Wave, and Dyne.
Why it matters for rare disease developers: a reminder that delivery-platform claims still need to translate into clean clinical dystrophin data; Duchenne investors are likely to reprice next-gen exon-skipping risk and tighten focus on the leaders with cleaner Phase 1/2 readouts.

**Pfizer deals extend patent life for a top-selling rare disease drug** *(context piece, April 28, 2026 — flagged as slightly outside the 7-day window because of direct relevance to rare disease IP economics).*
Ben Fidler, *BioPharma Dive*, April 28, 2026. [Source](https://www.biopharmadive.com/news/pfizer-tafamidis-vyndamax-settlement-generic-patents-ttr-cardiomyopathy/818705/).
Pfizer settled patent litigation with Dexcel, Hikma, and Cipla, extending tafamidis (Vyndaqel/Vyndamax) exclusivity through June 1, 2031 — pushing back what had been a 2028 cliff. Tafamidis is the market leader in transthyretin-mediated cardiomyopathy, with roughly $6.4 billion in 2025 sales. The settlement protects multiple billions of additional Pfizer revenue and reshapes the competitive backdrop for BridgeBio's Attruby, Alnylam's Amvuttra, and the AstraZeneca/Ionis program.
Why it matters for rare disease developers: rare disease franchises with long, patent-thicketed monopolies can be successfully defended well past their nominal cliffs — a reassuring precedent for owners of orphan-protected IP, though it stiffens the competitive headwind for next-in-class entrants.

## What to watch

The acromegaly slate is dominated by drug-delivery and real-world-evidence work, with no new pivotal trial data this week — paltusotine, oral somatostatin formulations, and combination strategies remain the open questions to watch into the back half of 2026. On the industry side, the Ebvallo reversal and the post-Prasad CBER are the bigger signal: if the agency is meaningfully more willing to credit single-arm packages, that loosens the regulatory math for any rare disease developer (acromegaly included) considering smaller, faster registration trials. And the Catalyst takeout — together with the Pfizer tafamidis defense — keeps reinforcing that strategics will still pay for rare disease cash flows when the underlying IP and label position are clean.
