## Part B — Rare Disease Industry News

**Angelini Pharma to acquire Catalyst Pharmaceuticals for $4.1B**
*STAT, May 7, 2026* — Andrew Joseph. [Source](https://www.statnews.com/2026/05/07/angelini-pharma-acquires-catalyst-pharmaceuticals-rare-disease-neurology-firdapse/)
Italian pharma Angelini said Thursday it will acquire Florida-based Catalyst Pharmaceuticals for roughly $4.1 billion in cash, valuing Catalyst at $31.50 per share — a 28% premium to the 30-day period before the deal first leaked on April 22. Catalyst markets three approved rare-disease medicines including Firdapse for Lambert-Eaton myasthenic syndrome. The transaction gives Angelini its first real U.S. foothold and is the fourth biotech acquisition announced in May alone, putting 2026 ahead of every year since at least 2018 on M&A pace.
*Why it matters for rare-disease developers:* Marketed-asset rare-disease specialists remain prime acquisition targets, particularly for European pharma seeking U.S. commercial reach — a tailwind for any company with one or two approved orphan drugs.

**FDA reverses course on Atara/Pierre Fabre's rare-cancer drug Ebvallo**
*STAT, May 7, 2026* — Adam Feuerstein. [Source](https://www.statnews.com/2026/05/07/fda-reconsider-rare-disease-drug-ebvallo/)
Atara Biotherapeutics and Pierre Fabre Pharmaceuticals announced that a late-April meeting with FDA ended with the agency walking back its primary reason for rejecting Ebvallo (tabelecleucel) in January — namely, that the companies' completed single-arm trial was insufficient. FDA now agrees the existing study can support review and potential approval. The original rejection had been issued by CBER under Vinay Prasad, who left the agency at the end of April.
*Why it matters for rare-disease developers:* The reversal hints that the rare-disease evidentiary bar at CBER may be loosening post-Prasad — directly relevant to any sponsor with a single-arm pivotal program in an ultra-rare indication.

**Entrada Therapeutics' next-gen Duchenne therapy disappoints in early study**
*STAT, May 7, 2026* — Jason Mast. [Source](https://www.statnews.com/2026/05/07/entrada-duchenne-exon-skipping-study-disappointment/)
Entrada Therapeutics reported disappointing early-trial data for its next-generation exon-skipping therapy in Duchenne muscular dystrophy, raising real questions about the company's competitiveness in a crowded class that also includes Avidity, Dyne, Wave Life Sciences, and incumbent Sarepta. Next-gen exon skippers are designed to deliver dramatically higher dystrophin levels than Sarepta's first-generation drugs, which were approved in 2016 with only marginal protein production.
*Why it matters for rare-disease developers:* Underscores how narrow the margin for differentiation has become in rare neuromuscular disease — investors and potential partners will increasingly demand both deep dystrophin and clean functional readouts before backing follow-on assets.

**Cytokinetics' Myqorzo hits both endpoints in Phase 3 ACACIA trial of non-obstructive hypertrophic cardiomyopathy**
*STAT, May 5, 2026* — Adam Feuerstein. [Source](https://www.statnews.com/2026/05/05/cytokinetics-myqorzo-non-obstructive-hypertrophic-cardiomyopathy-trial/)
Cytokinetics said its cardiac myosin inhibitor Myqorzo (aficamten) significantly improved heart-failure symptoms and cardiovascular fitness in patients with non-obstructive HCM, hitting both primary endpoints with statistical significance in the Phase 3 ACACIA trial. The drug is already in early launch for the obstructive form of HCM. If the new label is granted, analysts model peak sales as high as $5 billion per year.
*Why it matters for rare-disease developers:* A clean second Phase 3 in a genetically driven cardiomyopathy materially expands the addressable population for the leading cardiac myosin inhibitor and validates the broader "drug-for-genetic-heart-disease" investment thesis.

**Latus Bio raises $42M to push AAV Huntington's gene therapy into the clinic**
*STAT, May 4, 2026* — Allison DeAngelis. [Source](https://www.statnews.com/2026/05/04/biotech-startup-latus-bio-raises-42-million-run-huntingtons-trial/)
Latus Bio closed a $42 million round to fund the first-in-human trial of its AAV-based Huntington's disease gene therapy, building on its $97M Series A from earlier this year (covered by [BioPharma Dive](https://www.biopharmadive.com/news/latus-huntington-gene-therapy-cln2-biotech-startup/819174/)). The company is also developing a CLN2 program for late-infantile Batten disease.
*Why it matters for rare-disease developers:* Even with a broader biotech funding chill, capital is finding its way to ultra-rare neurogenetic gene therapy — likely encouraged by FDA's new bespoke-mechanism and single-arm pathways for ultra-rare indications.
