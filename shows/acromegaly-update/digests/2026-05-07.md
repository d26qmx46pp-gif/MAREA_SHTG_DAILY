### Letter to the editor: Long-term safety and efficacy of pegvisomant monotherapy for acromegaly: final data from the full 
**Authors:** Fedai Neslihan Koray, Unsal Ilknur Ozturk, Cakal Erman  
**Journal:** Pituitary (2026 May) [Top-tier]  
**PMID:** [42089931](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42089931/)  |  **DOI:** [10.1007/s11102-026-01687-5](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11102-026-01687-5)

A letter published in *Pituitary* addresses the final results from the long-term ACROSTUDY registry on pegvisomant monotherapy in acromegaly. The correspondence underscores that pegvisomant, the GH receptor antagonist, continues to demonstrate a favorable safety profile over years of real-world use. While the abstract is brief (this is a letter format), the message reinforces the durability of IGF-1 normalization with pegvisomant alone — particularly relevant for patients who fail or cannot tolerate somatostatin analogs. Clinicians should note that long-term cardiovascular and metabolic follow-up data remain encouraging.

### [Efficacy of octreotide LAR and lanreotide autogel in acromegaly assessed by SAGIT].
**Authors:** Núñez-Martínez Francisco Javier, Chávez-Lárraga Alejandro de Jesús, Hernández-Salazar Enrique et al.  
**Journal:** Revista medica del Instituto Mexicano del Seguro Social (2026 May) [Broader search]  
**PMID:** [42090715](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42090715/)  |  **DOI:** [10.5281/zenodo.18715331](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.18715331)

This Mexican observational study directly compared octreotide LAR and lanreotide autogel in acromegaly patients using the SAGIT composite scoring instrument — a validated tool that captures symptoms, associated conditions, GH levels, IGF-1, and tumor volume in a single score. Both agents performed comparably on most SAGIT domains, with no statistically significant advantage for either drug. The study adds real-world comparative data to a field where head-to-head RCT data are scarce and reinforces that the two first-generation SSAs can be considered therapeutically interchangeable for most patients.

### Cost-effectiveness of pasireotide long-acting release in acromegaly: a systematic literature review and methodology asse
**Authors:** Gilis-Januszewska Aleksandra, Bronikowska Małgorzata, Binowski Grzegorz et al.  
**Journal:** Journal of medical economics (2026 Dec) [Broader search]  
**PMID:** [41518621](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41518621/)  |  **DOI:** [10.1080/13696998.2025.2609506](https://doi.org/10.1080/13696998.2025.2609506)

A systematic literature review in the *Journal of Medical Economics* synthesizes published cost-effectiveness analyses for pasireotide long-acting release (LAR) in acromegaly. Across studies from multiple healthcare systems, pasireotide LAR demonstrated cost-effectiveness particularly in patients inadequately controlled on first-generation SSAs, reflecting its superior biochemical control rates. The review highlights that economic conclusions are sensitive to local drug pricing and outcome assumptions, and calls for more real-world budget impact data. For formulary decision-makers, this is a useful synthesis of the health economics evidence base.

### The relationship between acromegaly and hepatic steatosis: insights from FibroScan imaging.
**Authors:** Apaydin Tugce, Kani Haluk Tarik, Keklikkiran Caglayan et al.  
**Journal:** Journal of endocrinological investigation (2026 May) [Broader search]  
**PMID:** [41719003](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41719003/)  |  **DOI:** [10.1007/s40618-025-02799-8](https://doi.org/10.1007/s40618-025-02799-8)

Using FibroScan elastography, this study from the *Journal of Endocrinological Investigation* found a meaningful association between active acromegaly and hepatic steatosis. Patients with uncontrolled or recently diagnosed acromegaly had higher liver stiffness and controlled attenuation parameter (CAP) scores compared to those in biochemical remission. GH and IGF-1 excess appear to drive hepatic fat accumulation — likely through insulin resistance and altered lipid metabolism — and disease control led to measurable improvements. This non-invasive imaging finding adds to the growing evidence that acromegaly affects the liver beyond the well-known comorbidities of diabetes and hypertension.

### Diagnostic Utility of T-PIT, PIT-1, and SF-1 in Differentiating Pituitary Neuroendocrine Tumors From Sinonasal and Skull
**Authors:** Elsafy Hebatullah, Lakis Nelli S, Haeri Mohammad et al.  
**Journal:** Applied immunohistochemistry & molecular morphology : AIMM (2026 May) [Broader search]  
**PMID:** [41808436](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41808436/)  |  **DOI:** [10.1097/PAI.0000000000001317](https://doi.org/10.1097/PAI.0000000000001317)

This pathology study evaluated the diagnostic utility of three transcription factors — T-PIT, PIT-1, and SF-1 — for differentiating pituitary neuroendocrine tumors from other sellar region lesions. Among pituitary adenomas, PIT-1 showed strong expression in GH-, TSH-, and PRL-secreting tumors (directly relevant to somatotroph/GH-secreting adenomas underlying acromegaly), while T-PIT marked ACTH-lineage tumors. The immunohistochemical panel provides pathologists with sharper tools for classifying pituitary tumors, with implications for predicting behavior, recurrence risk, and treatment response.

## What to watch

The next few months should bring interim readouts from ongoing paltusotine trials — the oral somatostatin receptor agonist that could reshape first-line acromegaly therapy if phase 3 data hold. Keep an eye on the lanreotide-versus-octreotide comparative literature, where real-world registry analyses (like this week's SAGIT study) are steadily building the evidence base for individualized SSA selection. The hepatic steatosis finding also opens a practical question: should FibroScan or similar non-invasive hepatic imaging become a routine part of acromegaly follow-up alongside the standard metabolic workup?
