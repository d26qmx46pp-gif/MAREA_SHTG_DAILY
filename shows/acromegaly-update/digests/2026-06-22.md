## Part A — Acromegaly Research

### 1. **Focus on renal morphology, chronic kidney disease, and urinary system malignancies in acromegaly: report on data collected over a 40-year period**
Ercan Polat, Firlatan Yazgan Busra, Sendur Suleyman Nahit, Oguz Seda Hanife, Dagdelen Selcuk et al.
*Pituitary*, June 19, 2026
[PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42321588/) | DOI: 10.1007/s11102-026-01712-7

This retrospective single-center study of 394 acromegaly patients followed over four decades provides the most comprehensive picture yet of how long-term growth hormone excess affects the kidneys. The investigators characterized the prevalence and independent predictors of renal cysts, chronic kidney disease (CKD), and urinary-system malignancies. Renal cysts are known to be more common in acromegaly than in the general population, but this dataset — spanning 40 years — offers longitudinal granularity rarely available. The findings help clinicians anticipate and screen for renal complications in a disease that is often viewed primarily through a pituitary and cardiovascular lens.

### 2. **Co-secretion of growth hormone and prolactin defines a high-risk acromegaly phenotype**
Lee Won Kyu, Oh Hyeong-Cheol, Shim Jin-Kyung, Oh Hyeyeon, Won So Young et al.
*Pituitary*, June 18, 2026
[PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42315804/) | DOI: 10.1007/s11102-026-01713-6

This retrospective analysis compared outcomes between GH/prolactin co-secreting pituitary neuroendocrine tumors (GH/PRL-PitNETs) and pure GH-secreting tumors in acromegaly patients. Co-secreting tumors represent a biologically distinct subtype that has historically been undercharacterized in the literature, and this study finds they confer worse remission rates and a more aggressive clinical course. The implication is straightforward: identifying GH/PRL co-secretion at diagnosis may flag patients who need earlier escalation to medical therapy and closer surveillance. The data add weight to the idea that acromegaly should be subclassified at the molecular tumor level rather than managed as a single disease entity.

### 3. **Approach to the patient: Precision Medicine-Guided Evaluation and Treatment of Acromegaly**
Puig-Domingo Manel, Marques-Pamies Montserrat, Sampedro Miguel, Gil Joan, Araujo-Castro Marta et al.
*Journal of Clinical Endocrinology & Metabolism*, June 18, 2026
[PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42314069/) | DOI: 10.1210/clinem/dgag241

This clinical review — published in JCEM as an "Approach to the Patient" piece — directly challenges the traditional stepwise model of acromegaly management, in which first-generation somatostatin receptor ligands are universally deployed as the first-line medical option. The authors argue that cluster analyses consistently identify three major patient subtypes that respond differently to each class of medical therapy, and that upfront tumor profiling (including somatostatin receptor expression, AIP mutation status, and granulation pattern) should guide initial drug selection rather than empirical escalation. The review provides practical guidance for implementing precision medicine in clinical practice and directly informs current debates about where newer agents like paltusotine and pasireotide should fit in treatment sequencing.

### 4. **The renin-angiotensin system is activated by short-term, but repressed by long-term growth hormone excess**
Baumgartner Clemens, Fellinger Paul, Tosin Anna, Blanchard Anne, Metz Matthäus et al.
*European Journal of Endocrinology*, June 17, 2026
[PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42308154/) | DOI: 10.1093/ejendo/lvag107

This exploratory study used a two-pronged design — 10 healthy male volunteers exposed to short-term GH excess, plus a cohort of acromegaly patients representing long-term GH/IGF-1 excess — to probe how the renin-angiotensin system (RAS) responds to GH at different exposure durations. The finding of opposing RAS regulation (activation early, repression chronically) helps reconcile conflicting prior data and offers a mechanistic explanation for the complex blood pressure phenotype seen in acromegaly. Understanding how GH duration modulates RAS activity has implications for cardiovascular management and may inform when ACE inhibitor or ARB therapy is most relevant in this patient population.

### 5. **Cancer risk in a large acromegaly cohort: a standardized incidence ratio analysis**
Ercan Polat, Yazgan Busra Firlatan, Sendur Suleyman Nahit, Dagdelen Selcuk, Erbas Tomris
*Pituitary*, June 16, 2026
[PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42301595/) | DOI: 10.1007/s11102-026-01700-x

This rigorous analysis of 393 acromegaly patients followed from 1980 to 2023 at a single Turkish tertiary center used standardized incidence ratios (SIRs) against national cancer rates to quantify overall and site-specific cancer risk. The study employed multiple sensitivity analyses — including thyroid cancer exclusion, graded landmark timepoints, and reference rate variation — to control for surveillance bias, a critical methodological challenge in acromegaly cancer studies. The results contribute to the ongoing debate about whether long-term IGF-1 excess confers a meaningful excess cancer risk beyond thyroid malignancy, and have direct implications for cancer screening protocols in acromegaly follow-up.

### 6. **Reassuring long-term data on efficacy and safety of pegvisomant monotherapy in a large series of patients with acromegaly**
Kasuki Leandro, Heck Ansgar
*Pituitary*, June 14, 2026
[PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42289536/) | DOI: 10.1007/s11102-026-01708-3

*Note: Abstract not yet indexed in PubMed at time of publication.* Based on the title, this report from Kasuki and Heck presents long-term efficacy and safety data for pegvisomant monotherapy in a large patient series — likely the most comprehensive real-world pegvisomant dataset published to date. Pegvisomant, as a GH receptor antagonist rather than a somatostatin analog, remains the most potent biochemical normalizer available and is a key option for patients with SSA resistance. Long-term safety data supporting its use should strengthen the evidence base for earlier and more sustained pegvisomant deployment.

## Part B — Rare Disease Industry News

### 1. **FDA reverses course on Regenxbio's childhood gene therapy for Hunter syndrome after rejection**
*STAT News* — June 22, 2026
[Source](https://www.statnews.com/2026/06/22/fda-reverses-course-regenxbio-gene-therapy-navsunli-hunter-syndrome/)

The FDA has announced it will reconsider approving Regenxbio's experimental gene therapy for mucopolysaccharidosis type II (Hunter syndrome), which it had rejected just four months ago. Children with the neurological form of Hunter syndrome face progressive cognitive decline because the enzyme replacement therapy that addresses the condition cannot cross the blood-brain barrier — making a gene therapy that addresses the underlying CNS pathology critically important. This reversal is the latest in a series of FDA U-turns since the departure of former Commissioner Marty Makary and other Trump-era agency leaders, with the agency now appearing significantly more receptive to rare disease gene therapies that were previously rebuffed on evidence grounds.

*Why it matters for rare disease developers:* A second straight FDA reversal on a gene therapy for a neurological orphan disease signals a structural, not incidental, shift at the agency — one that could accelerate filing timelines and lower the evidentiary threshold for developers working in ultra-rare CNS indications.

### 2. **UniQure stock surges 80% after FDA U-turn clears path for Huntington's disease gene therapy filing**
*Fierce Biotech* — June 17, 2026
[Source](https://www.fiercebiotech.com/biotech/uniqure-stock-surges-after-fda-u-turn-clears-path-gene-therapy-filing)

The FDA reversed its prior position and told uniQure that a three-year analysis from a Phase 1/2 study would be acceptable as the primary basis for an accelerated approval application for AMT-130 in Huntington's disease — the same data package the agency had previously deemed insufficient. UniQure plans to file the biologics license application in the third quarter of 2026. The company still needs to reach agreement with the FDA on the confirmatory study design, with the agency seeking to align on using a concurrent standard-of-care control rather than the previously requested sham procedure. UniQure shares surged 80% on the news, with spillover gains also seen in Regenxbio and Rezolute shares.

*Why it matters for rare disease developers:* The FDA's new willingness to accept single-arm and Phase 1/2 data as the primary evidence base for rare disease accelerated approvals represents a significant policy inflection point that could benefit any rare disease sponsor currently weighing whether their Phase 2 data is sufficient to file.

### What to Watch

In acromegaly, this week's science points clearly in one direction: the field is moving away from one-size-fits-all somatostatin analog therapy and toward molecular subtyping at diagnosis. The JCEM precision medicine piece, taken alongside the GH/PRL co-secretion data, makes a compelling case that tumor biology — not just biochemistry — should drive treatment sequencing. For acromegaly drug developers, these findings argue for richer patient stratification in future clinical trials.

Zooming out to the rare disease space, the FDA appears to be systematically revisiting prior rejections across gene therapy indications, and the pattern is consistent enough to be actionable for sponsors. If the agency's new leadership is willing to accept Phase 1/2 data as the basis for accelerated approval in rare CNS diseases, that could compress development timelines for conditions that have historically struggled to power late-stage trials. The dual reversal on Huntington's (uniQure) and Hunter syndrome (Regenxbio) in a single week is not coincidence — it is a regulatory signal worth watching closely.
