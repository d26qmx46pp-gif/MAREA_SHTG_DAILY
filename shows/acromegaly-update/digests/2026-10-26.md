# Acromegaly Update — Monday October 26

**Bottom line:** A thin top-tier week for acromegaly (one Pituitary paper plus a handful of solid next-tier studies, including a 2026 pipeline review), while rare disease news was dominated by positive Phase 3 readouts from Disc Medicine and Roivant, a second FDA look at Biohaven's ataxia drug, and early Huntington's data from Alnylam.

*Note: only one paper met the top-tier journal filter this week; the rest are from the next-best tier of journals.*

## Part A — Acromegaly research

**Proteomic profiling of somatotropinoma subtypes reveals TNIP1 overexpression in sparsely granulated tumors in a small discovery cohort**
Kaur V, Chatterjee D, Medenica S, et al. *Pituitary* (top-tier). [PMID 42927587](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42927587/) · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11102-026-01778-3)
Sparsely granulated somatotroph tumors behave more aggressively than densely granulated ones, but why is unclear. Using mass spectrometry on a small discovery set (3 vs 3 tumors), then validating in larger tissue sets, the team found TNIP1 higher and COQ6 lower in sparsely granulated tumors. TNIP1 tracked with higher IGF-1, larger and more invasive tumors, and SSTR5 expression. It is early, small-cohort work, but it points to candidate markers of aggressive disease.

**New and emerging pharmacological treatment options for acromegaly: a 2026 update**
Wildemberg LE, Marques NV, Kasuki L, et al. *Expert Opin Pharmacother*. [PMID 42878008](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42878008/) · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1080/14656566.2026.2750961)
A narrative review of what is new in acromegaly drug treatment. It covers long-acting subcutaneous octreotide (CAM2029), oral paltusotine, extended-interval Debio 4126, subcutaneous pasireotide depots (CAM4071), the growth hormone receptor antagonist MAR002, and GH-neutralizing antibodies, alongside biomarker-guided, personalized treatment approaches. The authors conclude these options could improve convenience and control for patients not well served today.

**Structured physiotherapy improves functional outcomes in acromegalic arthropathy after biochemical remission: a prospective controlled study**
Yilmaz MK, Coskun E, Karanfil B, et al. *Endocr Connect*. [PMID 42895208](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42895208/) · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1530/EC-26-0506)
Joint symptoms often persist after hormone control. In 13 patients in remission versus 13 matched knee osteoarthritis patients, a 15-session knee physiotherapy program significantly improved pain and function; 85% met the clinically meaningful threshold for pain and 100% for function. Stiffness improved least, and higher pre-treatment IGF-1 predicted less stiffness improvement. Small study, but it supports early musculoskeletal assessment.

**Thymic hyperplasia in patients with acromegaly: imaging-based assessment and clinical implications**
Koylu NY, Basaran H, Yazgan BF, et al. *Arch Endocrinol Metab*. [PMID 42905597](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42905597/) · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.20945/2359-4292-2026-0122)
On chest CT, the thymus was visible in 80.3% of 66 acromegaly patients versus 44.6% of 65 controls, and larger (median 13.1 vs 10.3 cm³). Volume did not correlate with GH or IGF-1. The takeaway for clinicians: consider acromegaly when an anterior mediastinal mass is found, before invasive procedures.

**Macrophage-secreted factors promote invasiveness in somatotroph PitNETs: Cathepsin Z as a macrophage-associated marker**
Chen M, Duan L, Miao H, et al. *Mol Cell Endocrinol*. [PMID 42476336](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42476336/) · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.mce.2026.112874)
Cathepsin Z co-localized with macrophages in human somatotroph tumors, and macrophage-conditioned medium made GH3 tumor cells more proliferative, invasive, and GH-secreting, apparently via TGF-β signaling. Recombinant CTSZ alone had no direct effect, so macrophage-derived factors, not CTSZ itself, appear to drive the effect. Preclinical mechanism work.

*Also noted:* a review of ectopic acromegaly from GH/GHRH-producing neuroendocrine tumors (PMID 42774755); a systematic review of pasireotide LAR cost-effectiveness (PMID 41518621); and a correction notice for an MRI radiomics paper (PMID 42861400). PMID 42798838 was covered in a prior episode and omitted.

## Part B — Rare Disease Industry News

**Disc Medicine's bitopertin hits all goals in Phase 3 EPP trial**
BioPharma Dive, Oct 19, 2026 — https://www.biopharmadive.com/news/Disc-bitopertin-apollo-erythropoietic-protoporphyria-study-results/833327/
Disc reported that bitopertin met all primary and secondary objectives in a Phase 3 study in erythropoietic protoporphyria. At six months it cut the toxic compound PPIX by about 46% versus just over 4% on placebo, and added 12.2 hours of pain-free time in light versus placebo. The FDA had declined an accelerated approval in February; Disc plans to resubmit by the end of 2026 and could launch in 2027. Dizziness was the most common treatment-related side effect.
*Why it matters for rare disease developers:* a clear example of a Phase 3 readout rescuing a drug after an accelerated-approval rejection.

**Roivant's Priovant: brepocitinib wins two Phase 3 trials in non-infectious uveitis**
BioPharma Dive, Oct 21, 2026 — https://www.biopharmadive.com/news/roivant-brepocitinib-uveitis-study-results/833569/
In the Clarity-1 and Clarity-2 trials, patients on brepocitinib went a median of more than 48 weeks before treatment failure versus 12 on placebo, and all secondary endpoints were met. The drug is already approved as Lisraya for dermatomyositis; a supplemental filing is planned for the first half of next year. One analyst estimated peak sales for the eye indication near $5 billion.
*Why it matters:* shows the label-expansion playbook for rare-disease drugs into larger adjacent indications.

**Biohaven's rejected ataxia drug gets a fresh FDA review**
BioPharma Dive, Oct 22, 2026 — https://www.biopharmadive.com/news/biohaven-troriluzole-vyglxia-sca-fda-accept-application/833703/
The FDA accepted Biohaven's resubmission of Vyglxia for spinocerebellar ataxia, plans an advisory panel, and expects a decision in Q1 2027. It was rejected in November 2025 over trial design concerns. Shares rose about 25%; analysts cited FDA leadership changes and stated interest in rare-disease flexibility, though one cautioned outcomes depend on how far that flexibility goes.
*Why it matters:* a live test of FDA flexibility on single-trial rare disease packages.

**Alnylam's brezosiran lowers mutant huntingtin in early Huntington's study**
STAT News, Oct 23, 2026 (partly paywalled) — https://statnews.com/2026/10/23/huntingtons-alnylam-gene-silencing-brezosiran
Alnylam reported that at the highest dose, mutant huntingtin in spinal fluid fell about 60–64% on average depending on the assay, with an "encouraging" safety profile. A placebo-controlled study to test disease slowing is planned for next year. Based on the visible portion of the article.
*Why it matters:* RNAi enters the Huntington's race; the real test is clinical benefit.

**Mark Cuban's Cost Plus Drugs moves into custom rare disease medicines**
STAT News, Oct 21, 2026 (partly paywalled) — https://www.statnews.com/2026/10/21/mark-cuban-cost-plus-drugs-rare-disease-medicines/
Cost Plus Drugs has begun receiving shipments of individualized genetic medicines at its Dallas facility, including custom molecules for single-patient mutations. Based on the visible portion of the article.
*Why it matters:* a possible new manufacturing and access channel for N-of-1 therapies.

## What to watch
In acromegaly, the pipeline review underscores how many convenience-driven options (oral paltusotine, long-acting depots) are converging, while tumor-biology work like TNIP1 and macrophage signaling hints at future markers of aggressive disease. In the wider rare disease world, Biohaven's Q1 2027 decision and Disc's year-end resubmission will show how the FDA treats rare-disease packages that were previously rejected.
