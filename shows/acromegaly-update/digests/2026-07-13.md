# Acromegaly Update — Monday July 13

**Bottom line:** It was a quiet week for new acromegaly research, but the rare disease industry delivered its biggest story of the year — Vertex's $10 billion acquisition of Crinetics Pharmaceuticals, maker of the acromegaly drug Palsonify, directly linking this week's two segments.

## Part A — Acromegaly research

**Long-term Cancer Risk in Acromegaly: A Population-Based Cohort Study with 44 Years of Follow-up**
Authors: Christian Rosendal, Mai Christiansen Arlien-Søborg, Marianne Skovsager Andersen, Claus Larsen Feltoft, Marianne Klose, Niels Henrik Bruun, Eigil Husted Nielsen, Olaf M Dekkers, Jens Otto Lunde Jørgensen, Jakob Dal
Journal: European Journal of Endocrinology (July 6, 2026)
[PMID: 42405492](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42405492/) | DOI: [10.1093/ejendo/lvag121](https://doi.org/10.1093/ejendo/lvag121)

This Danish nationwide cohort study followed 809 biochemically confirmed acromegaly patients (versus 80,900 matched controls) for up to 44 years — one of the longest-running looks yet at cancer risk in this population. Overall cancer risk was not clearly elevated, but the study found a real signal for two specific cancers: colorectal cancer risk was up 78%, and thyroid cancer risk was more than tripled, compared to the general population. Colorectal cancers in acromegaly patients also tended to be caught earlier (more localized) than in controls, likely reflecting more frequent colonoscopy screening in this group. All-cause mortality was modestly elevated, but cancer-specific mortality was not. The takeaway for clinicians: acromegaly patients don't appear to face a broadly elevated cancer burden, but colorectal and thyroid cancer surveillance still deserve specific attention.

*Note: a second acromegaly-related item indexed this week — an authors' reply to a letter to the editor regarding the ACROSTUDY pegvisomant safety data — was a brief editorial exchange with no new data, so it isn't covered as a standalone item.*

## Part B — Rare Disease Industry News

**Vertex to acquire Crinetics Pharmaceuticals for $10 billion, gaining the acromegaly drug Palsonify**
BioPharma Dive, July 7, 2026 — [https://www.biopharmadive.com/news/vertex-crinetics-acquire-deal-palsonify-acromegaly/824545/](https://www.biopharmadive.com/news/vertex-crinetics-acquire-deal-palsonify-acromegaly/824545/)
Vertex Pharmaceuticals announced its largest-ever acquisition: an all-cash, $85-per-share ($10 billion) deal for Crinetics Pharmaceuticals, a 102% premium to Crinetics' prior closing price. Crinetics markets Palsonify (paltusotine), an oral somatostatin receptor agonist cleared last September for acromegaly, plus atumelnant, a late-stage candidate for congenital adrenal hyperplasia that Vertex believes could also extend into Cushing's syndrome. Vertex says the two drugs could eventually generate over $5 billion in combined peak annual sales; some analysts questioned whether the price is too rich given competition from Pfizer, Novartis, and Neurocrine in these markets. The deal is expected to close in Q3 2026.
Why it matters for rare disease developers: this is the clearest signal yet that large pharma sees durable commercial value in acromegaly and other rare endocrine disorders — and it puts a concrete price tag on what a differentiated oral therapy in this space can command.

**FDA approves Vera Therapeutics' Trutakna for IgA nephropathy**
STAT News, July 7, 2026 — [https://www.statnews.com/2026/07/07/vera-therapeutics-kidney-disease-treatment-approval/](https://www.statnews.com/2026/07/07/vera-therapeutics-kidney-disease-treatment-approval/)
The FDA approved Trutakna, a new Vera Therapeutics medicine, for IgA nephropathy (IgAN), a chronic autoimmune kidney disease that causes progressive loss of kidney function. Coverage of the approval was limited by a paywall beyond the initial announcement and CEO comment, so additional trial or label detail isn't available at this time.
Why it matters for rare disease developers: IgAN has become a crowded, closely watched category for biotechs chasing orphan and quasi-orphan kidney indications, and a new approval adds another data point on the FDA's bar for this disease.

**Roche discontinues two Huntington's disease gene-silencing programs**
STAT News (The Readout), July 10, 2026 — [https://www.statnews.com/2026/07/10/biotech-news-roche-ends-huntingtons-gene-silencing-program/](https://www.statnews.com/2026/07/10/biotech-news-roche-ends-huntingtons-gene-silencing-program/)
Roche has abandoned two gene-silencing drug programs for Huntington's disease after disappointing data, according to STAT's Readout newsletter. Full detail on the specific data or programs affected is behind STAT's paywall.
Why it matters for rare disease developers: another reminder that gene-silencing approaches to neurodegenerative rare disease remain a high-risk, high-attrition category, even for well-resourced players.

**ARPA-H commits $160 million to custom gene-editing treatments for rare diseases**
STAT News, July 9, 2026 — [https://www.statnews.com/2026/07/09/arpa-h-160-million-custom-gene-editing-funding-rare-disease/](https://www.statnews.com/2026/07/09/arpa-h-160-million-custom-gene-editing-funding-rare-disease/)
ARPA-H, the U.S. government's "moonshot" health research agency, launched a program called THRIVE that will fund seven research teams over several years to turn one-off, personalized gene-editing treatments (in the mold of the "Baby KJ" case) into scalable systems for a range of rare diseases. Each team is expected to reach clinical trials by year three of the program.
Why it matters for rare disease developers: government funding aimed specifically at making custom gene editing repeatable — rather than one bespoke case at a time — could open a new pathway for ultra-rare, N-of-1-style conditions that don't support traditional drug development economics.
See also: [Fierce Biotech coverage of the broader 2026 biotech M&A wave](https://www.fiercebiotech.com/biotech/ipsen-gets-memo-penning-eu700m-deal-buy-rare-disease-biotech-clinical-stage-asset) touches on the same dealmaking environment driving the Vertex-Crinetics deal.

## What to watch

The Vertex-Crinetics deal is the story that bridges both halves of this week's digest: a $10 billion bet that Palsonify, an oral acromegaly therapy less than a year old commercially, has room to grow into a genuine blockbuster despite competition from established players — a thesis that will be tested in real time as Vertex integrates the drug and pushes atumelnant toward approval in congenital adrenal hyperplasia and, potentially, Cushing's syndrome. Meanwhile, this week's lone acromegaly research paper is a reminder that even well-controlled acromegaly carries specific, actionable cancer risks worth monitoring long after biochemical control is achieved. Watch for Vertex's Q3 close timeline and any read-through to how acquirers value differentiated oral endocrine therapies more broadly.
