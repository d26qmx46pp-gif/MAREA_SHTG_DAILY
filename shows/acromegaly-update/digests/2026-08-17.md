# Acromegaly Update — Monday August 17

**Bottom line:** It's an unusually rich week for acromegaly research — five new papers spanning a first-in-human antagonist trial and two large Swedish outcomes studies — while the rare disease industry saw a $1.3B rare-epilepsy buyout, a bankruptcy-auction gene therapy pickup, and a safety scare over a newly approved Prader-Willi drug.

## Part A — Acromegaly research

**A Randomized First-in-human Study of a Novel Growth Hormone Receptor Peptide Antagonist ALXN2420 in Healthy Subjects.** Allas S, Ravel G, Farrell C, et al. *European Journal of Endocrinology.* PMID: [42573163](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42573163/). DOI: [10.1093/ejendo/lvag148](https://doi.org/10.1093/ejendo/lvag148).
This phase 1 study tested ALXN2420, a growth hormone receptor antagonist peptide being developed as an add-on for acromegaly patients whose IGF-1 isn't fully controlled by somatostatin receptor ligands like octreotide or lanreotide. In 101 healthy volunteers, single and two-week multiple doses up to 120 mg/day were well tolerated with no safety concerns, and the drug had an approximately 22-hour half-life. Doses of 20 mg and above produced dose-dependent, sustained drops in IGF-1, with effects lasting up to 72 hours after a single dose and through the end of a two-week dosing course. It's early-stage data in healthy people rather than patients, but it lays groundwork for combination therapy in harder-to-control acromegaly.

**Acromegaly is associated with an increased incidence of primary malignant tumors: data from a national study in Sweden.** Tsatsaris E, Robèrt J, Burman P, et al. *The Journal of Clinical Endocrinology and Metabolism.* PMID: [41913644](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41913644/). DOI: [10.1210/clinem/dgag137](https://doi.org/10.1210/clinem/dgag137).
Using Swedish national registries, researchers matched 1,035 acromegaly patients to over 10,000 population controls and tracked cancer outcomes. Acromegaly was linked to significantly higher rates of colorectal, lung, hematologic, and (in women) breast cancer, with elevated risk emerging as early as five years before diagnosis. Cancer-related death was only significantly higher in the 40–60 age group, and patients whose IGF-1 stayed elevated after treatment had higher overall mortality — but not more cancer specifically — than those who reached biochemical control. It reinforces guideline emphasis on cancer screening and tight IGF-1 control in these patients.

**Cardiovascular morbidity and mortality in acromegaly-associated diabetes: a Swedish Nationwide study.** Milioto A, Ragnarsson O, Ferone D, et al. *The Journal of Clinical Endocrinology and Metabolism.* PMID: [42573188](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42573188/). DOI: [10.1210/clinem/dgag322](https://doi.org/10.1210/clinem/dgag322).
This companion nationwide study compared 348 patients with acromegaly-associated diabetes to 3,480 matched patients with ordinary type 2 diabetes. Despite similar age and diabetes duration, the acromegaly group had much higher baseline cardiovascular disease (61% vs. 43%), including more hypertension, heart failure, arrhythmia, and stroke, and a 55% higher hazard of cardiovascular events going forward, driven largely by venous thromboembolism and arrhythmia. Overall mortality was numerically but not statistically significantly higher. The takeaway for clinicians: diabetes in acromegaly carries a distinct, heavier cardiovascular burden than typical type 2 diabetes and may warrant closer cardiovascular monitoring.

**Postural Stability, Fall Risk, and Effects of Balance-Based Exercise in Acromegaly.** Yorulmaz Kaya R, Sansal E, Sodan HN, et al. *European Journal of Endocrinology.* PMID: [42592857](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42592857/). DOI: [10.1093/ejendo/lvag154](https://doi.org/10.1093/ejendo/lvag154).
A smaller case-control study (32 acromegaly patients vs. 32 matched healthy controls) found that acromegaly patients had worse postural stability, higher fall risk, and more fear of falling than controls, with active disease associated with worse balance than inactive disease. An 8-week home-based balance exercise program improved outcomes in the subset who completed it. It's a modest sample, but it flags fall risk as an underappreciated, modifiable complication of acromegaly.

*Editorial context:* **Paltusotine and Its Place in the Treatment Armamentarium for Acromegaly.** Yuen KCJ. *The Journal of Clinical Endocrinology and Metabolism.* PMID: [41555064](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41555064/). DOI: [10.1210/clinem/dgag012](https://doi.org/10.1210/clinem/dgag012). An editorial/commentary (no abstract available) discussing how the oral somatostatin receptor agonist paltusotine fits alongside existing injectable therapies — flagged here for context rather than new data.

## Part B — Rare Disease Industry News

**Jazz Pharmaceuticals to buy rare-epilepsy drugmaker Actio Biosciences for up to $1.3B.** BioPharma Dive, Aug. 10, 2026. [Source](https://www.biopharmadive.com/news/jazz-actio-acquisition-deal-rare-neurological-epilepsy/827409/).
Jazz agreed to acquire privately held Actio Biosciences, paying $820 million upfront plus up to $500 million in milestones. Actio's lead drug, ABS-1230, targets KCNT1-related epilepsy, a rare and severe seizure disorder with no approved treatments that affects an estimated 2,500 people in the U.S.; it recently showed "meaningful seizure reductions" in a proof-of-concept trial and is now in a roughly 55-patient study that could support a future approval filing. As part of the deal, Actio will spin out a separate company (with Jazz holding a minority stake) focused on an early-stage Charcot-Marie-Tooth disease program. *Why it matters for rare disease developers:* it's another data point in this year's surge of private biotech buyouts — 19 in the first half of 2026 alone — and shows large pharma still paying up for rare neurological pipelines even pre-approval.

**PTC Therapeutics picks up Sangamo's Fabry disease gene therapy out of bankruptcy.** BioPharma Dive, Aug. 13, 2026. [Source](https://www.biopharmadive.com/news/ptc-sangamo-fabry-gene-therapy-bankruptcy-sale-lilly/827767/).
PTC agreed to pay up to $211 million ($111 million upfront, with $80 million and $20 million milestones tied to accelerated and full FDA approval) to acquire ST-920, an experimental one-time gene therapy for Fabry disease, from Sangamo Therapeutics, which is selling assets through Chapter 11 bankruptcy proceedings. PTC plans to finish the FDA application already underway and potentially launch in 2027; separately, Eli Lilly won a competing piece of the Sangamo estate (its zinc finger technology platform) for $50 million. *Why it matters for rare disease developers:* it's a case study in distressed-asset M&A — a promising late-stage rare disease program surviving its originator's collapse — and a sign that large pharma will still bet on gene therapy despite recent regulatory turbulence in that space.

**Newly approved Prader-Willi drug Vykat XR faces safety scrutiny after patient deaths.** STAT News, Aug. 12, 2026. [Source](https://www.statnews.com/2026/08/12/neurocrine-biosciences-vykat-prader-willi/).
A group of physicians and Prader-Willi syndrome experts notified clinicians that seven patients have died and more than 100 serious adverse events (mostly hospitalizations for swelling, respiratory, and heart complications) have been reported to the FDA since Vykat XR, sold by Neurocrine Biosciences, was approved in March 2025 to curb the disease's characteristic insatiable hunger. The group and Neurocrine both stress that none of the deaths or events has been definitively linked to the drug — the statement's stated purpose is simply to raise clinician awareness of the risk. *Why it matters for rare disease developers:* it's a reminder that post-marketing safety surveillance carries outsized weight in small, closely watched rare disease populations, where a handful of adverse events can quickly become a program-defining story.

## What to watch

Between this week's Swedish data confirming that acromegaly patients carry meaningfully elevated cardiovascular and cancer risk, and the ongoing scrutiny of Vykat XR's post-approval safety profile, the throughline is the same: for small patient populations, long-term surveillance after a drug reaches the market is proving just as consequential as the pivotal trial that got it there. On the deal side, rare disease assets — whether pre-revenue (Actio), bankrupt (Sangamo), or aging (ALXN2420 following on from earlier antagonists) — remain some of the most actively bid-for pipelines in biopharma.
