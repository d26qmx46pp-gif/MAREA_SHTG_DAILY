# Acromegaly Update — Monday May 18

**Bottom line:** New proteomic biomarkers could sharpen acromegaly diagnosis beyond GH/IGF-1, while the rare disease industry absorbed a $4.1B M&A deal and confronted a meaningful gene therapy safety-efficacy tradeoff — a tension with direct relevance to future pituitary tumor treatment development.

## Part A — Acromegaly Research

*Top-tier journal filter applied (Pituitary, European Journal of Endocrinology, Endocrine Reviews, etc.); 5 papers retrieved, 4 selected on relevance.*

---

**Targeted Proteomics Reveals Novel Biomarkers of Disease Activity and Inflammation in Acromegaly**
Godang K, Wessel Maren I, Khan Y, Ueland T, Lekva T, et al.
*European Journal of Endocrinology* — May 12, 2026
[PMID 42120545](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42120545/) | [DOI 10.1093/ejendo/lvag083](https://doi.org/10.1093/ejendo/lvag083)

Researchers enrolled 37 acromegaly patients and used a high-throughput Olink proximity extension assay to profile 184 plasma proteins at baseline, after presurgical somatostatin receptor ligand (SRL) treatment, and at follow-up post-surgery. Thirty-seven proteins were significantly differentially expressed before versus after disease control, with several correlating strongly with GH and IGF-1 levels. Enrichment analysis highlighted extracellular matrix remodeling and systemic inflammation as dominant biological themes — consistent with the cardiovascular and metabolic comorbidities seen in the disease. Notably, five proteins showed a temporal response to SRL treatment, raising the possibility of pharmacodynamic monitoring beyond GH/IGF-1 alone. The findings suggest that standard two-biomarker biochemical testing may miss clinically important disease activity, and that a proteomic panel could offer both finer diagnostic accuracy and insight into end-organ effects.

**Recent Progress in the Understanding of Psychological Complications of Acromegaly: Toward a Longitudinal Clinical Phenotype**
Martino G, Silvestro O, Ferraù F, Cannavò S
*Pituitary* — May 13, 2026
[PMID 42126760](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42126760/) | [DOI 10.1007/s11102-026-01685-7](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11102-026-01685-7)

This review synthesizes cross-sectional, longitudinal, and registry evidence to propose a longitudinal clinical phenotype for the psychological burden of acromegaly. The authors argue that biochemical remission frequently fails to translate into psychological recovery — a well-recognized but underaddressed phenomenon. They frame the burden along a temporal gradient: health-related quality of life (HRQoL) captures persistent functional and somatic sequelae; emotional distress reflects adaptive strain during disease transitions; and frank psychiatric morbidity emerges from cumulative dysfunction. Treatment burden, chronic arthropathy/pain, and subtle cognitive deficits are identified as mediators. The paper calls for routine longitudinal psychological assessment and integrated management of residual structural burden, representing a practical framework clinicians can apply today.

**Clinicopathological Features and Outcomes of Immature PIT-1 Lineage Tumors: A High-Risk Pituitary Neuroendocrine Tumor Subtype per WHO 2022**
Ozkahraman AU, Pekmezci A, Karatay H, Akpinar E, Erkan B, et al.
*Pituitary* — May 13, 2026
[PMID 42126653](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42126653/) | [DOI 10.1007/s11102-026-01691-9](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11102-026-01691-9)

This retrospective single-center series characterized 13 patients with confirmed immature PIT-1 lineage tumors — a WHO 2022 high-risk category of pituitary neuroendocrine tumor (PitNET) — treated between 2022 and 2024. Of these, 38.5% presented with acromegaly (GH-secreting), and another 7.7% had combined GH and prolactin co-secretion, making this subtype directly relevant to acromegaly practice. All had macroadenomas (median diameter 28 mm); 38.5% showed cavernous sinus invasion; residual disease persisted in 46% after maximal resection; and 31% required reoperation within 16 months. Cytologic atypia was present in all cases and a plurihormonal phenotype in 77%. The data reinforce that immature PIT-1 lineage tumors carry a genuinely aggressive course that standard surgical management often cannot control, underscoring the need for medical adjuncts and closer surveillance.

**Pituitary Adenoma and Intracerebral Aneurysms: Case Series, Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis**
Marino Picciola V, Borghesi M, Trombin V, Chirico S, Ambrosio MR, et al.
*Pituitary* — May 16, 2026
[PMID 42142200](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42142200/) | [DOI 10.1007/s11102-026-01690-w](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11102-026-01690-w)

This PRISMA-compliant meta-analysis pooled 71 studies (5,264 aggregate patients) to quantify the prevalence of intracranial aneurysms (IAs) in pituitary adenoma patients. Pooled IA prevalence was 4% overall — significantly higher than the general population — with sex-stratified estimates of 8% in women and 6% in men (both p < 0.01 versus population norms). Most adenomas were macroadenomas (91.9%), predominantly non-functioning (40.5%), and IAs were most commonly in the cavernous segment of the internal carotid artery. While not acromegaly-specific, the co-occurrence has practical implications for pre-surgical imaging planning in GH-secreting adenomas, where larger tumor size and vascular remodeling from excess GH may further elevate aneurysm risk.

**Angelini Pharma Acquires Catalyst Pharmaceuticals and Its Rare Disease Drugs for $4.1 Billion**
*STAT News* — May 7, 2026
[Source](https://www.statnews.com/2026/05/07/angelini-pharma-acquires-catalyst-pharmaceuticals-rare-disease-neurology-firdapse/)

Italian pharma company Angelini Pharma announced it will acquire Florida-based Catalyst Pharmaceuticals in an all-cash deal valued at approximately $4.1 billion — a 28% premium to Catalyst's 30-day average share price before April 22, when deal speculation became public. Catalyst holds three approved drugs, including Firdapse (amifampridine) for Lambert-Eaton myasthenic syndrome, a rare neuromuscular disease, along with treatments in neurology and rare disease. The acquisition gives Angelini its first substantial foothold in the U.S. market and meaningfully expands its neurology-rare disease portfolio. Deal close is pending customary regulatory review.

*Why it matters for rare disease developers:* A $4.1B exit premium demonstrates continued strong acquirer appetite for companies with approved rare-disease drugs in hand — validating the regulatory and commercial pathway for neuromuscular rare disease assets at a time of broader biopharma M&A acceleration.

**Astellas Resumes Gene Therapy Trials for X-Linked Myotubular Myopathy, Five Years After Fatal Patient Deaths**
*STAT News* — May 11, 2026
[Source](https://www.statnews.com/2026/05/11/astellas-gene-therapy-trials-resume-promising-myotubular-myopathy-treatment/)

Astellas has restarted clinical trials of its gene therapy for X-linked myotubular myopathy (XLMTM), a fatal muscle-wasting disease affecting infant boys, five years after the original program was halted following the deaths of several children with pre-existing liver disease. The therapy showed remarkable early efficacy — some treated boys who required continuous ventilator support and airway suctioning were able to breathe independently after treatment — but severe liver toxicity in a subset of patients caused the original trial to be suspended. Astellas has since redesigned the program with additional safety protocols and patient selection criteria. The resumption represents a high-stakes second attempt for a rare disease community that has lived with the bittersweet memory of both miraculous outcomes and devastating losses.

*Why it matters for rare disease developers:* XLMTM's story is a defining case study in the rare disease gene therapy risk-benefit calculus — this restart will be closely watched as a precedent for how sponsors can responsibly reopen programs after fatal adverse events.

**Sirolimus Used to Improve Gene Therapy Safety May Also Blunt Its Effectiveness, Encoded Therapeutics Study Finds**
*STAT News* — May 13, 2026
[Source](https://www.statnews.com/2026/05/13/encoded-therapeutics-dravet-syndrome-study-sirolimus-safety-tradeoff/)

Encoded Therapeutics reported findings from a 21-patient pediatric trial of its gene therapy for Dravet syndrome — a severe genetic epilepsy — that raise an important concern for the broader gene therapy field. Sirolimus (rapamycin), increasingly co-administered with gene therapies to blunt immune responses against the AAV viral vectors used to deliver new genes into the brain, appeared to reduce the therapy's overall effectiveness in the subset of children who received it, compared to children treated with steroids alone. The finding is a heads-up for companies including Sarepta and Regenxbio that also use sirolimus-based immunosuppression regimens in their gene therapy programs. The study does not definitively settle causality, but it raises the possibility that the immune-protection strategy currently relied upon by much of the field may carry a therapeutic cost.

*Why it matters for rare disease developers:* If sirolimus consistently blunts gene therapy efficacy, companies will need to rethink immunosuppression protocol design — a challenge that affects virtually every AAV-based program in development for rare diseases today.

**Capsida Biotherapeutics Still Cannot Explain Child's Death in Gene Therapy Trial**
*STAT News* — May 12, 2026
[Source](https://www.statnews.com/2026/05/12/capsida-gene-therapy-trial-death-protein-target-adam-15/)

Capsida Biotherapeutics disclosed that its investigation into the death of a child enrolled in one of its gene therapy trials — which occurred last September — remains unresolved, in part because the treating hospital has declined to share tissue samples from an autopsy. Capsida's scientists have been unable to determine whether the therapy's target protein, ADAM-15, played a role. The disclosure underscores persistent safety transparency challenges in early gene therapy trials and raises regulatory questions about data-sharing obligations between trial sites and sponsors after serious adverse events.

*Why it matters for rare disease developers:* This case may accelerate FDA and IRB scrutiny of safety data-sharing agreements between sponsors and enrolling institutions — a process issue that could add complexity and cost to early-phase rare disease gene therapy trials.

## What to Watch

On the acromegaly science front, the proteomics paper from the European Journal of Endocrinology signals a maturing interest in moving beyond the GH/IGF-1 binary toward richer biomarker panels — a trend that, if validated in larger cohorts, could reshape how we monitor disease activity and therapeutic response, including to new oral agents like paltusotine. Meanwhile, the immature PIT-1 lineage tumor data remind us that a subset of acromegaly-causing tumors remain genuinely refractory to surgery and call for more aggressive multimodal thinking.

Zooming out to the broader rare disease space, the week's two gene therapy stories — Astellas' XLMTM restart and Encoded's sirolimus finding — together frame the central tension in the field right now: how to make therapies safe enough to use without undermining the efficacy that makes them worth using. That tension is not hypothetical for pituitary rare diseases. As gene and cell-based strategies for GH-secreting tumors move through early preclinical development, the immunosuppression question will arrive at their doorstep too. The Angelini/Catalyst deal, meanwhile, is a reminder that the commercial exit environment for approved rare disease assets remains robust — a tailwind for Acromegaly-adjacent pipeline companies seeking partners or acquirers.
