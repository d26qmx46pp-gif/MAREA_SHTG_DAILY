**Cancer Risk in Acromegaly: Reassessing the Evidence and Impact of Biochemical Control**
Fleseriu M, Melmed S. *The Journal of Clinical Endocrinology & Metabolism.* June 2026.
[PMID 42274358](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42274358/) | [DOI 10.1210/clinem/dgag230](https://doi.org/10.1210/clinem/dgag230)

In this JCEM editorial/commentary, two of the field's most prominent voices — Maria Fleseriu (Oregon Health & Science University) and Shlomo Melmed (Cedars-Sinai) — take a critical look at the evidence linking acromegaly to elevated cancer risk. The piece reassesses what we actually know about which cancers are truly overrepresented in acromegaly, what role chronic IGF-1 excess plays, and — crucially — whether achieving biochemical control reduces that risk over time. This kind of authoritative synthesis is exactly what clinicians need when counseling patients about long-term surveillance. The abstract was not yet indexed in PubMed at time of publication; this summary reflects the title, authorship, and journal context.

**Endoscopic Resection of Knosp Grade 0–2 Growth Hormone-Secreting Pituitary Adenomas with Routine Medial Cavernous Sinus Wall Excision** *(Broadened tier)*
Ladegaard PBJ, Andersen MS, Darling P, Møller MW, Petersen JK, Pedersen CB, et al. *Acta Neurochirurgica.* June 2026.
[PMID 42277469](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42277469/) | [DOI 10.1007/s00701-026-06946-9](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00701-026-06946-9)

Standard endoscopic transsphenoidal surgery for GH-secreting pituitary adenomas achieves biochemical remission in roughly 40–60% of cases — a number that has stubbornly resisted improvement. This single-center study from Denmark tests whether routinely excising the medial cavernous sinus wall, even in low-grade (Knosp 0–2) tumors, improves outcomes. The hypothesis is that microscopic invasion of the medial cavernous sinus wall may harbor residual tumor cells that drive recurrence even when macroscopic resection looks complete. The paper reports clinical and biochemical outcomes for a cohort treated with this modified technique and compares them to historical controls. For neurosurgeons and endocrinologists managing GH-PA patients, this is a potentially practice-shifting technical refinement that deserves attention — if the remission rates hold up, it could push the field toward more aggressive resection strategies even for seemingly straightforward tumors.

**The Relationships Among Nutrition, Body Composition, Muscle Strength and Physical Performance in Patients with Acromegaly** *(Broadened tier)*
Hupalowski NA, Rocha CPS, Andrade VFC, Boguszewski CL, Borba VZC. *Archives of Endocrinology and Metabolism.* August 2026.
[PMID 42155079](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42155079/) | [DOI 10.1016/j.redox.2020.101819](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.redox.2020.101819)

This observational cross-sectional study from Brazil takes a close look at something often overlooked in acromegaly: how dietary intake patterns affect body composition, physical performance, and muscle strength in patients with the disease compared to matched controls. Using DEXA to assess total and appendicular lean mass, combined with food frequency questionnaires and physical performance testing in 82 patients (41 with acromegaly), the investigators found nuanced relationships between nutritional profile and musculoskeletal function in acromegalic patients. Since acromegaly affects both muscle tissue and fat distribution in complex ways — with patients sometimes appearing muscular but actually having impaired functional capacity — understanding the dietary contributors to these differences could inform rehabilitation strategies. This paper won't change medical management directly, but it's a useful dataset for multidisciplinary care teams.

**Hyperthyroidism, Acromegaly, and Hyperprolactinemia in a Patient with a Mature Plurihormonal PIT-1 Lineage Adenoma** *(Broadened tier)*
Ferreira de Carvalho J, Slack D, Shrivastava R, Richardson TE, Yokoda RT, Levine AC. *JCEM Case Reports.* July 2026.
[PMID 42256640](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42256640/) | [DOI 10.1210/jcemcr/luag129](https://doi.org/10.1210/jcemcr/luag129)

A fascinating case report describing a 42-year-old woman who presented with weight loss, palpitations, amenorrhea, and progressive acromegalic features. Biochemical workup revealed central hyperthyroidism (elevated free T4 with inappropriately normal TSH), markedly elevated IGF-1 and GH, and hyperprolactinemia — all from a single, mature plurihormonal pituitary tumor of the PIT-1 lineage. Plurihormonal PIT-1 adenomas that simultaneously secrete biologically active TSH and GH, causing both central hyperthyroidism and acromegaly, are exceedingly rare. The case highlights the diagnostic complexity when acromegaly presents alongside thyroid abnormalities, a combination that can easily mislead clinicians. Immunohistochemistry confirmed co-expression of all three hormones from the same tumor. This is a good reminder to evaluate the full anterior pituitary hormone panel — not just GH/IGF-1 — in patients with pituitary adenomas.

**Amino Acids Partially Override Inhibitory Effects of Octreotide on Islet Hormone Secretion in Healthy Individuals** *(Broadened tier)*
Reiche J, Richter MM, Kjeldsen SAS, Winther-Sørensen M, Wewer Albrechtsen NJ. *The Journal of Physiology.* June 2026.
[PMID 42285749](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42285749/) | [DOI 10.1113/JP290901](https://doi.org/10.1113/JP290901)

A mechanistic human study relevant to the pharmacology of octreotide and other somatostatin analogues. The investigators examined whether intravenous amino acid infusion can overcome the suppressive effects of octreotide on pancreatic islet hormone secretion — specifically insulin and glucagon — in healthy volunteers. This is clinically important because amino-acid-stimulated glucagon release is a key driver of hyperglucagonemia and metabolic dysregulation in conditions including type 2 diabetes and liver disease, and somatostatin analogues like octreotide are used to suppress hormone hypersecretion in acromegaly, neuroendocrine tumors, and other conditions. The finding that amino acids can "partially override" octreotide's inhibitory effects has implications for how we think about metabolic management during somatostatin analogue therapy — particularly regarding nutritional timing, protein intake, and glucagon physiology in patients on long-acting somatostatin analogues like lanreotide or octreotide LAR.

**Incyte to Buy Blood Disorder Drug in Possibly $2B Deal**
*BioPharma Dive* — June 8, 2026
[Source](https://www.biopharmadive.com/news/incyte-vega-acquisition-star-biotech-deal-blood-disorder-vwd/822238/)

Incyte has agreed to acquire Vega Therapeutics — a subsidiary of the hub-and-spoke biotech Star Therapeutics — for $1.25 billion upfront, with up to $750 million in additional sales-based milestones, bringing the total potential deal value to $2 billion. The asset being acquired is VGA039, an experimental once-monthly subcutaneous injection for von Willebrand disease, the most common inherited bleeding disorder, estimated to affect up to 135,000 diagnosed patients in the United States. Current standard of care requires intravenous factor replacement infusions two to three times per week; VGA039's convenience profile, if confirmed in late-stage data, would represent a meaningful quality-of-life advance. The drug is currently in late-stage testing, and the deal is expected to close between July and September 2026. Incyte's existing revenue base depends heavily on Jakafi (ruxolitinib), whose patents begin expiring in the coming years, giving the company a strategic imperative to build new growth franchises — and VGA039 fits that mandate.

*Why it matters for rare disease developers:* This deal signals continued appetite from established mid-cap pharma companies to acquire late-stage rare disease assets as pipeline diversification ahead of major patent cliffs — and demonstrates that rare bleeding disorders remain commercially attractive targets.

**City Therapeutics Banks Nearly $100M for Next-Gen RNAi Drugs, Including Stargardt Disease**
*BioPharma Dive* — June 8, 2026
[Source](https://www.biopharmadive.com/news/city-therapeutics-rnai-series-b/822266/)

City Therapeutics, co-founded by longtime Alnylam CEO John Maraganore and Ohio State University researcher Kotaro Nakanishi, has closed a $99.5 million Series B round led by Viking Global Investors with participation from Sofinnova, Arch Venture Partners, and Fidelity. The company's lead program, CITY-FXI, targets Factor XI for thromboembolic conditions such as pulmonary embolism, stroke, and deep vein thrombosis, and recently entered Phase 1 testing. Its second program, CITY-RBP4, is expected to enter human testing later this year for Stargardt disease, a rare inherited retinal dystrophy caused by toxic accumulation of vitamin A byproducts in the eye. City's platform uses a proprietary class of synthetic RNAs ("cleavage-inducing tiny nucleotide RNAs") designed to be smaller and more potent than conventional siRNAs, and aims to extend RNAi delivery beyond the liver — a longstanding technical challenge in the field. The company plans to advance both named programs into mid-stage testing within the next year.

*Why it matters for rare disease developers:* City's raises signal that investors are funding next-generation RNA platforms that can access tissue types beyond the liver, which has historically limited RNAi's reach to hepatic diseases — opening new ground for rare genetic conditions affecting the eye, muscle, and CNS.

**Alnylam, Inceptive Ink AI Deal Potentially Worth $2B to Accelerate RNA Drug Discovery**
*BioPharma Dive* — June 4, 2026
[Source](https://www.biopharmadive.com/news/alnylam-inceptive-ai-drug-discovery-rna-deal-artificial-intelligence/822008/)

Alnylam Pharmaceuticals — the pioneer in RNAi therapies for rare diseases including TTR-mediated amyloidosis, acute hepatic porphyria, and primary hyperoxaluria type 1 — has struck a deal with Inceptive Nucleics, an AI company whose models are designed to learn the biological rules governing RNA structure and function. The deal starts with $30 million upfront (cash plus equity) and carries milestone-based payments that could bring the total value to $2 billion. Alnylam's goal is to use Inceptive's tools to prioritize the most promising small interfering RNA molecules, reduce the trial-and-error that currently governs preclinical drug design, and support its "Alnylam 2030" pipeline expansion strategy. The companies say joint exploratory work showed "exceptional performance within weeks" in characterizing siRNA molecules. The announcement continues a pharma-wide trend of large AI collaborations, with BMS, Merck, Novo Nordisk, Takeda, and Lilly having all announced major AI partnerships in the past several months.

*Why it matters for rare disease developers:* For a company whose entire franchise is built on rare disease RNAi, bringing AI into the molecule-design process could meaningfully expand the pipeline at lower per-program cost — potentially enabling Alnylam to pursue rarer or more complex targets that would otherwise not be economically viable.

## What to Watch

In acromegaly, the Fleseriu-Melmed JCEM editorial and the Danish cavernous sinus wall excision study both push toward the same underlying question: are we doing enough, early enough, to achieve true cure? Better surgical technique may improve remission rates for low-grade tumors; better understanding of cancer risk under biochemical control will inform how aggressively we pursue and sustain IGF-1 normalization over decades. On the industry side, the von Willebrand deal and the City and Alnylam financings all cluster around the same technology family — gene-silencing via RNA interference — that underpins several acromegaly-adjacent modalities now in development for pituitary diseases. As the FDA continues to clarify its pathway for ultra-rare diseases (including the new bespoke gene therapy guidance unveiled in February), watch for companies developing highly targeted RNA-based treatments for pituitary conditions to begin filing for accelerated approval designations. The convergence of surgical precision, better biochemical targets, and powerful RNA platforms puts the field in an interesting position heading into the second half of 2026.
