## Part A — Acromegaly research

**Upper limb arthropathy in acromegaly: Radiological findings and functional impairment**
Coan MN, Aguiar DPC, de Aguiar Dos Santos G, Simoneti LEL, et al. *Pituitary*, Nov 2026.
PMID: [43026417](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/43026417/) · DOI: [10.1007/s11102-026-01784-5](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11102-026-01784-5)
In this prospective study of 48 patients with uncontrolled, medically controlled, or cured acromegaly, 85% reported at least one joint complaint and 75% had measurable upper-limb functional limitation on the QuickDASH questionnaire (moderate or severe in 24 patients). Of the 27 patients who had X-rays, 78% showed structural damage, most often in the hands (70%) and shoulders (59%), including 13 cases of glenohumeral osteoarthritis. Notably, joint problems were present regardless of current biochemical control. The takeaway is that arthropathy persists after GH/IGF-1 control and deserves routine screening and rehabilitation planning, not just hormonal management.

**Evaluation of voice features using Wav2vec2.0-based deep learning as a diagnostic tool in acromegaly**
Aksoy S, Saygili ES, Akyel S, Onur O, et al. *Pituitary*, Nov 2026.
PMID: [43026384](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/43026384/) · DOI: [10.1007/s11102-026-01768-5](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11102-026-01768-5)
Researchers recorded voice samples on an ordinary mobile phone from 70 acromegaly patients and 68 matched controls, then used a speech-AI model (Wav2Vec2.0) to extract voice features for machine-learning classifiers. The best model (logistic regression) reached an AUC of 0.84 on a held-out test set, with high specificity (0.92) but more modest sensitivity (0.71). The authors frame voice as a possible non-invasive digital biomarker to shorten acromegaly's long diagnostic delay. This is a small, single-center study with internal validation only, so it is hypothesis-generating pending external cohorts.

**Prognostic value of immediate and short-term postoperative biochemical markers for long-term remission in acromegaly: A single-center study** *(next-best tier — Growth Horm IGF Res)*
Chen TH, Shih SR, Wang HC, Yang SH, et al. *Growth Hormone & IGF Research*, 2026.
PMID: [42335807](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42335807/) · DOI: [10.1016/j.ghir.2026.101715](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ghir.2026.101715)
This retrospective cohort of patients who had transsphenoidal surgery from 2000 to 2022 applied the latest consensus IGF-1–based outcome definitions. Higher Knosp grade, larger tumors, and higher baseline IGF-1 predicted poor short-term outcomes; higher baseline IGF-1 and elevated postoperative GH and IGF-1 predicted long-term recurrence. Patients with persistent biochemical disease early after surgery but no visible residual tumor had about a six-fold higher risk of long-term persistence than those in early remission. The study supports an IGF-1–centric approach to post-surgical risk stratification.

**Association of GH and IGF-1 Levels with Increased Carotid Intima-Media Thickness and Cardiovascular Risk Scores in Acromegaly: A Cross-Sectional Study** *(next-best tier — Int J Gen Med)*
Kartal Baykan E, Akhanlı P, Usta Çiftel S, Pir A, et al. *International Journal of General Medicine*, 2026.
PMID: [43034633](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/43034633/) · DOI: [10.2147/IJGM.S648753](https://doi.org/10.2147/IJGM.S648753)
In 104 acromegaly patients versus 50 matched controls, patients had worse glucose and lipid profiles, thicker carotid artery walls (0.79 vs 0.57 mm), and higher 10-year cardiovascular risk scores. Carotid thickness tracked closely with GH and IGF-1 levels and was greater in active versus controlled disease, while traditional risk factors were not independently associated. It is cross-sectional, so it shows association rather than causation, but adds to the case that hormonal control matters for vascular risk.

## Part B — Rare Disease Industry News

**Lilly adds to buyout streak with $1.5B purchase of startup Calytrix**
BioPharma Dive (Ben Fidler) — Nov 16, 2026
https://www.biopharmadive.com/news/lilly-calytrix-bio-acquire-deal-myelofibrosis-essential-thrombocythemiaenial-/874719/
Eli Lilly will pay up to $1.5 billion, split between an undisclosed upfront and milestones, for Calytrix Bio, a Massachusetts startup developing treatments for myelofibrosis and essential thrombocythemia. Calytrix's lead approach is a biparatopic antibody aimed at the cell-surface protein associated with CALR mutations, positioning it as a disease-modifying alternative to JAK inhibitors like Incyte's Jakafi. BioPharma Dive counts this as Lilly's 15th acquisition this year, with more than $30 billion spent, mostly on private startups. Calytrix was formed only last year by J.P. Morgan Asset Management's life sciences venture arm.
*Why it matters for rare disease developers:* Big pharma is paying early for mutation-targeted approaches in rare blood cancers, even for very young private companies.

**Argenx to buy peptide platform in potentially billion-dollar deal**
BioPharma Dive (Jacob Bell) — Nov 9, 2026
https://www.biopharmadive.com/news/argenx-unnatural-products-acquisition-deal-peptides-protein-macrocyclic-immune/874119/
Argenx, the maker of the rare autoimmune disease drug Vyvgart, agreed to acquire Unnatural Products for $550 million upfront plus up to $595 million in milestones. Unnatural Products uses machine learning and screening to design macrocyclic peptides meant to be oral, durable medicines for immune-mediated diseases. Argenx already held a minority stake; the deal is expected to close by year-end and follows its potentially $2.2 billion Forte Biosciences purchase. Vyvgart generated $4.5 billion in net sales in the first nine months of the year.
*Why it matters for rare disease developers:* A leading rare-autoimmune company is using its Vyvgart cash to move from injectable antibodies toward oral drugs, which could change competitive expectations in the space.
See also: Fierce Biotech, "Argenx inks $1.15B Unnatural Products buyout to expand in oral peptides."

**Vir hepatitis D drug succeeds in first pivotal study**
BioPharma Dive (Delilah Alvarado) — Nov 9, 2026
https://www.biopharmadive.com/news/vir-chronic-hepatitis-d-drug-results-elebsiran-tobevibart/874089/
Vir Biotechnology's combination of elebsiran and tobevibart met the primary endpoint of ECLIPSE 1, the first of three Phase 3 trials in chronic hepatitis delta. After 48 weeks, 44% of treated patients had undetectable virus plus a normal liver enzyme level, versus none in the delayed-treatment group; 79% achieved a response on viral levels alone. Two more Phase 3 readouts are expected early next year, supporting a possible filing in 2027. Gilead's Hepcludex was approved earlier this year as the first U.S. treatment, and Mirum's brelovitug also succeeded in Phase 3 in September.
*Why it matters for rare disease developers:* A rare viral liver disease with no U.S. options a year ago is quickly turning into a three-way race.
See also: STAT, The Readout (Nov 9).

**Novo submits sickle cell drug** (roundup item)
BioPharma Dive — Nov 13, 2026
https://biopharmadive.com/news/novo-etavopivat-flagship-gates-ascidian-immuneering-neurosterix/874120
Novo Nordisk filed with the FDA and EMA for etavopivat, a once-daily oral sickle cell disease treatment it gained through its 2022 Forma Therapeutics buyout. In the Phase 3 HIBISCUS trial, etavopivat reduced vaso-occlusive crisis events by 27% over a year. Novo calls it a future blockbuster, and it is also in mid-stage testing for thalassemia.
*Why it matters for rare disease developers:* A new oral pyruvate kinase activator heads to regulators in a sickle cell market that has seen withdrawals and slow gene therapy uptake.

## What to watch

The FDA's decision on Beren Therapeutics' adrabetadex for Niemann-Pick disease type C is due Nov 17, after a three-month extension (context: Fierce Biotech, publication date not confirmed). The filing rests on an external-control-arm survival comparison, so the outcome will be a closely watched signal of how the agency treats non-randomized evidence in ultra-rare diseases, the same question rare endocrine programs face. Meanwhile, this week's acromegaly papers point to burdens that persist after biochemical control: joint damage and vascular risk. That is a reminder that next-generation therapies, including the oral options now coming to market, will increasingly be judged on long-term outcomes beyond IGF-1 normalization.
