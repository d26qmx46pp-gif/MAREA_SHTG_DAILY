## Part A — Acromegaly research

Note: the top-tier-journal PubMed query (NEJM, Lancet, JAMA, JCEM, Pituitary, etc.) returned zero hits in the last 8 days, so this section draws from the broader acromegaly/somatostatin-analogue literature search, next-best tier as instructed.

**Differential Somatostatin Sensitivity of Corticotropic and Somatotropic Responses Following Amino Acid Infusion.** Sturlaugsson Jóhönnuson EM, Reiche J, Richter MM. *Endocrine Connections*, Sept 4, 2026. [PMID: 42696271](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42696271/) | [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1530/EC-26-0537). In a randomized crossover study of 15 healthy adults, researchers infused amino acids alongside octreotide (the somatostatin analogue used to treat acromegaly) to see how growth hormone, ACTH, and cortisol responses interact. Growth hormone secretion was almost completely shut down by octreotide, while the ACTH/cortisol response only partially blunted — showing the GH axis is far more somatostatin-sensitive than the stress-hormone axis. That gap helps explain why somatostatin analogues control GH so effectively in acromegaly while leaving adrenal function largely intact, and it's useful baseline physiology for anyone refining dosing or combination strategies.

**Impact of dosage form on the pharmacokinetics of octreotide after oral administration with permeation enhancers.** Fredholt F, Raptis K, Rønholt S. *International Journal of Pharmaceutics*, Sept 5, 2026. [PMID: 42503373](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42503373/) | [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ijpharm.2026.127237). A preclinical (rat) formulation study compared octreotide mini-tablets against oral solutions, each paired with one of two permeation enhancers (SNAC or sodium decanoate), to see which combination best gets the peptide across the gut wall. SNAC-containing mini-tablets boosted relative bioavailability up to roughly 5-fold over controls, meaningfully outperforming the decanoate formulations. This is early-stage drug-delivery science, but it's directly relevant to the field's push toward oral somatostatin-analogue and oral peptide options for acromegaly, an area where paltusotine and oral octreotide programs are already competing.

**Radiomics and artificial intelligence in pituitary adenomas (PitNETs): from imaging biomarkers to clinical translation.** Ruiz S, Oliva A, Mateu R. *Reviews in Endocrine & Metabolic Disorders*, Sept 2, 2026. [PMID: 42684588](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42684588/). This narrative review surveys six years of MRI-based radiomics and machine-learning studies on pituitary tumors, including GH-secreting adenomas. AI models built on texture and shape features from MRI can predict tumor consistency, somatotroph granulation pattern, invasiveness, and likelihood of surgical cure with reported AUCs often above 0.80 — potentially helping surgeons plan before ever entering the operating room. The authors caution that most of this evidence is still single-center and retrospective, so multicenter validation is the next needed step before this reaches routine acromegaly care.

**When growth hormone levels drop — management with urea of postoperative inappropriate antidiuretic hormone secretion syndrome in a patient who underwent surgery for acromegaly.** Guillén C, Roldán Sánchez AM, Torregrosa-Quesada ME. *Nutrición Hospitalaria*, Sept 1, 2026. [PMID: 42704007](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42704007/) | [DOI](https://doi.org/10.20960/nh.07101). A case report of a 74-year-old woman who developed severe low sodium (SIADH) 11 days after transsphenoidal surgery for acromegaly — a complication the authors note occurs in over 40% of acromegaly patients postoperatively, notably higher than in other pituitary surgery patients. Oral urea corrected her sodium safely within 48 hours, avoiding more aggressive interventions. It's a single case, but a useful practical reminder that acromegaly patients need closer postoperative sodium monitoring than the general pituitary-surgery population.

## Part B — Rare Disease Industry News

**Ultragenyx's Angelman syndrome drug fails Phase 3.** Fierce Biotech / STAT News, Sept 2–3, 2026. [Fierce Biotech](https://www.fiercebiotech.com/biotech/ultragenyx-fails-phase-3-angelman-test-plots-significant-expenses-reduction) | [STAT News](https://www.statnews.com/2026/09/02/ultragenyx-drug-angelman-syndrome-clinical-trial/). Ultragenyx's apazunersen missed both its primary endpoint (Bayley-4 cognitive score) and key secondary endpoint in the Aspire trial for Angelman syndrome, a rare genetic condition causing severe intellectual disability. Shares fell 43% after hours; the company says it's evaluating the program's future and planning "significant expense reductions," on top of a 10% workforce cut earlier this year tied to separate failed trials. Why it matters for rare disease developers: this is a stark reminder that even drugs with strong early-phase signals can fail at registration scale in ultra-rare, heterogeneous populations — and it puts pressure on the handful of remaining Angelman programs, including Ionis's ongoing Phase 3 Reveal trial, to prove the mechanism can work.

**Ionis wins first-ever FDA approval for Alexander disease.** Fierce Pharma / STAT News, Sept 3–4, 2026. [Fierce Pharma](https://www.fiercepharma.com/pharma/ionis-breakthrough-alexander-disease-approval-establishes-pillar-rare-disease-pipeline) | [STAT News](https://www.statnews.com/2026/09/03/ionis-fda-approval-zanvastro-alexander-disease/). The FDA approved Ionis Pharmaceuticals' Zanvastro (zilganersen), the first-ever disease-modifying therapy for Alexander disease, an ultra-rare and typically fatal neurological condition caused by toxic protein buildup from the GFAP gene. The pivotal trial showed walking speed stayed stable in treated patients versus a 33% decline in the untreated comparison group, using a natural-history control arm given the disease's extreme rarity. Why it matters for rare disease developers: this is both a human milestone — patients previously had no options — and a case study in FDA flexibility, since Ionis built its approval case around a natural-history control rather than a traditional placebo arm. Ionis says this is the first of several rare pediatric neuro programs (Angelman, Pelizaeus-Merzbacher, MECP2 duplication, Dravet) it's now positioned to push through on its own.

## What to watch

The acromegaly literature this week stayed close to mechanism and tooling — somatostatin's differential grip on the GH versus stress axis, better oral-delivery chemistry for octreotide, and AI-assisted imaging for pituitary surgery planning — rather than new clinical trial readouts. That's consistent with the broader rare-neurology story playing out in industry news: Ionis's Alexander disease win shows regulators are willing to flex trial design for ultra-rare conditions, while Ultragenyx's Angelman setback shows how hard it still is to translate strong early biology into registration-grade efficacy. Watch whether Ionis's own Angelman asset, obudanersen, can succeed where apazunersen didn't — and whether that FDA flexibility on natural-history controls starts showing up in future acromegaly trial designs for very rare GH-axis subtypes.
