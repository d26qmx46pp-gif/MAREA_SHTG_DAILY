## Part A — Acromegaly Research

**Safety and Efficacy of Once-Daily Oral Paltusotine in Acromegaly: ACROBAT Advance Open-Label Extension Up to 4 Years**
Gadelha MR, Gordon MB, Doknic M, Mezősi E, Tóth M, et al.
*The Journal of Clinical Endocrinology & Metabolism* | Published June 26, 2026
[PubMed 42359578](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42359578/) | DOI: [10.1210/clinem/dgag254](https://doi.org/10.1210/clinem/dgag254)

Paltusotine is the first non-peptide, once-daily oral somatostatin receptor type 2 agonist approved for acromegaly — a meaningful departure from the injectable somatostatin receptor ligands (SRLs) that have long defined standard care. The ACROBAT Advance open-label extension (OLE) enrolled patients who completed the earlier ACROBAT Edge phase 2 study, in which individuals were switched from injected SRL-based regimens to oral paltusotine. This interim report covers up to four years of continuous treatment. Across that period, biochemical control of IGF-1 and growth hormone was maintained in a substantial proportion of patients, and the safety profile remained consistent with prior shorter-term data — no new signals emerged. Tolerability was generally good, with gastrointestinal events the most common adverse effects. These findings are clinically important: they suggest that patients achieving biochemical control on oral paltusotine can sustain that response over multiple years without returning to injections, supporting a meaningful quality-of-life benefit for a condition that otherwise requires lifelong, often burdensome parenteral therapy.

## Part B — Rare Disease Industry News

**Sangamo Therapeutics Files for Bankruptcy; Lilly and Astellas Emerge as Lead Bidders for Key Assets**
*BioPharma Dive* | June 24, 2026
[https://www.biopharmadive.com/news/sangamo-bankruptcy-serapha-china-saturnus-cenrifki-carsgen/823588/](https://www.biopharmadive.com/news/sangamo-bankruptcy-serapha-china-saturnus-cenrifki-carsgen/823588/)

Sangamo Therapeutics — a pioneer of zinc finger gene editing that has spent decades in the rare disease space without bringing a product to market — filed for bankruptcy and announced it will sell its assets in a court-supervised auction. Eli Lilly has agreed to serve as the lead bidder for Sangamo's gene editing intellectual property and an experimental prion disease treatment, while Astellas Pharma is positioned to acquire ST-920, a gene therapy for Fabry disease that was already nearing regulatory submission. Other assets going to auction include cell therapies and treatments for hemophilia A and chronic pain. The bankruptcy closes a difficult chapter for a company that had once been among the most prominent names in rare disease gene therapy — it never commercialized a product despite numerous pivots, partnership failures, and restructurings.

*Why it matters for rare disease developers:* Sangamo's collapse is a cautionary tale about the cost and risk of platform-first strategies without near-term commercial anchors — but Astellas inheriting the Fabry disease program could keep that therapy on a path to patients, and Lilly gaining zinc finger tools may accelerate their broader genetic medicine ambitions.

**Serapha Bio Launches with $230 Million to Develop One-Time Gene Editing Treatment for AATD**
*STAT News* | June 23, 2026
[https://www.statnews.com/2026/06/23/gene-editing-startup-serapha-bio-aatd/](https://www.statnews.com/2026/06/23/gene-editing-startup-serapha-bio-aatd/)

Serapha Bio emerged this week as a fully-formed rare disease gene editing company, having secured $230 million in financing — $138 million in Series A led by RA Capital Management and RTW Investments, plus $92 million tied to a reverse merger with Boundless Bio, a cancer-focused biotech that ran into trouble with its lead program. Serapha is developing a one-time gene editing treatment for Alpha-1 Antitrypsin Deficiency (AATD), a rare inherited disease that can cause both liver damage and progressive lung disease. The underlying therapy was licensed from Shanghai-based Yoltech Therapeutics and is currently in an investigator-initiated trial in China, putting Serapha in direct competition with Beam Therapeutics, which has a base editing AATD program in more advanced clinical development. The China-linked technology and unconventional capital structure (reverse merger + Series A) illustrate the creative deal structures rare disease biotechs are using to get to scale quickly.

*Why it matters for rare disease developers:* AATD remains an area of unmet need with no curative therapy, and a well-funded competitor entering the space signals investor conviction in gene editing as a path to one-time treatment — a model with potential relevance across many rare monogenic disorders.

**Merck KGaA and Versant Ventures Launch Saturnus Bio with $50M Build-to-Buy Deal for Rare Cardiomyopathies**
*BioPharma Dive* | June 24, 2026
[https://www.biopharmadive.com/news/sangamo-bankruptcy-serapha-china-saturnus-cenrifki-carsgen/823588/](https://www.biopharmadive.com/news/sangamo-bankruptcy-serapha-china-saturnus-cenrifki-carsgen/823588/)

Merck KGaA and Versant Ventures have co-launched Saturnus Bio, a new startup focused on targeted treatments for rare mono-genetic cardiomyopathies. Merck KGaA is providing $50 million in initial financing and holds an exclusive pre-negotiated option to acquire Saturnus outright if the company's research programs progress successfully. The so-called "build-to-buy" structure — in which a large pharma funds an independent startup in exchange for a future acquisition right at a set price — has become an increasingly common way for large companies to explore high-risk rare disease science without absorbing it prematurely onto their balance sheets. The specific genetic cardiomyopathy targets Saturnus will pursue have not been disclosed, but rare hereditary heart diseases such as hypertrophic cardiomyopathy and arrhythmogenic cardiomyopathy have seen growing drug development interest.

*Why it matters for rare disease developers:* Build-to-buy deals are becoming a preferred structure for rare disease discovery-stage programs, providing early capital and a defined exit path while keeping drug development risk off large pharma books — a model worth watching as other companies look to externalize early rare disease R&D.

## What to Watch

Paltusotine's four-year OLE data set a new durability benchmark for oral acromegaly therapy — the next question is whether this class of agents can also outperform or replace injectable pasireotide for patients with inadequate IGF-1 control, and whether paltusotine's label eventually expands to first-line use. In the broader rare disease ecosystem, Sangamo's bankruptcy and the simultaneous launch of Serapha Bio in the same week illustrate a recurring pattern: the rare disease gene editing field is winnowing out companies without commercial anchors while new entrants with better-capitalized starts and China-linked technology are entering the fray. Regulators and investors alike are watching whether the assets Astellas inherits from Sangamo — particularly the Fabry disease gene therapy — can finally reach patients, and whether build-to-buy structures like Saturnus Bio's can bridge the gap between promising rare disease science and viable commercial programs.
