## Part A — Acromegaly research

**Pasireotide-LAR and pegvisomant combination in resistant acromegaly: a case series of predominantly mammosomatotroph adenomas**
Edward Mignone, Elena V Varlamov, Rinki Pandya, Melanie Hakar, Maria Fleseriu — *Pituitary* (Sept 23, 2026)
[PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42778805/) · PMID: 42778805 · [DOI: 10.1007/s11102-026-01750-1](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11102-026-01750-1)
This single-center retrospective case series followed six patients (ages 15–44) with treatment-resistant somatotroph or mammosomatotroph macroadenomas who received combined pasireotide-LAR and pegvisomant for a median of 3.3 years after surgery and other medical therapy failed to control disease. All six achieved normal IGF-1 and stable tumor size; one had biochemical escape after nine months and needed further surgery and radiosurgery, one stopped pegvisomant for injection-site pain, and three developed pre-diabetic hyperglycemia. No hepatotoxicity or arrhythmias were seen. The authors conclude the combination can offer durable control and allow pegvisomant dose reduction, but stress that glucose needs close monitoring since pegvisomant does not blunt pasireotide's glycemic effects — and that larger prospective studies are needed to identify who responds best.

**Dietary Interventions and Vitamin D3 Replacement in Pituitary Tumors: a Systematic Review**
L C Te Nijenhuis-Noort, R van de Groef, J Refardt, I C M Pelsma, N R Biermasz, K A Berk, et al. — *European Journal of Endocrinology* (Sept 20, 2026)
[PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42764013/) · PMID: 42764013 · [DOI: 10.1093/ejendo/lvag182](https://doi.org/10.1093/ejendo/lvag182)
This systematic review screened over 2,200 records down to eight studies (355 participants) testing dietary interventions in pituitary neuroendocrine tumors, including acromegaly, Cushing's disease, prolactinoma, and hypopituitarism. In acromegaly specifically, an isocaloric very-low-carbohydrate ketogenic diet lowered IGF-1 levels, and vitamin D3 replacement reduced vertebral fracture risk. The evidence base is thin (only five interventional and three observational studies total), but the signal that diet and vitamin D status may be modifiable levers alongside standard biochemical control is a useful one for clinics counseling patients on lifestyle. The authors call for larger randomized trials before these become practice-changing.

**Pituitary Society Delphi consensus on the role of radiotherapy for pituitary adenomas**
Frederic Castinetti, Cecilia Piazzola, John Ayuk, Michael Kosmin, Marco Losa, Hani J Marcus, et al. — *Pituitary* (Sept 23, 2026)
[PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42776342/) · PMID: 42776342 · [DOI: 10.1007/s11102-011-0310-7](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11102-011-0310-7)
A 29-expert international Delphi panel convened by the Pituitary Society reached consensus on 24 of 27 statements about when and how to use radiotherapy — including stereotactic radiosurgery — as second- or third-line treatment for functioning adenomas like those causing acromegaly, as well as nonfunctioning tumors. Panelists agreed indication and timing should go through a multidisciplinary tumor board, that hormonal control should continue while awaiting radiotherapy's delayed effect, and that more than 10 years of follow-up is needed to properly judge recurrence and side effects. It's a practical, if not novel, guidance document rather than new data, but it's the kind of consensus statement that shapes how community endocrinologists sequence therapy.

**Pituitary adenomas in older patients — approaches, challenges and future directions**
Sandrine A Urwyler, Daniela Esposito, Athanasios Fountas, Georgia Ntali, Elena Valassi, Niki Karavitaki — *European Journal of Endocrinology* (Sept 23, 2026)
[PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42773730/) · PMID: 42773730 · [DOI: 10.1093/ejendo/lvag183](https://doi.org/10.1093/ejendo/lvag183)
This narrative review of 20 years of literature covers how pituitary adenomas, including GH-secreting tumors, present and are managed differently in elderly patients — where age-related changes can mask or mimic acromegaly's features and complicate diagnosis. It's a general review rather than acromegaly-specific data, but flags real diagnostic and treatment-tailoring challenges (comorbidity burden, frailty, surgical risk) that are relevant as the diagnosed population ages. Included here as the most acromegaly-adjacent of the general pituitary literature this week, given a thin week for on-topic trial data.

## Part B — Rare Disease Industry News

**Beacon Therapeutics' gene therapy hits the primary endpoint in first-ever positive pivotal XLRP trial**
BioPharma Dive, September 21, 2026 — [biopharmadive.com](https://www.biopharmadive.com/news/beacon-xlrp-laru-zova-phase-3-study-results/830918/)
Beacon Therapeutics' pivotal VISTA trial of laruparetigene zovaparvovec (laru-zova), a one-time gene therapy for X-linked retinitis pigmentosa (XLRP), met its primary endpoint: among 85 male participants aged 12–48, 24% on the low dose and 31% on the high dose gained at least 15 letters of low-light visual acuity at one year, versus 0% of controls. Adverse events were mostly mild-to-moderate, with two serious events tied to the surgical delivery procedure. Beacon says this is the first time any XLRP gene therapy has hit its main goal in a pivotal study and plans rolling regulatory submissions by year-end.
*Why it matters for rare disease developers:* XLRP has been a graveyard for prior gene therapy attempts; a clean pivotal win here is a proof point for ocular gene therapy delivery more broadly and could re-open investor and partner interest in inherited retinal disease programs.

**Mirum wins FDA approval for Atebrioz in FOP, setting up a head-to-head with Regeneron**
Fierce Pharma, September 25, 2026 — [fiercepharma.com](https://www.fiercepharma.com/pharma/mirum-bags-fda-approval-ultra-rare-bone-lesion-disorder-heating-rivalry-regeneronns-pasatru)
The FDA approved Mirum Pharmaceuticals' Atebrioz (zilurgisertib), an oral once-daily ALK2-pathway inhibitor, for fibrodysplasia ossificans progressiva (FOP) in patients 12 and older. The approval rests on a 63-patient Phase 2 trial (Progress) showing a 99% reduction in new bone formation over 24 weeks versus placebo (1 of the drug-treated patients developed new bone growth vs. 5 on placebo). Atebrioz becomes the second approved FOP therapy after Regeneron's Pasatru (garetosmab) in August 2026 — both hit the same pathway but differ in mechanism, with Mirum betting its oral pill and broader 12+ label can offset Regeneron's first-mover edge and Phase 3 data package.
*Why it matters for rare disease developers:* a second approval in an ultra-rare indication within a month shows the FDA is willing to greenlight competing mechanisms quickly once a pathway is validated — good news for pipelines racing a first mover rather than assuming they need to be first.

**Roche and Ionis' sefaxersen hits phase 3 endpoint in IgA nephropathy**
Fierce Biotech / BioPharma Dive, September 23, 2026 — [biopharmadive.com](https://www.biopharmadive.com/news/roche-ionis-rna-study-iga-nephropathy/831152/)
Roche and Ionis reported that sefaxersen, a monthly self-injected antisense oligonucleotide, hit its primary endpoint in a Phase 3 study of IgA nephropathy, significantly improving proteinuria versus control. The companies plan to present full data at a medical meeting and pursue regulatory filing. IgA nephropathy sits at the edge of "rare disease" definitions but is served by a small set of orphan-designated drugs priced in the $400,000–500,000/year range, and Ionis in particular has built its franchise on rare and ultra-rare genetic disease programs.
*Why it matters for rare disease developers:* a positive readout gives Ionis — coming off a rougher stretch with other pipeline setbacks — renewed momentum, and shows RNA-targeted approaches continuing to expand beyond ultra-rare monogenic disease into higher-prevalence orphan-adjacent nephrology.

## What to watch

The acromegaly literature this week leans practical rather than novel — real-world combination-therapy data, dietary/vitamin D adjuncts, and a radiotherapy consensus statement — while the bigger acromegaly story of the season remains a business one: Vertex's approved Palsonify and Marea's earlier-stage MAR002 are both racing toward more data as Vertex, Pfizer, and Novartis compete for share of a market this week's broader rare disease news suggests is only getting more crowded and more willing to greenlight fast-follower mechanisms (see Mirum vs. Regeneron in FOP). If that FDA posture — approving a second-in-class drug in an ultra-rare indication within a month of the first — holds, it's a good sign for any earlier-stage rare disease program worried about not being first to market.
