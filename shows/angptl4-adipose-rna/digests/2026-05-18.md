# ANGPTL4 & Adipose RNA — Monday May 18

**Subject:** ANGPTL4 & Adipose RNA — Monday May 18

**Bottom line:** This week's literature shows ANGPTL4 emerging as a multi-context tumor suppressor in head and neck cancer and a ferroptosis regulator in lung injury, while Mendelian randomization studies strengthen its causal standing in metabolic-cardiovascular disease; LNP delivery science advances on bone-marrow and lung-tumor fronts but adipose-selective RNA targeting remains an active gap.

**Inhibitory role of angiopoietin-like 4 for cancer progression in oropharyngeal squamous cell carcinoma**
*Mikoshiba T, Sekimizu M, Nakahara N, Saito S, Yoshihama K, et al.*
*Oncology Reports* | 2026 Jun | [PMID 42028751](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42028751/) | [DOI 10.1016/j.canlet.2014.08.035](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.canlet.2014.08.035)

ANGPTL4 plays notoriously context-dependent roles across cancer types — sometimes promoting metastasis, sometimes suppressing it. In this study of oropharyngeal squamous cell carcinoma (OPSCC), a form of head and neck cancer associated with HPV infection, the authors find that high ANGPTL4 expression correlates with improved patient survival and reduced tumor invasion. Mechanistically, ANGPTL4 appears to restrict epithelial-to-mesenchymal transition in this tissue, acting as a barrier to disease progression rather than a driver. The findings add OPSCC to the growing list of tumor types where ANGPTL4 functions as a suppressor, and raise the question of whether restoring or mimicking ANGPTL4 signaling could have therapeutic value in HPV-associated head and neck cancers.

**Chrysin reshapes ferroptosis sensitivity to reverse nickel refining fumes-induced lung injury via the miR-210/ANGPTL4 signaling axis mediated by the PHD1/HIF-1α pathway**
*Yao W, Wu R, Sun Q, Yv H, Liu W, et al.*
*Ecotoxicology and Environmental Safety* | 2026 May 13 | [PMID 42127541](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42127541/) | [DOI 10.1016/j.ecoenv.2026.120239](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ecoenv.2026.120239)

This study places ANGPTL4 at the intersection of ferroptosis regulation and hypoxia-inducible factor signaling in occupational lung injury caused by nickel fumes. The researchers show that nickel exposure activates PHD1/HIF-1α, which in turn suppresses miR-210; loss of miR-210 allows ANGPTL4 expression to rise, sensitizing lung cells to ferroptotic cell death. Chrysin, a plant flavonoid, reverses this cascade — partly by restoring miR-210 levels and thereby restraining ANGPTL4. The work is notable because it identifies a non-metabolic, hypoxia-driven regulatory arm of ANGPTL4 and frames it as a druggable target in toxicant-induced tissue injury. For the ANGPTL4 field broadly, it reinforces that miRNA regulation of ANGPTL4 is a recurring theme with tissue-specific consequences.

**Drug targets for lipid modification and risk of type 2 diabetes: a cis-Mendelian randomization study**
*Chen Z, Triatin RD, Luo L, Snieder H, Schmidt AF, et al.*
*Cardiovascular Diabetology* | 2026 May 14 | [PMID 42135799](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42135799/) | [DOI 10.1186/s12933-026-03209-w](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12933-026-03209-w)

A comprehensive cis-Mendelian randomization study evaluates the T2D liability of all major lipid-modifying drug targets using genetic proxies for their plasma effects. ANGPTL4 inhibition — modeled by LDL-lowering genetic variants at the ANGPTL4 locus — emerges with a favorable T2D risk profile compared to some other lipid targets. This is clinically meaningful: PCSK9 inhibitors reduce LDL but increase T2D risk signals, while the ANGPTL4 genetic evidence suggests lipid lowering via this pathway may carry less diabetogenic liability. The study is relevant to companies developing ANGPTL4-targeting therapeutics (including monoclonal antibodies and RNA-based silencing strategies) who are navigating the cardiovascular-metabolic safety landscape.

**Shared proteomic landscape between arteriosclerosis and cardiovascular endpoints: a Mendelian randomization and observational study integrating AlphaFold3 for structural prediction**
*Huang J, Meena D, Achtari M, Smith A, Judy R, et al.*
*Cardiovascular Research* | 2026 May 14 | [PMID 42133815](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42133815/) | [DOI 10.1093/cvr/cvag095](https://doi.org/10.1093/cvr/cvag095)

Integrating large-scale plasma proteomics with Bayesian colocalization and MR, this study maps proteins causally associated with both arteriosclerosis and cardiovascular endpoints. ANGPTL4 is among the proteins identified in the shared proteomic landscape, appearing as a causal contributor to vascular disease risk. The study also applies AlphaFold3-based structural modeling to prioritize druggable interfaces, potentially providing new leads for ANGPTL4-directed intervention. For the field, this reinforces that ANGPTL4 sits at the convergence of lipid metabolism and vascular pathology and is a high-confidence therapeutic target by multiple lines of genomic and proteomic evidence.

**Baiting-enhanced extravasation of lipid nanoparticles for targeted co-delivery of decitabine and siTNF-α to the bone marrow niche in leukemia therapy**
*Zhu Y, Li Q, Zhou Y, Wu D, Qin H, et al.*
*Journal of Controlled Release* | 2026 Jun 10 | [PMID 41905408](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41905408/) | [DOI 10.1016/j.jconrel.2026.114874](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jconrel.2026.114874)

Although the target tissue here is bone marrow rather than adipose, this paper advances the engineering principles directly applicable to tissue-selective RNA delivery. The "bait and extravasation" strategy uses decoy nanoparticles to saturate endothelial uptake pathways and thereby enable a therapeutic LNP carrying TNF-α siRNA to breach the bone marrow endothelial barrier at higher efficiency. The siRNA component (siTNF-α) combined with a hypoxia/ROS-responsive decitabine prodrug achieves synergistic anti-leukemia activity in vivo. The broader relevance: programmable extravasation strategies like this could be adapted for adipose endothelium, which has distinct fenestration and lipid-transport machinery, suggesting a design template worth tracking.

**Endogenous targeting lipid nanoparticles for systemic mRNA delivery to lung cancer tumors**
*Goo YT, Grigoriev V, Korzun T, Salem A, Sharma KS, et al.*
*Journal of Controlled Release* | 2026 Jun 10 | [PMID 41903756](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41903756/) | [DOI 10.18632/oncotarget.7796](https://doi.org/10.18632/oncotarget.7796)

This study introduces a cationic-ionizable LNP formulation (DC_113) that achieves preferential mRNA translation in lung cancer tumors over healthy lung tissue after systemic delivery — exploiting differences in the tumor microenvironment's lipid metabolism and endosomal escape kinetics. While adipose is not the target, the principle of "endogenous targeting" — harnessing the lipid-sensing biology of a particular tissue to drive selective uptake — is precisely the conceptual frame being explored for adipose RNA delivery. Ionizable lipid selection and PEG-lipid density are the primary tuning knobs described, both of which are relevant parameters for future adipose-tropic LNP design.

## What to Watch

The convergence of Mendelian randomization evidence from two independent large studies this week makes a strong case that ANGPTL4 is not merely correlated with cardiometabolic disease but causally embedded in it — and that inhibiting it by genetic proxy doesn't carry the same T2D liability as some other lipid-lowering targets. For RNA therapeutic developers, this is a green light signal: the genetic human data support the direction of intervention. On the delivery side, no papers this week specifically tackle adipose-selective RNA delivery, but the LNP engineering space continues to mature with organ-targeting strategies that could be redirected toward fat tissue. The miR-210/ANGPTL4/ferroptosis axis in lung injury is a sleeper finding worth monitoring — if this regulatory logic extends to adipose macrophages or adipocytes under metabolic stress, it could open a new entry point for RNA silencing approaches targeting ANGPTL4 in metabolic disease.
