## Papers

**Aberrant alternative splicing memorized in adipose tissue macrophages impedes efferocytosis during postobesity weight loss**
Takuro Miyazaki, Akira Shiraishi, Yuki Sugiura, et al. *Science Translational Medicine*, Aug 26, 2026.
[PMID: 42647594](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42647594/) | DOI: [10.1126/scitranslmed.adv0221](https://doi.org/10.1126/scitranslmed.adv0221)

Obesity leaves an epigenetic "memory" in adipose tissue macrophages (ATMs) that keeps proinflammatory programs active even after weight loss. This group traces that memory to faulty mRNA splicing driven by dysfunction of the CWC22/exon junction complex, which impairs efferocytosis (the clearance of dead cells) in ATMs. Using multiomics and gene-targeting in mouse models, they found that over half of the obesity-induced splicing changes in ATMs persist after weight loss, with about a quarter of them dependent on CWC22. The finding gives a mechanistic handle on why fat tissue inflammation and dysfunction can outlast the obesity itself — a "weight-loss doesn't fully reset the tissue" story that's relevant background for anyone thinking about durable adipose tissue interventions, including RNA-based ones aimed at the macrophage compartment.

**The role of adipose tissue macrophages in adipose tissue fibrosis**
Danhuai Zhang, Na Wu. *Metabolism: Clinical and Experimental*, Aug 24, 2026.
[PMID: 42636953](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42636953/) | DOI: [10.1016/j.metabol.2026.156752](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.metabol.2026.156752)

A timely review reframing adipose tissue macrophages (ATMs) beyond the old M1/M2 binary into a continuum of functional states — lipid-associated, perivascular, sympathetic-neuron-associated, septal, and collagen-expressing subsets — each with distinct effects on fibrosis, inflammation, and lipid handling. The authors map how ATM-driven fibro-inflammation connects to MASLD/MASH progression via TGF-beta/Smad, Piezo1/YAP, and integrin/FAK signaling, and they explicitly stratify emerging therapeutic strategies by evidence maturity: CCR2/CCR5 blockade and macrophage metabolic reprogramming have preclinical support, while Piezo1/integrin-targeted mechanotransduction, CAR-M therapy, and RNAi-based macrophage-targeting approaches are flagged as earlier-stage, proof-of-concept ideas. Worth a skim as a map of where adipose-targeted RNAi could plug into the fibrosis/complications side of the field, even though it isn't ANGPTL4-specific.

## What to watch

The two papers above both point the same direction: the adipose tissue macrophage is emerging as a therapeutic node in its own right, with RNAi explicitly named (if still early-stage) as one route in. Worth watching whether any group pairs adipose-tropic LNP or GalNAc-class delivery with an ATM-directed target — that would sit squarely at the intersection this show tracks. On the core ANGPTL4 side, no new mechanistic or clinical papers cleared the bar this week; expect the pipeline to refill as several groups with ANGPTL4-antibody and RNAi programs report out over the coming months.
