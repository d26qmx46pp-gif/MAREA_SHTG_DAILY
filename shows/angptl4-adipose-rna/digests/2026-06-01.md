# ANGPTL4 & Adipose RNA Update — Monday June 1, 2026

**Bottom line:** Three new papers this week illuminate ANGPTL4's role in tumor microenvironments, adipose-driven metabolic dysfunction in heart failure, and as an early-life obesity biomarker; no new adipose RNA delivery papers appeared in the lookback window.

## Papers

**Hypoxia Drives ANGPTL4 Expression in Tumor-Associated Macrophages and Promotes Intrahepatic Cholangiocarcinoma Intrahepatic Metastasis**
Lin Z, Hua H, Huang Y, Li X et al. *Cancer Letters*, May 2026.
[PMID: 42214805](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42214805) | [DOI: 10.1016/j.canlet.2026.218614](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.canlet.2026.218614)

This study identifies a new axis by which ANGPTL4 promotes cancer spread: under hypoxic conditions, macrophages infiltrating intrahepatic cholangiocarcinoma (iCCA) tumors upregulate the transcription factor CEBPB, which directly drives ANGPTL4 expression. Macrophage-secreted ANGPTL4 then binds Integrin α5β1 on iCCA cells, activating FAK/Src/GSK3β signaling and facilitating intrahepatic metastasis. Single-cell RNA sequencing of 15 iCCA samples and a 255-patient transcriptomic cohort confirmed that ANGPTL4+ tumor-associated macrophages were enriched specifically in patients with intrahepatic metastasis. The findings add to a growing body of evidence that ANGPTL4 is not just a lipase regulator but also a paracrine pro-metastatic signal shaped by the tumor microenvironment. From a therapeutic standpoint, ANGPTL4 blockade in the hypoxic niche may warrant investigation in biliary cancers.

**Exerkine Dysregulation Links Visceral Adiposity to Skeletal Muscle Impairment in End-Stage Heart Failure with Reduced Ejection Fraction: Proteomic Evidence for a Cardio-Adipose-Muscle Axis**
Załęska-Kocięcka M, Mazuruk M, Szcześniak K, Łaba P et al. *Cardiovascular Diabetology*, May 2026.
[PMID: 42192415](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42192415) | [DOI: 10.1186/s12933-026-03190-4](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12933-026-03190-4)

In 73 patients with end-stage heart failure with reduced ejection fraction (HFrEF), proteomic profiling revealed profound dysregulation of circulating exerkines — signaling proteins secreted by adipose tissue, skeletal muscle, and other organs in response to exercise. ANGPTL4 emerged among the disrupted exerkines, linking elevated visceral adipose tissue volume to impaired skeletal muscle quantity and quality (assessed by psoas muscle density on CT). The study provides proteomics-level evidence for a "cardio-adipose-muscle axis" in advanced HFrEF, where aberrant adipokine/myokine signaling perpetuates cachexia and exercise intolerance. For ANGPTL4, this positions it as a potential biomarker of the adipose-muscle crosstalk failure state in cardiovascular disease, independent of its canonical role in triglyceride metabolism.

**Early-Life Proteomic and Microbiome Features Signal Obesity Risk Across 26 Years of Follow-Up**
Ahrens A, Dias R, Hyötyläinen T, White P et al. *mSystems*, May 2026.
[PMID: 42206849](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42206849) | [DOI: 10.1128/msystems.01424-25](https://doi.org/10.1128/msystems.01424-25)

A prospective analysis of the Swedish ABIS birth cohort (n = 16,683) used cord blood proteomics, gut microbiome profiling at age one, and metabolomics to identify early biomarkers of obesity risk followed to age 26. ANGPTL4 was elevated in cord blood among infants who went on to develop obesity, implicating it as part of a neonatal lipid-metabolic programming signature alongside other inflammatory and lipid-handling proteins. The gut microbiome at age one showed compositional differences in future-obese children, suggesting early gut-liver-adipose axis alterations that track long-term adiposity. This longitudinal evidence raises the question of whether ANGPTL4 dysregulation in early life is a cause, consequence, or biomarker of the developmental metabolic set-point for adiposity.

## What to Watch

The convergence of ANGPTL4 across three very different disease contexts this week — biliary cancer, advanced heart failure, and neonatal obesity programming — underscores how broadly this protein participates in metabolic-inflammatory crosstalk beyond its triglyceride-clearing function. The cholangiocarcinoma paper is particularly striking: CEBPB-driven ANGPTL4 in hypoxic macrophages as a metastasis driver is a mechanistically clean story that could attract oncology attention to a target traditionally owned by cardiometabolics. On the RNA delivery front, no new adipose-tropic siRNA, LNP, or GalNAc papers appeared in the 8-day window — a thin week for that topic, likely reflecting normal publication rhythm. Watch for follow-on work linking any of these ANGPTL4 findings to targeted knockdown strategies, as the combination of mechanistic clarity and druggable biology makes each of these contexts a potential therapeutic entry point.
