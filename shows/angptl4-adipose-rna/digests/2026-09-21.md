# ANGPTL4 & Adipose RNA — Monday September 21

**Bottom line:** A thin week for ANGPTL4-specific news, but one strong hit on the delivery side — a microneedle/LNP-mRNA platform that directly targets subcutaneous adipose tissue to reverse fibrotic changes in skin disease.

## Papers

**Layer-Specific Dermal and Subcutaneous Delivery via Modular LNP-Hydrogel-Integrated Threaded Microneedles**
Fangzhou Xie, Yixiang Zhang, Shifeng Ling, et al. — *Advanced Science*, September 21, 2026
[PMID: 42765288](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42765288/) | [DOI: 10.1002/advs.77624](https://doi.org/10.1002/advs.77624)

A team led by researchers using spatial transcriptomics built a threaded microneedle device that delivers two different payloads to two different tissue layers in a single insertion. A fast-releasing hydrogel on the needle's surface delivers a SIRT1 agonist to calm senescent dermal fibroblasts, while helical grooves on the needle shaft directionally deposit PGC1α mRNA packaged in lipid nanoparticles (LNPs) into the subcutaneous adipose layer as the needle rotates in. The mRNA is expressed locally and reverses fibrotic changes in adipocytes. In a mouse model of bleomycin-induced skin fibrosis, the system reduced dermal senescence, cut down extracellular matrix buildup, and restored the architecture of subcutaneous fat. This is a notable proof of concept for precisely routing mRNA-LNP cargo into adipose tissue using mechanical/anatomical targeting rather than relying solely on lipid chemistry or ligand conjugation — a complementary approach to GalNAc- or ionizable-lipid-based tropism strategies that this show usually covers.
