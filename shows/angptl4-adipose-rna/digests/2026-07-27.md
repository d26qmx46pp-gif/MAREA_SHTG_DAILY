# ANGPTL4 & Adipose RNA — Monday July 27

**Bottom line:** A quiet week for the core beat — no new adipose-targeted RNA delivery papers and no new patents, but two ANGPTL4 biology papers stand out: a new placental biomarker study in gestational diabetes and a review reaffirming ANGPTL4's place in the causal triglyceride-ASCVD pathway.

## Papers

**ANGPTL4 in gestational diabetes: a diagnostic biomarker linked to placental senescence**
He Y, Chen H, Zhang C, Yang S, Yang X, et al. *Functional & Integrative Genomics*, July 23, 2026.
[PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42487020/) | [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10142-026-01978-x)

The authors mined placental transcriptomic data to find senescence-related genes that track with gestational diabetes mellitus (GDM), then built a machine-learning classifier combining ANGPTL4 and DST that discriminated GDM cases with an AUC of 0.95 in training and 0.87 in validation. In a prospective nested case-control study (30 GDM cases, 30 controls), plasma ANGPTL4 was measured by ELISA across all three trimesters, and single-cell RNA sequencing was used to map which placental cell types express ANGPTL4 and how they communicate with neighboring cells. The findings position ANGPTL4 as a candidate early, blood-based biomarker for GDM risk tied to accelerated trophoblast senescence — a reproductive-metabolic angle that's outside Marea's core lipid indication but adds to the growing list of contexts where ANGPTL4 dysregulation shows up as a readable signal.

**Triglyceride-Rich Lipoproteins and ASCVD: Evidence for Causality and Challenges in Therapeutic Translation**
Björnson E, Adiels M. *Current Atherosclerosis Reports*, July 20, 2026.
[PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42474848/) | [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11883-026-01442-y)

This review lays out the current genetic and epidemiological case that triglyceride-rich lipoproteins (TRLs) causally drive atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease, with human genetic studies of LPL, APOC3, ANGPTL3, and ANGPTL4 cited as the strongest support. The authors note that TRLs may act through both cholesterol deposition and inflammatory activation, distinguishing them mechanistically from LDL. They also flag the gap between genetic causality and clinical translation: fibrate trials have been inconsistent, icosapent ethyl's benefit likely runs through non-triglyceride mechanisms, and potent APOC3 inhibition hasn't yet shown short-term plaque regression. Useful framing for where ANGPTL4-directed approaches sit relative to other TRL-lowering strategies and why plaque-regression endpoints remain a high bar.
