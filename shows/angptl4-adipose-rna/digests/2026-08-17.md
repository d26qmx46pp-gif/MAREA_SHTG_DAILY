# ANGPTL4 & Adipose RNA — Monday August 17

**Bottom line:** The week's strongest signal is a Molecular Therapy paper using antibody-targeted lipid-coated nanoparticles to deliver siRNA into a specific pathogenic adipose stromal cell population — a concrete adipose-RNA-delivery advance — alongside a cluster of new mechanistic data tying ANGPTL4 to cancer metastasis, immune evasion, and renal fibrosis, plus a registered systematic-review protocol on ANGPTL3/ANGPTL4/APOC3 inhibitors for hypertriglyceridemia-driven pancreatitis.

## Papers

**Integrin α7 Defines a Profibrotic and Proinflammatory Adipose Stromal Population Targeted for Nanoparticle PAI-1 Gene Silencing in Obesity**
Mota L, Zhang D, Ortiz Soto Y, et al. *Molecular Therapy*, Aug 10, 2026.
[PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42576383/) | [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ymthe.2026.08.005)

The authors identify integrin α7 (ITGA7) as a marker of a proinflammatory, profibrotic stromal subpopulation within obese white adipose tissue that resists normal adipogenic differentiation. To hit this population selectively, they built lipid-coated mesoporous silica nanoparticles decorated with anti-ITGA7 antibodies, which preferentially accumulate in ITGA7-high cells both in culture and in obese mice. Using this platform to deliver siRNA against PAI-1 (a driver of obesity-related inflammation and fibrosis), they achieved targeted gene silencing in the pathogenic cell subset in vivo. This is a direct, working example of tissue- and cell-subtype-selective RNA delivery into adipose tissue — precisely the kind of targeting problem GalNAc-class conjugates solve for liver, but here solved with an antibody-nanoparticle approach for a specific adipose stromal niche.

**Novel triglyceride-lowering therapies to reduce the risk of acute pancreatitis in hypertriglyceridaemia: a protocol for systematic review and meta-analysis**
Wu Y, Liu S, Luo W, et al. *BMJ Open*, Aug 14, 2026.
[PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42601105/) | [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1136/bmjopen-2026-120485)

This is a published protocol (not yet results) for a systematic review pooling randomized trials of APOC3, ANGPTL3, and ANGPTL4 inhibitors in patients with severe hypertriglyceridemia, with acute pancreatitis incidence as the primary outcome. It's a useful marker of where the field's evidence bar is heading: once enough RCT data exist across this inhibitor class, someone is positioned to run the head-to-head synthesis. Worth revisiting when the completed review posts.

**Hypoxia drives ANGPTL4 expression in tumor-associated macrophages and promotes intrahepatic cholangiocarcinoma intrahepatic metastasis**
Lin Z, Hua H, Huang Y, et al. *Cancer Letters*, Sep 28, 2026.
[PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42214805/) | [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.canlet.2026.218614)

Using single-cell RNA-seq across 15 tumor samples plus a 255-patient validation cohort, the authors show that hypoxic regions of intrahepatic cholangiocarcinoma induce the transcription factor CEBPB in tumor-associated macrophages, which turns on ANGPTL4. Macrophage-secreted ANGPTL4 then engages integrin α5β1 on tumor cells, activating a FAK/Src/GSK3β/β-catenin cascade that drives an EMT-like state and promotes metastasis. Antibody blockade of ANGPTL4 reduced metastatic spread in their models. It's another entry in the growing literature positioning ANGPTL4 as a paracrine signal connecting a hypoxic microenvironment to invasive, metastatic tumor behavior — mechanistically distinct from its lipid-metabolism role but relevant to any ANGPTL4-targeting therapeutic's off-tissue effects.

**Angiopoietin-like protein 4 deficiency promotes tumor immune infiltration and CD8+ T cell activity**
Liang L, Liu Y, Shi Y, et al. *Molecular Therapy — Oncology*, Sep 17, 2026.
[PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42540400/) | [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.omton.2026.201168)

Using ANGPTL4 knockout in both tumor cells and host, this group shows that loss of ANGPTL4 slows tumor growth and metastasis, remodels the extracellular matrix, and — notably — increases intratumoral CD8+ T cell infiltration. This adds an immuno-oncology angle to ANGPTL4 biology: beyond its established roles in lipid handling and angiogenesis, ANGPTL4 appears to help tumors evade T cell surveillance, which is relevant context for anyone evaluating systemic ANGPTL4 inhibition and its potential interactions with checkpoint immunotherapy.

**Renal tubular epithelial-derived Angptl4 promotes renal fibrosis by inducing tubular cell senescence**
Li H, Zhu Z, Li P, et al. *Biochemical and Biophysical Research Communications*, Aug 27, 2026.
[PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42289177/) | [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.bbrc.2026.154133)

In both chronic kidney disease patients and mouse models, the authors find elevated Angptl4 in renal tubular epithelial cells, and show it drives fibrosis by promoting tubular cell senescence via integrin β1/FAK signaling; senescent cells then trigger fibroblast activation and matrix deposition. Genetic knockout of Angptl4 blunted fibrosis. This extends ANGPTL4's complication profile beyond cardiometabolic disease into a locally-produced driver of kidney fibrosis — a tissue-specific, integrin-mediated mechanism that parallels the cholangiocarcinoma paper above.

**Chronic High-Fat Diet Feeding Reduces the Rate of Postprandial Triglyceride Absorption Independent of Intestinal ANGPTL4**
Shetty SK, Sylvers-Davie KL, Davies BSJ. *Journal of Lipid Research*, Aug 14, 2026.
[PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42600744/) | [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jlr.2026.101124)

This is a useful negative result. Intestinal ANGPTL4 had been proposed as an endogenous pancreatic lipase inhibitor that slows dietary fat absorption, with high-fat feeding expected to induce ANGPTL4 and further blunt triglyceride uptake. Using intestinal-epithelium-specific ANGPTL4 knockout mice, the authors confirm that chronic high-fat feeding does reduce postprandial triglyceride absorption — but the effect is preserved in ANGPTL4 knockouts, meaning it happens through a different, still-unidentified mechanism. Good housekeeping data for anyone modeling ANGPTL4's role in gut lipid handling; it narrows where ANGPTL4 actually matters physiologically.

## What to watch

The adipose-targeted nanoparticle paper is the most actionable item this week if the interest is delivery technology rather than pure biology — antibody-directed lipid-coated nanoparticles hitting a specific stromal subpopulation is a template worth tracking for ANGPTL4-directed adipose delivery specifically, since none of this week's papers combine the two (ANGPTL4 target + adipose RNA delivery) directly. On the biology side, the throughline across three papers (cholangiocarcinoma, renal fibrosis, tumor immune evasion) is ANGPTL4 acting locally through integrin signaling (α5β1, β1) rather than through its classical LPL-inhibition role — worth flagging if systemic ANGPTL4 inhibitors are being evaluated for off-target tissue effects. The BMJ Open protocol is one to bookmark and check back on once the underlying trial data are pooled.
