# ANGPTL4 & Adipose RNA — Monday August 3

**Bottom line:** The strongest new finding this week ties ANGPTL4 directly to podocyte lysosomal dysfunction in diabetic kidney disease, while a second paper reinforces ANGPTL4's role in tumor immune evasion — together they widen the therapeutic rationale for ANGPTL4 inhibition beyond lipid metabolism; no new adipose-tropic RNA delivery platform papers surfaced this week.

## Papers

**ANGPTL4 Exacerbates Renal Injury in Diabetic Kidney Disease by Impairing Podocyte Lipophagy via Compromised Lysosomal Degradative Function**
Xiaojing Liu, Shimin Jiang, Zhenkun Yang, Shunlai Shang, Jiayi Li, et al. *Advanced Science*, July 27, 2026.
[PMID: 42504995](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42504995/) | [DOI: 10.1002/advs.76771](https://doi.org/10.1002/advs.76771)

In diabetic kidney disease, podocytes upregulate ANGPTL4 in proportion to proteinuria and rate of renal decline, and the protein appears to actively worsen injury rather than just marking it. Mechanistically, high glucose plus palmitic acid drives ANGPTL4 up, which pushes TFEB out of the nucleus, raises lysosomal pH, and cripples cathepsin B and lysosomal acid lipase activity — the cell loses its ability to clear accumulated lipid droplets. Knocking down ANGPTL4 restored autophagic flux and lysosomal function, and forcing TFEB back into an active state (a constitutively active TFEB-S211A mutant) reversed the lipid clearance defect and reduced fibrotic remodeling. Extracellular neutralization of a recombinant ANGPTL4 fragment also partially reversed the phenotype, which is notable because it suggests an antibody or ligand-trap approach could work alongside knockdown strategies. This adds diabetic nephropathy to the growing list of ANGPTL4-driven complications and strengthens the case for renal-relevant delivery of ANGPTL4-lowering therapeutics.

**Angiopoietin-like protein 4 deficiency promotes tumor immune infiltration and CD8+ T cell activity**
Linjun Liang, Yingzi Liu, Yue Shi, Peng Zhang, Nguan Soon Tan, et al. *Molecular Therapy — Oncology*, September 17, 2026 (online ahead of print).
[PMID: 42540400](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42540400/) | [DOI: 10.1016/j.omton.2026.201168](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.omton.2026.201168)

This paper looks at ANGPTL4 from the tumor microenvironment side rather than metabolism: loss of ANGPTL4 relieves an immunosuppressive brake, allowing more CD8+ T cells to infiltrate tumors and function more effectively. That mirrors a pattern seen elsewhere with ANGPTL4 — it tends to protect tissue from immune or metabolic stress in ways that become a liability in disease states, whether that's a tumor evading immune clearance or a kidney accumulating lipid. For a company building ANGPTL4-directed therapeutics, oncology combination angles (pairing ANGPTL4 inhibition with checkpoint blockade) are worth tracking as an adjacent, non-metabolic indication space, even if it's not the primary program focus.

**Therapeutic Gfral silencing via antisense oligonucleotides ameliorates cancer-associated cachexia and extends survival in tumor-bearing mice**
Hyun Jung Hong, Min Hee Lee, Minsung Park, Jung Tae Kim, Jaehun Kim, et al. *Cell Reports Medicine*, July 27, 2026.
[PMID: 42508405](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42508405/) | [DOI: 10.1016/j.xcrm.2026.102939](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.xcrm.2026.102939)

Not an adipose-tropic delivery paper, but relevant to the RNA-therapeutics side of this show's beat: the authors built an ASO (Gfral-ASO, "A427") that silences the GDF15 receptor GFRAL specifically in the brainstem, administered by a single intracerebroventricular dose. In cachectic tumor-bearing mice, this reversed body weight loss, restored muscle mass and grip strength, and — notably for this audience — restored adipose depot mass, outperforming a systemic anti-GDF15 antibody on some measures. It's a reminder that central (CNS-restricted) ASO delivery can produce peripheral adipose benefits without the ASO ever needing to reach adipose tissue directly, which is a useful contrast case against adipose-targeted delivery strategies (GalNAc-style or LNP approaches that aim to act locally in fat).

**Gestational exposure to particulate matter and offspring metabolic programming via ANGPTL4/SIRT3 signaling**
Jianong Lv, Ruiyang Ding, Chen Liang, Li Tian, Zhiru Liu, et al. *Journal of Advanced Research*, August 2026.
[PMID: 41248709](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41248709/) | [DOI: 10.1016/j.jare.2025.11.022](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jare.2025.11.022)

This study identifies ANGPTL4, SIRT3, and D2-hydroxyglutarate as candidate biomarkers linking maternal fine-particulate-matter (PM2.5) exposure to metabolic programming in offspring. It's a mechanistic/epidemiological piece rather than a therapeutics paper, but it's a useful data point for the broader ANGPTL4 biology picture: it adds an early-life environmental exposure axis to the list of contexts (alongside diabetes, kidney disease, and cancer) where ANGPTL4 dysregulation shows up as a downstream signal.

## What to watch

The two strongest signals this week both point toward ANGPTL4 acting as an active driver of tissue damage rather than a passive biomarker — first in podocytes via a TFEB-lysosome-lipophagy axis, and separately in the tumor immune microenvironment. Both mechanisms suggest that lowering ANGPTL4 (whether by knockdown, antibody neutralization, or small molecule) could have benefits beyond the classic triglyceride story, and it's worth watching whether either group follows up with an RNA-based knockdown approach in vivo. On the delivery side, no new adipose-tropic siRNA/ASO/LNP/GalNAc platform papers appeared this week; the Gfral-ASO cachexia paper is the closest analog and is a good illustration of how CNS-restricted delivery can still move adipose biology, which is a useful comparator when benchmarking adipose-direct delivery approaches.
