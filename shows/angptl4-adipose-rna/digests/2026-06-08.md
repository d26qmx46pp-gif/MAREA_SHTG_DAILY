# ANGPTL4 & Adipose RNA Update — Monday June 8, 2026

**Bottom line:** ANGPTL4 emerges prominently as a therapeutic target in a landmark Circulation Research review of triglyceride-rich lipoprotein metabolism and cardiovascular risk in diabetes, while new work in adipocyte biology highlights PPARγ-mediated transcriptional programs and antiadipogenic peptide mechanisms with direct ANGPTL4 relevance.

## Papers

**Triglyceride-Rich Lipoprotein Metabolism and Cardiovascular Risk in Diabetes: Bedside to Bench to Bedside**
Ginsberg HN, Bornfeldt KE
*Circulation Research* | Jun 2026
[PMID: 42241511](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42241511/) | [DOI: 10.1161/CIRCRESAHA.125.327267](https://doi.org/10.1161/CIRCRESAHA.125.327267)

This high-impact review in Circulation Research positions ANGPTL4 — alongside ANGPTL3 and ANGPTL8 — as a key suppressor of triglyceride-rich lipoprotein (TRL) clearance that amplifies cardiovascular risk in diabetes when combined with genetic variants or metabolic stress. Using six clinical vignettes paired with mechanistic preclinical data, Ginsberg and Bornfeldt trace how insulin resistance drives excess VLDL secretion and impaired LPL-mediated clearance, with ANGPTL4's inhibitory effect on LPL at the center of the story. The review explicitly frames ANGPTL3, ANGPTL4, and APOC3 as the next generation of TRL-lowering therapeutic targets, noting that cardiovascular outcome trials specifically targeting these angiopoietin-like proteins are now underway or in active development. For Marea, this piece serves as a high-visibility anchor confirming the clinical case for ANGPTL4 inhibition in cardiometabolic disease. It is a must-read framing piece for anyone building the therapeutic rationale for ANGPTL4-targeted RNA medicines.

**Oligomeric Peptides LLRLTDL and GYALPCDCL Alleviate Obesity through HO-1/Nrf2-Dependent Pathways in High-Fat Diet-Induced Mice**
Marasinghe CK, Dayarathne LA, Suryaningtyas IT, Jasmadi, Je JY
*The Journal of Nutritional Biochemistry* | Aug 2026
[PMID: 41921669](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41921669/) | [DOI: 10.1016/j.jnutbio.2026.110360](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jnutbio.2026.110360)

Ark shell-derived bioactive peptides Bu1 and Bu2 suppress adipogenesis in mesenchymal stem cells by downregulating PPARγ, C/EBPα, SREBP1, and downstream adipocyte genes including FABP2, FASN, and LPL — the same transcriptional node where ANGPTL4 is controlled. The peptides activate AMPK and hormone-sensitive lipase to promote lipolysis and reduce adipose tissue accumulation in HFD mice, while blunting systemic inflammation. While this study does not use RNA-based delivery, the mechanism reinforces the importance of the PPARγ/LPL/adipogenesis axis that ANGPTL4 sits within and suggests combinatorial opportunities for antiobesity strategies that engage both ANGPTL4 suppression and AMPK activation. The in vivo reductions in adipose tissue mass and improved lipid profiles in the HFD model are notable.

**An Artemisia scoparia Extract and Rosiglitazone Have Distinct but Overlapping Effects on Adipocyte Function**
Boudreau A, Yoo L, Harvey I, Mota de Sà P, Javvadi P
*Obesity* | Jun 2026
[PMID: 42015583](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42015583/) | [DOI: 10.1002/oby.70196](https://doi.org/10.1002/oby.70196)

This study characterizes Artemisia scoparia extract (SCO) as a context-dependent, selective PPARγ agonist in adipocytes, with transcriptional profiling by RNA-seq revealing overlapping but distinct gene expression programs compared to the full agonist rosiglitazone. SCO and rosiglitazone both require PPARγ for their antilipolytic effects (confirmed by siRNA knockdown), but SCO acts as a partial agonist with unique effects on redox-dependent protein multimerization, including formation of high-molecular-weight adiponectin complexes. Because ANGPTL4 is a canonical PPARγ target gene in adipocytes, the RNA-seq datasets from this study (if made available) could yield interesting ANGPTL4 expression data under partial vs. full PPARγ agonism. Methodologically, the siRNA knockdown approach validates that PPARγ silencing is achievable in primary adipocyte cultures — a useful proof-of-concept for adipose-targeted RNAi.
