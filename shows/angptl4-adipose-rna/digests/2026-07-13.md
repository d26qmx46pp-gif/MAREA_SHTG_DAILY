# ANGPTL4 & Adipose RNA — Monday July 13

**Bottom line:** A new review makes the clinical case for ANGPTL-axis therapeutics in dyslipidemia, while two mechanism papers add fresh evidence that ANGPTL4 drives drug resistance and inflammation in solid tumors — no adipose-targeted RNA delivery papers surfaced this cycle.

## Papers

**The Angiopoietin-like Protein (ANGPTL) Axis in Dyslipidemia: Mechanisms, Cardiovascular Risk, and Emerging Therapies**
Isabela C. Bitencourt, André Zimerman, Nicholas A. Marston, et al. *Current Atherosclerosis Reports* (2026).
[PMID: 42435249](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42435249/) | [DOI: 10.1007/s11883-026-01441-z](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11883-026-01441-z)

This review lays out the current state of ANGPTL3-4-8 axis biology as a lipid-lowering target. It reiterates that loss-of-function variants in ANGPTL3 and ANGPTL4 track with lower triglycerides and reduced coronary artery disease risk, and that ANGPTL3-directed monoclonal antibodies and RNA-based therapies already produce meaningful drops in triglycerides, remnant cholesterol, LDL-C, and apoB — largely independent of the LDL receptor. The authors highlight ANGPTL3 inhibition as "practice-changing" in homozygous familial hypercholesterolemia and flag the ANGPTL3/8 complex and ANGPTL4 itself as the next frontier for refining lipid-lowering effects. Early genome-editing approaches aimed at durable ANGPTL3 suppression also get a mention. Useful as a state-of-the-field reference for where ANGPTL4-directed programs sit relative to the more mature ANGPTL3 space.

**ANGPTL4 contributes to osimertinib resistance through NDRG1-associated extracellular matrix remodeling in EGFR-mutant NSCLC organoid models**
Dai-Yan Zhou, Pei Xie, Si-Yuan Wang, et al. *Cancer Cell International* (2026).
[PMID: 42415069](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42415069/) | [DOI: 10.1186/s12935-026-04400-5](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12935-026-04400-5)

Using 3D osimertinib-resistant organoid models of EGFR-mutant lung cancer, this group found ANGPTL4 is upregulated in resistant tumors and functionally drives resistance by binding NDRG1 and activating extracellular-matrix remodeling programs. Knocking down ANGPTL4 restored drug sensitivity; overexpressing it induced resistance, with the effect reproduced in an independent cell line. A virtual screen against 2.08 million small molecules turned up five lead compounds with high predicted binding affinity for ANGPTL4, though these are computational hits awaiting biochemical validation. It's a cancer-biology paper rather than a cardiometabolic one, but it's a reminder that ANGPTL4 has drug-discovery relevance well beyond lipid metabolism, and any small-molecule ANGPTL4 binders that emerge from oncology work are worth tracking for off-target or on-target read-through into the lipid space.

**The significance of vascular endothelial growth factor and systemic inflammatory reaction in bladder cancer patients**
Zofia Strojny, Dominika Kanikowska, Alina Kanikowska, et al. *Cytokine* (2026).
[PMID: 42424711](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42424711/) | [DOI: 10.1016/j.cyto.2026.157190](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cyto.2026.157190)

A cross-sectional study (69 bladder cancer patients, 32 non-cancer controls, 34 healthy controls) tested whether circulating ANGPTL4, IGF-1, FGF, and VEGF track with systemic inflammation in bladder cancer. This is the thinnest of the three papers mechanistically — it's a biomarker correlation study, not a mechanistic or interventional one — but it adds another data point on ANGPTL4 as a circulating pro-angiogenic/inflammatory marker outside the metabolic disease context where it's usually studied.

## What to watch

This was a lean week for the core adipose-RNA-delivery arm of this beat: no siRNA, ASO, LNP, or GalNAc papers targeting adipose tissue cleared the relevance bar in the last 8 days. All three qualifying papers were ANGPTL4-biology stories, and two candidate papers on ANGPTL4 in tumor-associated macrophages (cholangiocarcinoma) and renal fibrosis were excluded as repeats from prior digests. The throughline worth watching is ANGPTL4's expanding footprint in oncology (drug resistance, angiogenesis) alongside its established cardiometabolic role — if small-molecule ANGPTL4 binders from cancer drug-discovery efforts mature, they could become relevant chemical starting points for lipid-focused programs too. Next week: watch for any follow-through on the ANGPTL3/8-complex and genome-editing angles flagged in the atherosclerosis review, and keep an eye out for the adipose-RNA-delivery literature to pick back up.
