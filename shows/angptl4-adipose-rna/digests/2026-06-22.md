# ANGPTL4 & Adipose RNA Update — Monday June 22, 2026

**Bottom line:** A thin week for direct ANGPTL4 therapeutics, but a standout paper demonstrates that circular RNA delivered via lipid nanoparticles can drive sustained GDF15-mediated fat burning in obese mice — a proof-of-concept for RNA-LNP therapies targeting adipose metabolism.

---

## Papers

**Preclinical efficacy of engineered CircRNA-GDF15@LNP for sustained weight loss and metabolic regulation in diet-induced obese mice.**
Wu Weiyan, Wei Wanli, Ge Huihui, Yang Di, Han Thumb Tai, Yao Yunxia, et al.
*Journal of Controlled Release*, June 2026.
[PMID: 42323019](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42323019/) | [DOI: 10.1016/j.jconrel.2026.115117](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jconrel.2026.115117)

This study presents CircGDF15@LNP, a circular RNA formulation encapsulated in lipid nanoparticles that encodes a mutant form of GDF15 — a hormone that suppresses appetite and boosts metabolism. Circular RNA was chosen specifically for its exceptional stability and prolonged protein expression compared to linear mRNA, a key advantage when targeting metabolic diseases that require sustained drug levels. Biweekly intravenous injection in diet-induced obese mice over four weeks produced durable GDF15 overexpression, activated PPARα/γ and UCP1 signaling in both liver and adipose tissue, and achieved meaningful weight loss without clear adverse effects. The work is notable for its mechanistic reach into adipose thermogenesis — UCP1 upregulation in fat tissue suggests the therapy may stimulate brown/beige fat activity, not just appetite suppression. For teams developing RNA therapeutics targeting metabolic disease, this is a useful benchmark for the circRNA-LNP modality.

**Fibrillin-1 inhibits early adipogenic commitment via αvβ3 integrin signaling.**
Siddiqui Iram Fatima S, Nelea Valentin, Tiedemann Kerstin, Brazeau Elina, Agourame Sarah, Reinhardt Dieter P.
*Journal of Molecular Biology*, June 2026.
[PMID: 42303090](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42303090/) | [DOI: 10.1016/j.jmb.2026.169912](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jmb.2026.169912)

Researchers used siRNA-mediated silencing of integrin αvβ3 in primary mesenchymal progenitors and 3T3-L1 cells to dissect how extracellular fibrillin-1 restrains the earliest stages of fat cell formation. They found that fibrillin-1 engages αvβ3 integrin to activate a FAK–Src–ERK signaling cascade that suppresses C/EBPα and PPARγ, locking progenitor cells in an undifferentiated state — a brake that is only relevant during the commitment window, not during later maturation. The clinical hook is Marfan syndrome: fibrillin-1 mutations cause paradoxically increased adiposity in older patients, and this pathway may explain why. The siRNA approach used here is mechanistic, not therapeutic, but understanding these adipogenesis checkpoints is foundational for anyone engineering RNA therapeutics that aim to modulate fat depot expansion or browning.

**Host-microbial co-regulation mediates rumen volatile fatty acid production and utilization in dairy cows.**
Wang Wei, Ren Jianrong, Li Jing, et al.
*Science China: Life Sciences*, July 2026.
[PMID: 41811561](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41811561/) | [DOI: 10.1007/s11427-025-3206-7](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11427-025-3206-7)

A large multi-omics study in 530 Holstein bulls found that rumen epithelial genes involved in fatty acid β-oxidation — including ANGPTL4 — are negatively correlated with volatile fatty acid concentrations and the microbes that produce them. This positions ANGPTL4 in the rumen as a brake on fat mobilization in the context of gut microbiota signaling, consistent with its well-established role in peripheral tissues as an LPL inhibitor. The study is livestock-focused and not directly translatable to human therapeutics, but it adds to the accumulating evidence that ANGPTL4 is a key node in the feedback loop between microbial metabolite production, epithelial fatty acid handling, and systemic lipid partitioning — biology that is relevant for understanding why ANGPTL4 inhibition can have complex metabolic consequences.

## What to watch

The CircRNA-GDF15@LNP paper is the headline result this week: it validates that circular RNA can be packaged in standard LNP formulations and achieve durable metabolic benefit in adipose tissue, without apparent toxicity. The key open questions are (1) whether IV delivery can be replaced with more practical routes for adipose targeting — subcutaneous or inhaled LNPs are an active area — and (2) whether GDF15's effects on brown adipose UCP1 are sufficient to drive meaningful thermogenesis in humans, where the GDF15 receptor GFRAL is expressed almost exclusively in the brainstem rather than fat. The fibrillin-1/αvβ3 axis adds nuance to adipogenesis control and may suggest future siRNA targets for conditions like lipodystrophy or metabolic syndrome characterized by unhealthy fat expansion. Watch for follow-up studies combining LNP-delivered RNA with adipose-selective lipid compositions to improve tissue targeting beyond the liver.
