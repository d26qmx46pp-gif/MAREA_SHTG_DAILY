# ANGPTL4 & Adipose RNA — Monday May 11

**Bottom line:** A large LURIC cohort analysis ties CD-ANGPTL4 to heart failure incidence and mortality, an OPSCC study reframes ANGPTL4 as a tumor suppressor in head-and-neck cancer, and a Cardiovascular Diabetology review flags ANGPTL4 inhibition as a candidate combination therapy for diabetic vascular complications. Patent search is offline this week.

## Papers

**Associations of ANGPTL3/4/8 proteins and complexes with heart failure and heart failure mortality.**
Silbernagel G, Maas AC, Li H, Lemen D, Chen YQ, Zhen EY. *Lipids in health and disease*, 2026 May 7. [PMID: 42098711](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42098711/) | [DOI: 10.1186/s12944-026-02973-8](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12944-026-02973-8)

In 2,394 patients from the LUdwigshafen RIsk and Cardiovascular Health (LURIC) cohort followed for a median of 9.8 years, the authors measured ANGPTL3, ANGPTL4, ANGPTL8, and the ANGPTL3/8 and ANGPTL4/8 complexes by dedicated immunoassays at baseline. ANGPTL4/8 and the C-terminal domain–containing form of ANGPTL4 (CD-ANGPTL4) were positively associated with both HFrEF and HFpEF, with NT-proBNP, and inversely with left ventricular ejection fraction. Of the 101 patients who died from heart failure, CD-ANGPTL4 showed the strongest positive association with HF mortality — stronger than ANGPTL3 — while the ANGPTL3/8 and ANGPTL4/8 complexes themselves were not linked to HF death. The mechanism is unclear, but the data argue that the LPL-inhibitory fragment of ANGPTL4 specifically, rather than the full-length protein or its 4/8 complex, may be the relevant cardiac risk signal. For programs developing ANGPTL4-lowering therapeutics, this is a useful clinical-biomarker datapoint suggesting that CD-ANGPTL4 readouts could matter for cardiovascular endpoint design.

**Inhibitory role of angiopoietin-like 4 for cancer progression in oropharyngeal squamous cell carcinoma.**
Mikoshiba T, Sekimizu M, Nakahara N, Saito S, Yoshihama K, Nagai R. *Oncology Reports*, 2026 Jun. [PMID: 42028751](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42028751/) | [DOI: 10.1016/j.canlet.2014.08.035](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.canlet.2014.08.035)

The authors retrospectively analyzed 137 OPSCC patients and measured tumor ANGPTL4 by immunohistochemistry. Patients with higher ANGPTL4 expression (≥7.7% positivity) had significantly better overall and disease-free survival, and on multivariate analysis low ANGPTL4 was an independent predictor of poor OS (HR 3.68) and DFS (HR 2.96). Functional confirmation came from siRNA-mediated ANGPTL4 knockdown in FaDu cells, which increased tumor cell proliferation, with GSEA on TCGA data implicating proliferation-linked pathways. This adds to the increasingly tissue-specific picture of ANGPTL4 in oncology, where the protein is pro-tumorigenic in some contexts (breast, melanoma, colorectal metastasis) and anti-tumorigenic in others. For RNAi or ASO programs targeting ANGPTL4 for cardiometabolic indications, the OPSCC signal reinforces the need for tissue-restricted delivery rather than broad systemic suppression.

**Single-cell insights into maladaptive endothelial plasticity and therapeutic targets in diabetic vascular complications.**
Alnuaimi NS, Lößlein L, Mousa M, Khader TA, Alkhaaldi A, Azzam SK. *Cardiovascular Diabetology*, 2026 May 6 (review). [PMID: 42092877](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42092877/) | [DOI: 10.1186/s12933-026-03187-z](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12933-026-03187-z)

This review synthesizes single-cell datasets on diabetic endothelial dysfunction across angiogenic, inflammatory, metabolic, and fibrotic axes, and proposes combination strategies anchored on the maladaptive transitions revealed by scRNA-seq. Notably, the authors call out pairing anti-VEGF therapy with inhibitors of MFAP4 or ANGPTL4 to defeat angiogenic bypass pathways that limit anti-VEGF efficacy in diabetic retinopathy and related vasculopathies. ANGPTL4 appears here not as a lipid-axis lever but as an angiocrine factor whose inhibition could rescue anti-VEGF failure — a different mechanism of interest than the LPL-regulating biology that drives the cardiometabolic ANGPTL4 thesis. The review also reinforces that tissue and vascular-bed context will determine when ANGPTL4 inhibition is helpful versus harmful, and underscores how thin human single-cell data on diabetic endothelium remains.

## What to watch

CD-ANGPTL4 as a clinical biomarker is the most actionable item from this week: if it tracks with heart failure mortality independent of the 4/8 complex, then trial designs for ANGPTL4 inhibitors in cardiometabolic disease may benefit from quantifying the cleaved C-terminal fragment alongside full-length protein. The OPSCC and diabetic vasculopathy data both push in the same direction strategically: ANGPTL4 has context-dependent effects and a systemic GalNAc-style hepatic silencer may not be the right tool — programs pursuing adipose-restricted RNA delivery, where the LPL-inhibitory C-terminal fragment originates locally with feeding/fasting cues, would sidestep the tumor-context and angiocrine concerns raised this week. We'll continue watching for adipose-targeted LNP, GalNAc-like adipocyte ligands, and the next clinical readouts from companies advancing ANGPTL4 silencing.
