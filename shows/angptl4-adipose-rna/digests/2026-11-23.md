# ANGPTL4 & Adipose RNA — Monday November 23

**Bottom line:** A new lipid-conjugated siRNA platform reports >90% extrahepatic knockdown in adipose, muscle, heart and aorta lasting six months in mice with supporting macaque data — the most consequential adipose-delivery result in some time — while four other papers position ANGPTL4 as a downstream effector across liver, spinal cord, heart and tumor immunity.

## Papers

**1. The LiCO platform: A novel benzimidazole-based lipid conjugate for versatile and sustained siRNA delivery beyond the liver**
Kang M, Gan Z, Ding Q, et al. *Molecular Therapy — Nucleic Acids*, 8 Dec 2026.
[PMID 43023057](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/43023057/) · [doi:10.1016/j.omtn.2026.103154](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.omtn.2026.103154)

This is the week's most directly relevant paper for adipose-targeted RNA. The authors built a lipid-conjugated oligonucleotide ("LiCO") — a duplex siRNA carrying a hexadecyl (C16) chain on the 5' end of the passenger strand, joined through a benzimidazole linker, synthesized as a single entity by standard solid-phase chemistry rather than formulated into a particle. The benzimidazole linker, not the lipid alone, was what drove activity: a linker-free C16 analog performed markedly worse. Work in CD36-knockout mice identified CD36-mediated uptake as the principal route to extrahepatic tissue, which is a mechanistically satisfying explanation for the tropism, since CD36 is highly expressed in adipose, muscle and heart. A single systemic dose produced greater than 90% target knockdown in skeletal muscle, heart, aorta and adipose tissue, with at least 50% silencing still present at six months and measurable activity at nine; hepatic silencing, by contrast, was transient. In cynomolgus macaques, two IV doses of a myostatin-targeting construct gave roughly 90% mRNA knockdown and 60–70% suppression of circulating myostatin protein through day 98, with progressive muscle hypertrophy and no adverse clinical, hematologic or biochemical findings reported. The caveats worth holding: these are the developers' own data, the adipose knockdown figure comes from mice rather than primates, and durability claims of six to nine months from a single dose invite independent replication. Still, if CD36-dependent conjugate uptake holds up, it is a credible non-LNP, non-GalNAc route into adipose.

**2. AT-RvD1 liposomes accumulate in spinal cord and reprogram microglia via an ANGPTL4–SUCLG1–propionate axis for treating neuropathic pain**
Wu K, Gao C, Fu S, et al. *Materials Today Bio*, Dec 2026.
[PMID 42898631](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42898631/) · [doi:10.1016/j.mtbio.2026.103726](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.mtbio.2026.103726)

Aspirin-triggered resolvin D1, a specialized pro-resolving mediator, shifts microglia toward an anti-inflammatory, pro-repair state — and the authors place ANGPTL4 at the head of the signaling chain, acting through SUCLG1 and propionate metabolism. To get around the need for intrathecal injection, they built an MG1 peptide-modified liposome carrying AT-RvD1 that reaches spinal microglia after intravenous dosing, with analgesic and anti-inflammatory effects and acceptable biosafety in mouse neuropathic pain models. The relevance here is indirect but real on two fronts: it is another report of ANGPTL4 operating as a metabolic-immune switch rather than purely an LPL inhibitor, and it is a worked example of peptide-directed liposomal tropism to a non-hepatic tissue. The mechanism is correlative in places and entirely rodent, so treat the axis as a hypothesis.

**3. Chronic stress-associated indoxyl sulfate upregulation exhausts CD8+ T cells and reduces anti-PD-1 efficacy in hepatocellular carcinoma**
Wang M, Zeng R, Liu P, et al. *Advanced Science*, 18 Nov 2026.
[PMID 43052051](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/43052051/) · [doi:10.1002/advs.78733](https://doi.org/10.1002/advs.78733)

ANGPTL4 shows up here as the terminal effector of a long mechanistic chain. Combining a clinical cohort with mouse models, the authors report that chronic stress and depressive-like states blunt anti-PD-1 efficacy in HCC. The proposed route: stress raises hepatic SULT1A1, which drives production of the tryptophan metabolite indoxyl sulfate; indoxyl sulfate activates AHR in the tumor cells (not in the T cells directly), inducing the E3 ligase RNF115, which ubiquitinates HDAC8 in a way that blocks its autophagic degradation; stabilized HDAC8 then increases secretion of ANGPTL4, which contributes to CD8+ T-cell exhaustion. Depleting or inhibiting HDAC8 restored PD-1 responsiveness under stress. Six sequential steps is a long causal chain and each link carries its own assumptions, but the immunosuppressive-secreted-factor role for ANGPTL4 is consistent with a growing oncology literature and worth tracking as a potential liability signal for systemic ANGPTL4 modulation.

**4. Multi-omics analysis reveals osteocyte loss as a potential trigger for liver lipid metabolic disorder**
Tian Y, Zhu S, Jiang C, et al. *BBA — Molecular Basis of Disease*, Feb 2027 (online now).
[PMID 42900995](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42900995/) · [doi:10.1016/j.bbadis.2026.168472](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.bbadis.2026.168472)

Using a postnatal osteocyte-ablation mouse (Dmp1-cre DTR-flox), the authors found that losing osteocytes produced hepatic steatosis, with single-cell and bulk transcriptomics plus metabolomics showing upregulated Cyp4a10, downregulated Scd1, and altered hepatocyte, endothelial and Kupffer cell programs. Among the remodeled cell-cell communication networks, they highlight an Angptl4–syndecan axis alongside Grn–Sort1. This is a bone-liver axis paper rather than an ANGPTL4 paper, and the Angptl4–Sdc signal is inference from ligand-receptor analysis of sequencing data, not a validated mechanism. Noted mainly because ANGPTL4–syndecan interactions remain an underexplored arm of ANGPTL4 biology distinct from the LPL story.

**5. Cellular senescence in diabetic cardiomyopathy: mechanistic insights and therapeutic perspectives**
Ye T, Yang D, Chang X, et al. *Cardiovascular Drugs and Therapy*, Dec 2026. Review.
[PMID 42518074](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42518074/) · [doi:10.1007/s10557-026-07923-1](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10557-026-07923-1)

A review placing cellular senescence at the center of diabetic cardiomyopathy, with FOXO1–ANGPTL4 named among the pathways driving senescence in cardiomyocytes and cardiac progenitor cells, alongside p53 and miRNA-dependent mechanisms. No new data; included as a marker that ANGPTL4 is now routinely listed in cardiac senescence frameworks. Useful for context, not for evidence.

## What to watch

The LiCO paper is the one to follow. A chemically simple, single-entity conjugate that reaches adipose through CD36 rather than through particle formulation or hepatocyte ASGPR would change the delivery calculus for any adipose-expressed target, and the macaque myostatin data make it more than a mouse curiosity. Watch for independent groups reproducing the CD36 dependency, and for whether adipose knockdown specifically — as opposed to muscle, where the macaque work was done — survives translation to primates. Separately, the recurring appearance of ANGPTL4 as a secreted immunosuppressive and senescence-associated factor across unrelated disease models is becoming hard to ignore; it is the kind of pleiotropy that eventually shows up in safety discussions for systemic ANGPTL4 knockdown, and it is worth building a picture of now rather than later.
