# ANGPTL4 & Adipose RNA — Monday June 29, 2026

**Bottom line:** A thin but focused week — ANGPTL4 surfaces in a gut-microbiome/adipose crosstalk review and in a mechanistic study linking YAP condensates to proangiogenic ANGPTL4 expression, while an adipocyte cell-death study employs adipocyte-specific siRNA to implicate caspase-8 in adipogenesis.

## Papers

**Reconsidering Obesity in India Through a Gut-Metabolic Lens: Mechanistic Insights and the Emerging Role of Synbiotics in Individuals with the Thin-Fat Phenotype**
Ganguly NK, Kalra S, Kapoor N, Rao P, Bakshi M
*Adv Ther*, Jul 2026 | [PMID: 41999447](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41999447/) | DOI: [10.1007/s12325-026-03577-5](https://doi.org/10.1007/s12325-026-03577-5)

This commentary addresses India's distinctive "thin-fat" obesity phenotype — normal or near-normal BMI with disproportionate visceral adiposity and insulin resistance — and argues that conventional caloric-imbalance models fall short. The authors weave ANGPTL4 into a gut-metabolic circuit: short-chain fatty acids from microbial fiber fermentation modulate ANGPTL4-mediated lipid partitioning, and when gut dysbiosis reduces SCFA output, altered ANGPTL4 activity contributes to ectopic fat deposition. Alongside TLR4-driven inflammatory signaling and bile acid biotransformation, ANGPTL4 is positioned as a key node connecting microbiome composition to adipose tissue crosstalk. The paper reviews randomized controlled trial evidence for synbiotic and prebiotic-botanical formulations as adjuncts to lifestyle modification, while acknowledging heterogeneity of formulations and limited long-term data. Though population-specific, the mechanistic framing reinforces ANGPTL4 as a tractable pharmacological target in metabolic disease.

**Disruption of YAP Biomolecular Condensates by Mechanical Stress Drives Intervertebral Disc Vascularization**
Mao J, Wu Q, Yuan F, Wang G, Wang D et al.
*Sci Adv*, Jun 2026 | [PMID: 42361184](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42361184/) | DOI: [10.1126/sciadv.aee1546](https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.aee1546)

This mechanistic study in *Science Advances* reveals how mechanical stress induces pathological angiogenesis in the intervertebral disc by dismantling YAP liquid-liquid phase-separation condensates. Normally, YAP condensates sequester the transcription factor SNAI1; when mechanical load disrupts them, SNAI1 is released and transcribes ANGPTL4 and VEGFA, which together drive disc vascularization. The authors validated this pathway in nucleus pulposus-specific Yap knockout mice, which showed enhanced vascularization. Crucially for the RNA delivery angle, inhibition of either the Hippo pathway or SNAI1 was achieved using nucleus pulposus cell membrane-coated liposomes loaded via microneedles — a targeted nanomedicine approach that suppressed vascularization and alleviated mechanical stress-induced low back pain in vivo. While the therapeutic target is disc degeneration rather than metabolic disease, the ANGPTL4 biology and the liposomal delivery architecture are directly relevant to Marea's interest in tissue-specific RNA medicines.

**Adipocyte Caspase-8 But Not RIPK3 Promotes Adiposity**
Chan CK, Aslam R, Yang F, Panesar A, Hakem A
*Cell Death Discov*, Jun 2026 | [PMID: 42337230](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42337230/) | DOI: [10.1038/s41420-026-03201-z](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41420-026-03201-z)

This study dissects the role of regulated cell death pathways in adipose tissue inflammation and adipogenesis. The authors generated adipocyte-specific caspase-8 knockout mice and found reduced adiposity relative to controls, while equivalent deletion of RIPK3 (the canonical necroptosis kinase) showed no such effect on weight or glucose homeostasis. Using siRNA-mediated knockdown of caspase-8 in 3T3-L1 adipocytes, the team confirmed that caspase-8 suppression impairs adipogenesis in vitro — a mechanism likely operating independently of RIPK3. The paper reframes caspase-8 as a pro-adipogenic regulator beyond its classical apoptotic role, and positions white adipose tissue necroptosis-independent inflammation as a driver of obesity-associated metabolic dysfunction. The siRNA validation in adipocytes is a clean proof-of-concept for RNA-based interrogation of adipocyte biology, even if the paper does not pursue a delivery technology.

## What to Watch

Three themes converge this week: ANGPTL4 keeps appearing as a downstream effector of upstream signals — gut microbiome dysbiosis, YAP condensate disruption — rather than as an autonomous driver, reinforcing its attractiveness as a sentinel readout for metabolic state. The *Sci Adv* disc vascularization study is worth tracking because it demonstrates that cell membrane-coated liposomes can achieve tissue-specific delivery and meaningful pharmacodynamic suppression of ANGPTL4 in vivo, even in a non-metabolic indication. Finally, the adipocyte caspase-8 paper adds another siRNA-validated target to the landscape of WAT inflammation, reminding the field that the same RNA delivery challenge — getting cargo into adipocytes efficiently — underlies diverse therapeutic hypotheses. Watch for follow-on work characterizing the ionizable lipid or GalNAc-conjugate requirements for adipocyte-selective delivery, which remains an open engineering problem.
