# ANGPTL4 & Adipose RNA Update — Monday May 25, 2026

**Subject:** ANGPTL4 & Adipose RNA — Monday May 25

**Bottom line:** Intestinal ANGPTL4 emerges as a diet-and-microbiome integrator driving gut barrier dysfunction in early fatty liver disease, while new ionizable-lipid LNP platforms continue to expand the toolkit for tissue-selective RNA delivery beyond the liver.

## Papers

### 1. Angptl4 integrates dietary and microbial signals to disrupt gut barrier function in MASH

**Chua D., Low Z., Kim J., Lee Y., Kiatbumrung R., et al.**  
*Nature communications* | 2026 May 22  
[PMID 42173835](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42173835/) | [DOI: 10.1038/s41467-026-72575-6](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-026-72575-6)

Chua et al. establish intestinal ANGPTL4 as a molecular hub that senses both dietary fat and gut microbial composition to regulate gut barrier integrity and hepatic oxidative stress in metabolic dysfunction-associated steatohepatitis (MASH). Using germ-free and antibiotic-treated mouse models, they show that high-fat diet feeding upregulates intestinal Angptl4, disrupting tight junction proteins and increasing gut permeability. Germ-free mice were partially protected, demonstrating that the microbiome amplifies ANGPTL4-driven barrier dysfunction. Importantly, intestinal-specific Angptl4 knockout mice were protected from early MASLD features, positioning the intestinal isoform as a promising tissue-selective therapeutic target. This work in *Nature Communications* shifts the ANGPTL4 conversation from purely lipase-inhibition toward a gut-liver axis regulatory role with direct implications for MASH therapeutics.

### 2. Inhibitory role of angiopoietin-like 4 for cancer progression in oropharyngeal squamous cell carcinoma

**Mikoshiba T., Sekimizu M., Nakahara N., Saito S., Yoshihama K., et al.**  
*Oncology reports* | 2026 Jun  
[PMID 42028751](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42028751/) | [DOI: 10.3892/or.2026.9122](https://doi.org/10.3892/or.2026.9122)

Mikoshiba et al. report that ANGPTL4 acts as a tumor suppressor in oropharyngeal squamous cell carcinoma (OPSCC), a finding that contrasts with its pro-tumorigenic role in many solid tumors. High ANGPTL4 expression correlated with improved overall survival and reduced lymph node metastasis in a clinical cohort. Mechanistic studies showed that ANGPTL4 overexpression suppressed cell proliferation, migration, and invasion in OPSCC cell lines, and was strongly associated with HPV-positive tumors. This tissue-context-dependent duality — tumor suppressor in HNSCC, pro-tumorigenic elsewhere — adds nuance to any therapeutic ANGPTL4 inhibition strategy and underscores the importance of tissue-specific biology when designing ANGPTL4-targeting agents.

### 3. Diabetic retinopathy: Research advances in canonical cellular pathways and emerging mechanisms

**Wu S., Gao J.**  
*Experimental eye research* | 2026 Jul  
[PMID 41985866](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41985866/) | [DOI: 10.1016/j.exer.2026.111023](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.exer.2026.111023)

This comprehensive review by Wu and Gao surveys the molecular pathogenesis of diabetic retinopathy, covering oxidative stress, neuroinflammation, vascular dysfunction, and emerging intercellular communication mechanisms including exosomes. ANGPTL4 is highlighted as a key angiogenic mediator contributing to retinal neovascularization in proliferative DR. The authors discuss how ANGPTL4 upregulation under hypoxic conditions promotes pathological vessel growth, making it a candidate co-target alongside VEGF for next-generation anti-angiogenic therapies. The review also notes early data on RNA-based approaches to silence angiogenic drivers in the retina, relevant to the broader ocular RNA delivery field.

### 4. Baiting-enhanced extravasation of lipid nanoparticles for targeted co-delivery of decitabine and siTNF-α in AML

**Zhu Y., Li Q., Zhou Y., Wu D., Qin H., et al.**  
*Journal of controlled release : official journal of the Controlled Release Society* | 2026 Jun 10  
[PMID 41905408](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41905408/) | [DOI: 10.1016/j.jconrel.2026.114874](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jconrel.2026.114874)

Zhu et al. introduce "Bait and Extravasation" LNPs (BIS-LNPs) that co-deliver a hypoxia/ROS-responsive decitabine prodrug with TNF-α siRNA to bone marrow in acute myeloid leukemia. The platform uses a bone-marrow-homing peptide to facilitate extravasation into the niche and achieves synergistic antitumor activity in both cell lines and mouse models. While the target organ is bone marrow rather than adipose, the ionizable lipid formulation and siRNA delivery engineering principles — particularly the dual-payload and stimuli-responsive prodrug strategy — are directly translatable to metabolic tissue targeting. This paper is notable for demonstrating how LNP surface engineering can overcome physiological barriers to non-liver delivery.

### 5. Endogenous targeting lipid nanoparticles for systemic mRNA delivery to lung cancer tumors

**Goo Y., Grigoriev V., Korzun T., Salem A., Sharma K., et al.**  
*Journal of controlled release : official journal of the Controlled Release Society* | 2026 Jun 10  
[PMID 41903756](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41903756/) | [DOI: 10.1016/j.jconrel.2026.114870](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jconrel.2026.114870)

Goo et al. describe a DC-cholesterol/113-O12B ionizable lipid LNP that achieves preferential mRNA translation in lung cancer tumors following systemic dosing, leveraging endogenous protein corona formation for tumor selectivity. The 113-O12B ionizable lipid conferred selective activity relative to MC3-based controls. Critically, the authors characterize how lipid composition shifts the protein corona and downstream receptor-mediated uptake — a framework that directly informs adipose-targeting LNP design, where ApoE and lipolysis-mediated uptake pathways are exploited. Understanding organ-selective LNP biology through the lens of corona dynamics is increasingly central to the adipose delivery problem.

## What to Watch

The ANGPTL4 story is fragmenting productively across tissues: the gut-liver axis paper in *Nature Communications* is perhaps the most clinically actionable finding this week, suggesting that intestinal ANGPTL4 may be a better therapeutic node than hepatic or circulating ANGPTL4 for MASH — particularly if targeted RNA delivery to enterocytes can be achieved. Meanwhile, the OPSCC tumor-suppressor finding is a timely reminder that systemic ANGPTL4 inhibition carries tissue-context risks that tissue-tropic RNA modalities (GalNAc-conjugated ASOs, gut-targeted LNPs) could help mitigate. On the delivery side, the protein corona and extravasation engineering papers reflect an industry-wide push to expand RNA medicines beyond the liver without sacrificing selectivity. Watch for adipose-selective LNP data to emerge from groups combining ApoE corona manipulation with ANGPTL4 or APOC3 payloads in the coming months.
