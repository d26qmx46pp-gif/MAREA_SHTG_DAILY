## Papers

**Fibrillin-1 Inhibits Early Adipogenic Commitment Via αvβ3 Integrin Signaling**
Siddiqui I, Nelea V, Tiedemann K, et al. *Journal of Molecular Biology* (2026).
[PMID: 42303090](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42303090/) | [DOI: 10.1016/j.jmb.2026.169912](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jmb.2026.169912)

This mechanistic study shows that fibrillin-1, an extracellular matrix protein found in adipose tissue, acts as a brake on early fat-cell differentiation. Using recombinant fibrillin-1 fragments and both bone-marrow and fat-derived progenitor cells, the authors found that an active integrin-binding fragment blocked adipogenic commitment, while siRNA knockdown of integrin αvβ3 (but not αvβ5) reversed that block. The pathway runs through Fak-Src-Erk signaling, which restrains the master adipogenic transcription factors Cebpα and Pparγ. It's a useful mechanistic addition to the adipogenesis-checkpoint literature, though siRNA here is used purely as a lab tool for target validation, not a therapeutic delivery strategy.

**Chloride-Permeable Channels in White Adipocytes: Their Role in Adipogenesis and Membrane Potential**
Meng, Smith. *Journal of Molecular Endocrinology* (2026).
[PMID: 42554722](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42554722/) | [DOI: 10.1530/JME-25-0111](https://doi.org/10.1530/JME-25-0111)

Using transcriptomics, electrophysiology, and siRNA knockdown across rat, mouse, and 3T3-L1 adipocyte models, this group maps out which chloride-permeable channels (SWELL1, TTYH2/3, EAAT1) control membrane potential and differentiation in white fat cells. Knockdown of SWELL1 and TTYH2/3 specifically suppressed adipocyte differentiation, nominating these ion channels as potential obesity-intervention targets. Again, siRNA is a bench tool here rather than a delivery-platform story, but the target-identification angle is relevant to anyone tracking novel adipose-directed mechanisms.

**Magnetically Guided Apoptotic Mesenchymal Stem Cell-Derived Nanovesicles for the Modulation of Pathological Remodeling in Cardiac Injury**
Yoo, Kang, Park, et al. *Acta Biomaterialia* (2026).
[PMID: 42570782](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42570782/) | [DOI: 10.1016/j.actbio.2026.08.013](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.actbio.2026.08.013)

Not an adipose-targeting paper, but flagged for delivery-platform interest: this group built a magnetically guided nanovesicle carrier (apoptotic MSC-derived vesicles plus iron-oxide nanoparticles plus an injury-homing antibody) to deliver siRNA against AEBP1 (adipocyte enhancer-binding protein 1) to injured heart tissue, silencing a pro-fibrotic gene to blunt post-injury remodeling. The tissue target is cardiac, not adipose, but the four-component targeting architecture — antibody-guided homing plus magnetic steering plus a cell-derived vesicle shell — is a design pattern worth watching for anyone building tissue-tropic RNA delivery systems generally.
