## Papers

**ANGPTL4 Induces Aberrant Lymphatic-Like Remodeling in Proliferative Diabetic Retinopathy**
Li Z, Guan L, Mu T, et al. *Diabetes* (May 1, 2026). [PMID 41805865](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41805865/) | [DOI](https://doi.org/10.2337/db25-0445)

This study examined vitreous humor from patients with proliferative diabetic retinopathy (PDR) and found markedly elevated ANGPTL4 and VEGF-C, both unaffected by anti-VEGF treatment. In mouse models, both full-length ANGPTL4 and its C-terminal fragment drove pathological angiogenesis and lymphatic-like vessel remodeling in the diabetic retina—a previously under-described mechanism. Single-cell sequencing exposed ANGPTL4-driven immune dysregulation, including abnormal infiltration of CD4+ T cells and dendritic cells. Critically, ANGPTL4 knockdown in oxygen-induced retinopathy mice alleviated retinal hypoxia, neovascularization, and vascular leakage. These findings establish ANGPTL4 as a promising secondary therapeutic target for patients who are non-responsive to anti-VEGF regimens.

**The PPARβ/Delta-Induced Mesenchymal Stromal Cell Secretome Has Cytoprotective Effects via ANGPTL4 in a Pre-Clinical Model of Acute Lung Inflammation**
Tunstead C, Dunlop M, Ryan S, et al. *FASEB Journal* (Apr 30, 2026). [PMID 42007763](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42007763/) | [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1096/fj.202504659R)

Human bone marrow-derived mesenchymal stromal cells (hBM-MSCs) are candidates for ARDS therapy, but their efficacy depends heavily on the inflammatory microenvironment. This paper shows that pharmacological activation of PPARβ/δ—a receptor responsive to free fatty acids—drives hBM-MSCs to secrete high levels of ANGPTL4, which in turn enhances endothelial barrier integrity in lungs of LPS-injured mice. The cytoprotective effect was amplified when MSCs were pre-conditioned with actual ARDS patient serum, suggesting a personalized priming strategy. This work frames ANGPTL4 as a key effector paracrine factor downstream of lipid-sensing pathways in stem cell therapy, opening a route to optimize MSC secretomes without genetic modification.

**ESM1 Drives Cancer Angiogenesis and Bevacizumab Resistance via Trioleate Synthesis**
Chen X, Wan J, Jiang Z, et al. *Neoplasia* (May 2026). [PMID 41864037](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41864037/) | [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neo.2026.101298)

Using multi-omics in hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) cell lines and patient-derived xenografts, this group uncovered a positive feedback loop between the endothelial factor ESM1 and ANGPTL4 that stabilizes fatty acid synthase (FASN) and drives synthesis of the lipid trioleate. Trioleate in turn activates NF-κB/IL-17 in tumor cells and upregulates CD99 in endothelial cells, promoting angiogenesis and anti-VEGF resistance. ESM1/ANGPTL4 dual knockdown suppressed tumor growth in vivo, an effect rescued by exogenous trioleate. Elevated ESM1/ANGPTL4 expression correlated with bevacizumab resistance and poor outcomes in clinical HCC samples. The ESM1–ANGPTL4–FASN–trioleate axis represents a therapeutically actionable metabolic vulnerability in anti-angiogenic-resistant liver cancer.

**Synergistic Inhibition of Macular Vascular Permeability in Diabetic Edema: Ginsenoside Rg3 Enhances Ranibizumab Efficacy by Targeting ANGPTL4 and VEGF via Neuropilin/RhoA Signaling**
Yu J, Li X, Liu Y, et al. *Journal of Diabetes Investigation* (May 2026). [PMID 41830319](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41830319/) | [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1111/jdi.70285)

Transcriptomic analysis of diabetic macular edema (DME) patients revealed co-upregulation of VEGF, ANGPTL4, and NRP1/RhoA—a pathway controlling vascular permeability. The authors show that ginsenoside Rg3, a bioactive compound from ginseng, reduces ANGPTL4 expression in human retinal microvascular endothelial cells, and that combining it with the anti-VEGF antibody ranibizumab produces synergistic reduction of retinal edema and vascular leakage in diabetic rats. The dual suppression of the NRP/RhoA axis is proposed as the key mechanism. While the compound is natural-product-derived and far from a clinical drug, the study highlights that ANGPTL4 and VEGF are co-regulators in DME, and that dual targeting outperforms single-pathway blockade.

**The Multifaceted Role of Angiopoietin-Like Proteins in Coronary Heart Disease: From Molecular Mechanisms to Clinical Translation**
Tan J, Gu J. *Journal of Cardiovascular Pharmacology* (May 1, 2026). [PMID 41770616](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41770616/) | [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1097/FJC.0000000000001815)

This review synthesizes current knowledge on the ANGPTL family in coronary heart disease, covering their roles in lipoprotein lipase inhibition, dyslipidemia, and vascular inflammation. Notably, it summarizes clinical-stage ANGPTL-targeted therapies, including monoclonal antibodies and siRNA approaches, that have demonstrated favorable safety and lipid-lowering efficacy in human trials. The piece serves as a useful primer on how ANGPTL inhibition is moving from bench to bedside in cardiovascular indications. For the RNA delivery community, the mention of siRNA therapeutics already in trials reinforces ANGPTL proteins as validated nucleic acid therapy targets.

## What to Watch

The concentration of ANGPTL4 papers in diabetic microvascular disease—retinopathy, nephropathy, and macular edema—signals an emerging consensus that ANGPTL4 is not just a lipid metabolism regulator but a central driver of vascular dysfunction across diabetic end-organ complications. Two papers this week independently confirm that anti-VEGF resistance in the diabetic eye is linked to persistent ANGPTL4 elevation, making ANGPTL4 knockdown (by any modality, including RNA therapeutics) a logical next-generation strategy. On the RNA delivery side, this week was quiet; watch for LNP and GalNAc conjugate papers targeting metabolic tissues as the field continues to move beyond liver-tropic formulations. The FASEB paper on PPARβ/δ and ANGPTL4 in MSC secretomes is a sleeper worth tracking—it suggests the lipid-sensing/ANGPTL4 axis may be druggable to enhance cell therapies, which could intersect with RNA-based cell conditioning strategies down the road.
