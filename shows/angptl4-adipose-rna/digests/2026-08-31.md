# ANGPTL4 & Adipose RNA — Monday August 31

**Bottom line:** A thin week for new ANGPTL4-specific literature, but one strong hit on the RNA-delivery-to-metabolic-tissue side: a circular RNA/LNP formulation delivering GDF15 shows sustained weight loss and adipose thermogenic activation in obese mice.

## Papers

**Preclinical efficacy of engineered CircRNA-GDF15@LNP for sustained weight loss and metabolic regulation in diet-induced obese mice**
Wu, Wei, Ge, et al. — *Journal of Controlled Release* (Sep 10, 2026)
[PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42323019/) | [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jconrel.2026.115117)

The authors built a long-acting circular RNA construct encoding a mutant form of GDF15 and packaged it in an optimized lipid nanoparticle (CircGDF15@LNP). Circular RNA is inherently more stable than linear mRNA, so a single formulation can drive weeks of continuous protein expression instead of the days typical of standard mRNA-LNP. In diet-induced obese mice, biweekly IV dosing over four weeks produced sustained GDF15 overexpression, which activated PPARα/γ signaling and UCP1 expression in liver and adipose tissue — the pathway responsible for adaptive thermogenesis and lipid handling. The result was durable weight loss and improved metabolic parameters without the dosing frequency problems that plague earlier GDF15 biologics. While this isn't an ANGPTL4-targeted therapy, it's a directly relevant proof-of-concept for using engineered RNA/LNP payloads to reprogram adipose and hepatic lipid metabolism — the same delivery logic that would apply to an ANGPTL4-lowering siRNA or ASO aimed at adipose tissue.

## What to watch

The CircGDF15@LNP result is a useful data point for the broader adipose-RNA-therapeutics thesis even though it targets GDF15 rather than ANGPTL4: it demonstrates that LNP-delivered engineered RNA can durably activate the PPAR/UCP1 thermogenic axis in adipose tissue from a systemic (IV) dose, without requiring adipose-specific targeting ligands. Worth tracking whether follow-up work from this group or competitors pairs similar circRNA/LNP stability improvements with an ANGPTL4-lowering payload, and whether GalNAc- or adipocyte-targeted conjugate approaches emerge as alternatives to systemic LNP dosing for tissue-restricted ANGPTL4 knockdown. On the biology side, this was a quiet week for new ANGPTL4 mechanism papers; worth revisiting the hypoxia/tumor-macrophage ANGPTL4 thread and the renal ANGPTL4-fibrosis thread from recent weeks once follow-up data appears.
