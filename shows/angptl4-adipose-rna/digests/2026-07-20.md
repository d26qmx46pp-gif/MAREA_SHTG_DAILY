# ANGPTL4 & Adipose RNA — Monday July 20

**Bottom line:** No new adipose-targeted RNA delivery papers turned up this week, but three fresh papers deepen the case for ANGPTL4 as a disease-driving signal outside classic lipid metabolism — in sleep-linked metabolic risk, cholangiocarcinoma metastasis, and colorectal cancer's tumor microenvironment.

## Papers

**Crosstalk between sleep disorders and adipokine secretory profiles: novel mechanisms for metabolic disease risk**
Shi P, Liu X, Li D, Zhou X, Chang X, Xu Z, Zhao Y. *Annals of Medicine.* [PMID 42427296](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42427296/) | [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1161/CIR.0000000000001078)
This review lays out the bidirectional relationship between poor sleep and metabolic disease, with ANGPTL4 named alongside leptin, adiponectin, and IL-6 as a key adipokine mediator. The authors highlight that obstructive sleep apnea's intermittent hypoxia potently upregulates ANGPTL4 (and IL-6), feeding into insulin resistance, dyslipidemia, and chronic inflammation. It's a useful reminder that ANGPTL4 sits downstream of hypoxia signaling broadly, not just in tumors — a mechanistic thread worth tracking across our other papers this week. No new data, but a clean synthesis for background/context slides.

**Hypoxia drives ANGPTL4 expression in tumor-associated macrophages and promotes intrahepatic cholangiocarcinoma intrahepatic metastasis**
Lin Z, Hua H, Huang Y, Li X, Liang Y, Yi T, Jin H, Wu J, Wan Y, Li G. *Cancer Letters.* [PMID 42214805](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42214805/) | [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.canlet.2026.218614)
Using single-cell RNA-seq across 15 tumor samples plus a 255-patient public cohort, the authors show hypoxic tumor-associated macrophages upregulate ANGPTL4 via the transcription factor CEBPB. Macrophage-derived ANGPTL4 then engages integrin α5β1 on cancer cells, activating a FAK/Src/GSK3β/β-catenin cascade that drives epithelial-mesenchymal transition and metastasis. ANGPTL4 blockade attenuated invasion and metastasis in their models, and elevated serum/tissue ANGPTL4 tracked with tumor burden. This is the strongest mechanistic paper of the week — it positions ANGPTL4 as both a candidate serum biomarker and a druggable node in the tumor-macrophage crosstalk axis, adding to the growing non-metabolic disease case for ANGPTL4-targeted therapeutics.

**ST3GAL1 mediates sialylation to promote adhesion of Fusobacterium nucleatum to colorectal cancer cells**
Xiao L, Gong T, Luo W, Jiang Y, Peng X, Zhou X, Cheng L, Zheng X. *Biochimica et Biophysica Acta - Molecular Basis of Disease.* [PMID 42342164](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42342164/) | [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.bbadis.2026.168332)
Primarily a paper about bacterial adhesion mechanics, but relevant here: the authors find that F. nucleatum colonization in colorectal cancer tissue drives H3K27 acetylation and upregulates ANGPTL4 expression as part of the bacterium's cancer-promoting program. This is a smaller mention but adds another tumor type (colorectal, alongside cholangiocarcinoma above) where microbiome-driven epigenetic changes converge on ANGPTL4 induction — worth flagging as a pattern rather than a standalone finding.

## What to watch

This was a thin week for the adipose-targeted RNA delivery side specifically — nothing new on siRNA/ASO/LNP/GalNAc platforms targeting adipose tissue turned up in PubMed's last 8 days. The ANGPTL4 biology side, though, keeps accumulating evidence that hypoxia is a common upstream trigger across very different tissue contexts (sleep-apnea-linked metabolic disease, cholangiocarcinoma, and now colorectal cancer via the microbiome). The Cancer Letters paper on macrophage-derived ANGPTL4 in cholangiocarcinoma metastasis is the one worth reading in full — it's the most mechanistically complete story and reinforces integrin α5β1 engagement as a potential intervention point beyond direct ANGPTL4 knockdown. Worth watching whether next week brings any delivery-platform news to pair with this steady drumbeat of biology.
