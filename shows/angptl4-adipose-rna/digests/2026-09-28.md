# ANGPTL4 & Adipose RNA — Monday September 28

**Bottom line:** A new PNAS paper introduces an adipose-directed antisense oligonucleotide that preserves lean mass and boosts fat-selective weight loss alongside semaglutide, while a large Mendelian-randomization study adds human genetic support for ANGPTL4 inhibition as a kidney-protective strategy.

## Papers

**An RNA thermogenic therapy to preserve lean mass and enhance metabolic health during GLP-1 weight loss**
Thorne AK, Waldeck NJ, Rose JP, et al. — *Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences (PNAS)*, 2026.
[PubMed: 42789307](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42789307/) | [DOI: 10.1073/pnas.2618845123](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2618845123)

GLP-1 receptor agonists drive weight loss but often cost patients lean mass and metabolic rate, with rapid fat regain after stopping treatment. This Northwestern group built an adipose-directed antisense oligonucleotide (ASO) against ZFP423, a transcriptional repressor that normally keeps white fat from turning into calorie-burning beige fat. Weekly dosing in mice induced browning of white adipose tissue, raised body temperature and oxygen consumption, and improved glucose handling, insulin sensitivity, and liver fat. Layered on top of semaglutide, the ASO produced greater fat loss than either treatment alone while protecting lean mass — a direct proof of concept for RNA-based, adipose-tropic therapeutics as a companion to incretin drugs rather than a competitor to them.

**The Role of Angiopoietin-Like Proteins 3 and 4 and Their Target Lipases in CKD: A Drug-Target Mendelian Randomization Study**
Yang G, Zhang DY, Fischman CJ, Cuchel M, Susztak K, Rader DJ — *American Journal of Kidney Diseases*, 2026.
[PubMed: 42772348](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42772348/) | [DOI: 10.1053/j.ajkd.2026.06.015](https://doi.org/10.1053/j.ajkd.2026.06.015)

Using genetic variants as proxies for lifelong drug exposure across nearly 2.4 million people (All of Us, Penn Medicine Biobank, and public GWAS), this Penn/Rader-lab analysis found that genetically lower ANGPTL4 activity tracked with meaningfully lower chronic kidney disease risk and higher eGFR, on par with the protective signal seen for ANGPTL3. Single-cell RNA-seq of kidney tissue showed ANGPTL3, ANGPTL4, and LPL are differentially expressed between CKD patients and controls across specific kidney cell types, giving the genetic association a plausible tissue-level anchor. The authors frame ANGPTL4 (and its lipase targets) as a credible therapeutic target for CKD prevention, independent of its established lipid-lowering rationale — relevant to any ANGPTL4 program watching for renal safety or renal-benefit signals.

**Nanogenetics in diabetes: converging nanoscale platforms and gene modulation for metabolic reprogramming** *(review — included for adjacent delivery-platform context)*
Tiwari R, Jahnavi P, Singh G, et al. — *Journal of Biomaterials Science, Polymer Edition*, 2026.
[PubMed: 41294211](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41294211/) | [DOI: 10.1080/09205063.2025.2591372](https://doi.org/10.1080/09205063.2025.2591372)

A broad review mapping nanoscale delivery platforms (lipid, polymeric, inorganic, exosomal, stimuli-responsive) against gene-modulation modalities (RNAi, CRISPR, epigenome editing) for diabetes, with adipose tissue named alongside β-cells, hepatocytes, and immune-metabolic interfaces as a target compartment. It's not ANGPTL4- or adipose-specific, but it's a useful survey of where tissue-tropic RNA/gene delivery platforms are heading for metabolic disease more broadly.

## What to watch

The PNAS ZFP423 ASO paper is the more mechanistically novel story this week: it's the clearest recent demonstration that an adipose-tropic RNA therapeutic can be layered onto an incretin regimen to fix the lean-mass problem everyone's been discussing since GLP-1 drugs went mainstream — worth tracking whether Northwestern or a licensee moves this toward IND-enabling work. On the ANGPTL4 side, the CKD Mendelian-randomization data is the kind of large-scale human genetic evidence that de-risks a target rather than proving a drug works; it's a good one to keep in a slide about ANGPTL4's therapeutic breadth beyond triglycerides. Both papers reinforce the same throughline for this show: adipose- and lipase-axis biology keeps surfacing new indications, and RNA-based modalities keep showing up as the delivery mechanism of choice.
