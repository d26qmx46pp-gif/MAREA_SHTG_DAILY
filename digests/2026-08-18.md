## Part A — SHTG / lipid science (PubMed)

**Novel triglyceride-lowering therapies to reduce the risk of acute pancreatitis in hypertriglyceridaemia: a protocol for systematic review and meta-analysis**
Wu Yongzi, Liu Shiyu, Luo Wenjuan, et al. *BMJ Open*. [PMID 42601105](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42601105/) | [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1136/bmjopen-2026-120485)
This is a registered protocol, not results yet, but it's a useful marker of where the field is heading: the group plans a formal meta-analysis pooling RCTs of APOC3, ANGPTL3, and ANGPTL4 inhibitors specifically for their effect on acute pancreatitis incidence in HTG (TG ≥500 mg/dL), not just triglyceride reduction as a surrogate. Secondary outcomes include LDL-C, HDL-C, and non-HDL-C changes and safety. Search covers PubMed, Web of Science, Embase, and Cochrane through May 2026, with GRADE certainty-of-evidence ratings. Worth flagging now so you can watch for the completed analysis, since AP incidence (rather than TG percent change) is the outcome regulators and payers actually care about.

**Injectable Lipid-Lowering Therapies in Advanced Chronic Kidney Disease: Efficacy, Safety and the Dialysis Paradox**
Damianaki Aikaterini, Stambolliu Emelina, Iliakis Panagiotis, et al. *Cardiovascular Drugs and Therapy*. [PMID 42603236](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42603236/) | [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10557-026-07934-y)
A review specifically on injectable lipid-lowering agents — PCSK9 antibodies, inclisiran, and the emerging ANGPTL3/APOC3/Lp(a)-targeted RNAi and ASO class — in advanced CKD and dialysis patients. The core argument: pharmacokinetics for these agents look largely preserved across CKD stages, and they offer mechanisms of benefit beyond LDL lowering that could matter in a population where the "dialysis paradox" (lower cholesterol tracking with worse outcomes, likely driven by malnutrition/inflammation) muddies the usual lipid-outcome relationship. Trial data in advanced CKD/dialysis patients remains thin, which the authors flag as the key evidence gap — relevant if you're thinking about label expansion or subgroup analyses for ANGPTL3/APOC3 programs into CKD populations.

**Chronic High-Fat Diet Feeding Reduces the Rate of Postprandial Triglyceride Absorption Independent of Intestinal ANGPTL4**
Shetty Shwetha K, Sylvers-Davie Kelli L, Davies Brandon S J. *Journal of Lipid Research*. [PMID 42600744](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42600744/) | [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jlr.2026.101124)
A clean negative-result mechanistic study out of the Davies lab. The hypothesis was that high-fat-diet-induced intestinal ANGPTL4 inhibits pancreatic lipase and slows postprandial TG absorption. Using intestinal epithelial-specific ANGPTL4 knockout mice, they confirmed that chronic high-fat feeding does reduce the rate of postprandial TG absorption — but the effect was identical in knockouts and wild-type mice, meaning intestinal ANGPTL4 isn't the mediator. Useful for anyone modeling ANGPTL4 biology: it narrows where the diet-adaptation signal is actually coming from and rules out one candidate mechanism.

**Integrating 2.5D multi-modal CT imaging with serum triglycerides for early risk stratification in hypertriglyceridemia-induced acute pancreatitis: A multicenter study**
Guo Qingping, Chen Wei, Qin Kexiang, et al. *European Journal of Radiology*. [PMID 42322722](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42322722/) | [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ejrad.2026.113028)
A multicenter (1,518-patient) retrospective study building a deep-learning CT + triglyceride model to predict which hypertriglyceridemia-induced acute pancreatitis patients will progress to severe disease. The imaging-plus-TG fusion model hit AUCs of 0.92–0.97 across training and two external validation cohorts, and adding TG level substantially boosted sensitivity for severe AP without hurting specificity. Not a drug study, but a plausible clinical-decision tool for triaging SHTG-AP patients early — the kind of thing that could get baked into ED protocols alongside triglyceride-lowering therapy.

## Part B — Industry news (SHTG / Arrowhead / Alnylam / Ionis)

Quiet cycle within the strict 5-day window — no new (Aug 13–18) stories from the allowed trade press met the bar. The one story worth carrying forward is older than the window but still the dominant open thread, so it's flagged as context rather than news:

***Context piece, July 22, 2026 — not new this cycle:*** **Arrowhead's plozasiran posts positive Phase 3 data in severe hypertriglyceridemia**
Kevin Dunleavy, *Fierce Pharma*, July 22, 2026. [Link](https://www.fiercepharma.com/pharma/look-out-ionis-here-comes-rival-arrowhead-competitive-hypertriglyceridemia-data)
Arrowhead reported topline results from the Phase 3 SHASTA-3 and SHASTA-4 trials of plozasiran (Redemplo) in roughly 750 sHTG patients, hitting all primary and secondary endpoints: median triglyceride reductions of 79% (SHASTA-3) and 81% (SHASTA-4) at month 12 versus 27% for placebo, and a statistically significant 78% reduction in acute pancreatitis events versus placebo — with a 100% reduction in the highest-risk subgroup (TG >880 mg/dL plus prior AP history). Arrowhead plans to file for FDA approval in sHTG by year-end 2026, competing directly with Ionis's Tryngolza (olezarsen), which won FDA approval in the same indication in late June. Full data will be presented as an ESC Congress Hot Line Late-Breaker on August 30, 2026.
*Why it matters for SHTG / lipid programs: this sets up a head-to-head APOC3-inhibitor rivalry (siRNA vs. ASO, quarterly vs. monthly dosing) in a population roughly 1,000x larger than FCS, and the August 30 ESC presentation is the next real catalyst to watch.*

## What to watch

The science and industry threads point the same direction: mechanistic work on ANGPTL3/4 and APOC3 is still filling in exactly why these targets lower triglycerides and pancreatitis risk, while on the clinical/commercial side plozasiran's Phase 3 win keeps the pressure on Ionis's early sHTG lead. The next real inflection points are the full SHASTA-3/4 data at ESC on August 30 and whatever completed meta-analysis eventually comes out of the BMJ Open protocol above — both worth flagging for the next digest cycle.
