**Heterozygosity for pathogenic variants in familial chylomicronemia syndrome genes: from carrier state to complex trait**

Robert A. Hegele | *Current Opinion in Lipidology* | June 2026 | [PMID 42308165](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42308165/) | DOI: [10.1097/MOL.0000000000001048](https://doi.org/10.1097/MOL.0000000000001048)

Hegele synthesizes 18 months of data on what it means to carry a single pathogenic variant in any of the five canonical FCS genes — *LPL*, *GPIHBP1*, *APOA5*, *APOC2*, or *LMF1*. The key finding is that triglyceride phenotype among heterozygotes varies enormously over time in the same individual, ranging from completely normal to severely elevated, driven by a "saturation of compromised lipolytic reserve" rather than simple haploinsufficiency. Heterozygosity contributes to 15–25% of multifactorial chylomicronemia syndrome (MCS) cases, and common intermediate-effect alleles (e.g., *LPL* p.D36N, *APOA5* p.S19W) behave differently from rare loss-of-function variants and should not be conflated with them. The clinical upshot is practical: cascade screening and counseling need to distinguish rare LOF from common risk alleles, and heterozygote status can help identify which patients may benefit from emerging APOC3-targeted therapies. This review is essential reading for anyone thinking about how to deploy olezarsen or plozasiran beyond genetically confirmed FCS.

**A Case of Familial Chylomicronemia Syndrome Caused by a Novel Homozygous *GPIHBP1* Mutation Successfully Treated with the Selective PPARα Modulator Pemafibrate**

Otsuki T, Chung Y, Kawachi Y, et al. | *Journal of Atherosclerosis and Thrombosis* | June 2026 | [PMID 42309774](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42309774/) | DOI: [10.5551/jat.66218](https://doi.org/10.5551/jat.66218)

A 47-year-old woman with childhood-onset FCS caused by a novel homozygous *GPIHBP1* variant presented with acute pancreatitis and extreme hypertriglyceridemia — her triglycerides had exceeded 6,000 mg/dL during pregnancy. GPIHBP1 deficiency is exceedingly rare; the protein anchors LPL to capillary endothelium and is essential for triglyceride hydrolysis. Treatment with pemafibrate, a selective PPARα modulator that upregulates LPL transcription and promotes VLDL clearance, produced meaningful TG reduction and avoidance of further pancreatitis events. This case adds to a sparse but growing literature on GPIHBP1-deficient FCS and highlights pemafibrate as a pragmatic option where RNA-targeting therapies are inaccessible. It also illustrates that clinical FCS includes genetic variants well beyond *LPL* itself — a point that matters for diagnosis and for interpreting trial eligibility criteria.

**Severe Hypertriglyceridemia With Lipemia-Induced Analytical Interference: Diagnostic Challenges in a Chylomicronemia Phenotype**

Kaur A, Bali R, Batta A, et al. | *JACC Case Reports* | June 2026 | [PMID 42301177](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42301177/) | DOI: [10.1016/j.jaccas.2026.108821](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jaccas.2026.108821)

A 51-year-old man with diabetes, hypertension, and a recent history of hypertriglyceridemia-induced normoenzymatic pancreatitis presented with chest pain and ischemic EKG changes; his triglycerides were 4,038 mg/dL at admission. The grossly lipemic serum caused a prothrombin time to be reported as "infinite" — no clot detected — mimicking a severe coagulopathy, which was disproven by a mixing study. Coronary angiography confirmed obstructive atherosclerotic disease, and the combination of severely elevated TG with a relatively preserved apolipoprotein B favored a type IV–V chylomicronemia spectrum. After TG-lowering therapy enabled the procedure, successful PCI was performed. The case is a crisp reminder of two under-recognized points: multifactorial chylomicronemia can coexist with premature ASCVD (not just pancreatitis), and lipemia-induced laboratory artifacts — spurious INR, spurious electrolytes, pseudohyponatremia — can misdirect management before anyone realizes the sample is invalid.

**Comparative Efficacy of Insulin and Alternative Therapies for Hypertriglyceridemia-Associated Acute Pancreatitis: A Systematic Review and Network Meta-Analysis**

Liang AC, Castillo YC, Murrieta BLM, et al. | *Digestive Diseases and Sciences* | June 2026 | [PMID 42301369](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42301369/) | DOI: [10.1007/s10620-026-10052-2](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10620-026-10052-2)

This network meta-analysis compared insulin, therapeutic plasma exchange (TPE), heparin, hemofiltration, and conservative management as acute TG-lowering strategies in HTGAP. Across predominantly observational evidence, no intervention demonstrated statistically significant superiority over insulin-based therapy for mortality, organ failure, or length of hospital stay — despite some interventions producing faster biochemical TG reduction. TPE showed the greatest speed of TG lowering, but this biochemical advantage did not translate into measurable clinical benefit in the pooled analysis. The findings reinforce insulin as the pragmatic first-line intervention and should temper enthusiasm for reflexive TPE in the absence of rapid clinical deterioration. The authors acknowledge that trial quality limits firm conclusions, and there is need for prospective RCTs with clinical endpoints rather than TG reduction as the primary outcome.

**SPP2 as an HNF4A Target Gene Regulating Triglyceride Homeostasis via LPL Activation**

Wen Y, Liu X, Wang M, Li C | *Biochemical and Biophysical Research Communications* | June 2026 | [PMID 42030881](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42030881/) | DOI: [10.1016/j.bbrc.2026.153778](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.bbrc.2026.153778)

Secreted phosphoprotein 2 (SPP2/SPP24), a cystatin-superfamily secreted protein previously known mainly in bone metabolism, turns out to be a hepatic fasting-regulated LPL activator. When fasting triggers free fatty acid mobilization, HNF4α binds the SPP2 promoter, driving SPP2 secretion; SPP2 then binds LPL directly and modulates its cofactor expression, increasing VLDL hydrolysis and lowering serum TG. AAV8-mediated SPP2 overexpression selectively reduced TG without affecting other metabolic parameters in mice. The HNF4A→SPP2→LPL axis is an entirely new node in TG homeostasis — one that sits upstream of ANGPTL3/4 and APOC3 regulation and could theoretically be pharmacologically leveraged. It also reinforces the idea that LPL activity is governed by a multifactorial regulatory network that extends well beyond the canonical inhibitory axes.

**Ionis olezarsen sHTG FDA decision is 11 days away — at $40K/year, the commercial race is on**

*BioPharma Dive* (tracker, last updated March 30, 2026); *Fierce Pharma* (price cut article, March 2026) | Context piece: coverage dates January–March 2026, but the PDUFA deadline is live on June 30, 2026 | [BioPharma Dive tracker](https://www.biopharmadive.com/news/fda-approval-decisions-watch-2026/808684/)

The FDA has until June 30 to rule on Ionis' supplemental NDA for Tryngolza (olezarsen) in severe hypertriglyceridemia — the first time the company has sought approval for a non-rare disease indication with a wholly-owned drug. In late March, Ionis moved preemptively on price, slashing the WAC from $595,000 to $40,000 per patient per year — a 93% reduction — to undercut Arrowhead's $45,000 price for REDEMPLO (plozasiran) and signal aggressive formulary positioning. In the two pivotal phase 3 studies, olezarsen reduced TG by up to 73% and 68% and cut acute pancreatitis events by 85% at one year. Approval would give Ionis access to the multifactorial sHTG population, which is several orders of magnitude larger than FCS. Analysts have estimated peak sHTG sales potential above $3 billion. **Why it matters for SHTG/lipid programs:** The June 30 PDUFA date is the most consequential near-term regulatory event in the SHTG space; approval would validate APOC3 inhibition as standard-of-care beyond rare FCS and ignite head-to-head commercial competition between olezarsen and plozasiran.

**Arrowhead REDEMPLO launch hitting stride; first dual-gene APOC3+PCSK9 RNAi molecule enters Phase 1**

*Arrowhead 8-K / Q2 FY2026 earnings*, May 7, 2026 | Context piece (outside 5-day window) | [SEC filing](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0000879407/000087940726000045/arwr-202605078kex991.htm)

Arrowhead's May earnings report revealed REDEMPLO now has more than 400 prescriptions received or in process — a 40% jump over the prior four weeks — with roughly 180 patients having received at least one injection. About 85% were therapy-naive to the APOC3 class, suggesting new-patient identification rather than mere switching. The company updated REDEMPLO's WAC to $45,000/year. Global regulatory momentum is strong: positive CHMP opinion in Europe, approvals in Australia, Canada, and China (the latter via Sanofi). On the pipeline side, Arrowhead dosed the first subjects in ARO-DIMER-PA, a single-molecule RNAi therapeutic targeting both *PCSK9* and *APOC3* simultaneously — the first dual-gene siRNA to enter clinical testing. The company also licensed ARO-PNPLA3 to Madrigal for $25 million upfront and up to $975 million in milestones. **Why it matters:** REDEMPLO's launch trajectory and the ARO-DIMER-PA Phase 1 underscore that plozasiran is maturing from a single-indication FCS drug toward a broad cardiometabolic franchise; the PCSK9+APOC3 co-targeting concept, if it works, could be a step-change for patients with both elevated TG and elevated LDL.

**What to watch**

The single most important event in this space over the next two weeks is the June 30 FDA decision on olezarsen for sHTG. An approval would immediately reframe the competitive landscape: Ionis enters the large-pool sHTG market with a drug priced to win formulary position, while Arrowhead's SHASTA-3/SHASTA-4 phase 3 data — primary completion dates were June 2026 — remain unreported. If those data read out concurrently or shortly after an olezarsen approval, the market will have its first real head-to-head data context, even if an indirect comparison. On the science side, the Hegele review on FCS-gene heterozygosity is clinically actionable today: it provides a framework for counseling and for determining which heterozygous patients warrant APOC3-targeted therapy — a question that will become urgent at scale if olezarsen is approved for the broader sHTG population.
