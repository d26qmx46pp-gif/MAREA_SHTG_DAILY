# SHTG / ANGPTL / APOC3 / LPL — Daily Digest — Tuesday May 26

**Bottom line:** A landmark Circulation imaging trial finds that olezarsen's dramatic triglyceride lowering does not translate into coronary plaque regression at 12 months—raising fundamental questions about TG-lowering as an atherosclerosis therapy just as the 2026 ACC/AHA dyslipidemia guideline reframes how we should think about hypertriglyceridemia, and with Ionis's PDUFA date for the SHTG indication just five weeks away.

## Part A — SHTG / lipid science (PubMed)

*Three top-tier papers this cycle, all published May 19, 2026.*

---

**Effect of APOC3 Inhibition With Olezarsen on Coronary Atherosclerosis: Essence-TIMI 73b Imaging Study**

Marston NA, Bergmark BA, Prohaska TA, et al. *Circulation.* May 19, 2026. PMID: [41910513](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41910513/) | DOI: [10.1161/CIRCULATIONAHA.126.080012](https://doi.org/10.1161/CIRCULATIONAHA.126.080012)

This is the most consequential paper of the cycle. The Essence-TIMI 73b imaging substudy enrolled 468 patients (349 olezarsen, 119 placebo) with largely moderate hypertriglyceridemia (median baseline TG ~249 mg/dL) and high cardiovascular risk. Olezarsen delivered robust lipid lowering: at 6 months, triglycerides fell 63.9%, remnant cholesterol fell 71.9%, and apoB dropped 16% relative to placebo, with no change in LDL-C. Despite these effects, the primary endpoint—percent change in noncalcified coronary plaque volume by CCTA at 12 months—showed no significant difference between groups (placebo-adjusted LS mean difference +2.98%, 95% CI −3.4 to +9.3, p = 0.36). Secondary endpoints for low-attenuation, calcified, and total plaque volumes were similarly neutral.

These results are sobering. They do not invalidate the APOC3/TG biology—olezarsen still robustly cuts cardiovascular events in other endpoints, and plaque imaging trials often lack power to detect modest changes over one year—but they challenge the hypothesis that TG-lowering per se drives plaque regression and raise the bar for mechanistic interpretations of the APOC3 pathway in atherosclerosis. The data add important nuance heading into Tryngolza's FDA PDUFA review for the broader SHTG indication.

---

**Triglycerides and Residual Risk, Where Do They Fit Now?**

Nordestgaard BG. *J Am Coll Cardiol.* May 19, 2026. PMID: [42153695](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42153695/) | DOI: [10.1016/j.jacc.2026.02.5133](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jacc.2026.02.5133)

An editorial from Børge Nordestgaard—one of the leading voices in TG residual risk epidemiology—published concurrently with the 2026 ACC/AHA dyslipidemia guideline. No abstract is available, but the title directly addresses the unresolved tension in the field: even as drugs that dramatically lower triglycerides reach approval, the question of where TG lowering fits in a residual-risk framework remains contested. Given Nordestgaard's track record, this is likely a measured, data-grounded synthesis of where the epidemiological and mechanistic evidence stands post-olezarsen and post-evinacumab. This editorial is a must-read for anyone trying to contextualize the APOC3/ANGPTL biology within current cardiovascular risk management.

**2026 ACC/AHA/AACVPR/ABC/ACPM/ADA/AGS/APhA/ASPC/NLA/PCNA Guideline on the Management of Dyslipidemia**

Blumenthal RS, Morris PB, Gaudino M, et al. *J Am Coll Cardiol.* May 19, 2026. PMID: [41824590](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41824590/) | DOI: [10.1016/j.jacc.2025.11.016](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jacc.2025.11.016)

The first major update to U.S. cholesterol management guidelines since 2018. This guideline retires the 2018 ACC/AHA/AACVPR/AAPA/ABC/ACPM/ADA/AGS/APhA/ASPC/NLA/PCNA Guideline on the Management of Blood Cholesterol and is based on a comprehensive literature search through December 2024. Critically for this audience, the document explicitly addresses hypertriglyceridemia and elevated lipoprotein(a) alongside LDL-C, representing an expansion of the guideline's scope beyond LDL-centric thinking. Full text will be required to assess how prominently APOC3 inhibitors and ANGPTL3 inhibitors appear, how the guideline handles the evidence for TG-lowering in cardiovascular risk reduction, and whether it codifies thresholds for treatment of SHTG beyond the traditional fibrate/omega-3 paradigm. The timing—one month before Tryngolza's PDUFA date for SHTG—is significant: guideline endorsement of TG-directed therapy could meaningfully shape prescribing behavior and payer coverage.

**Ionis Tryngolza FDA PDUFA date for SHTG: June 30, 2026**

*BioPharma Dive — context piece, updated March 30, 2026*
Source: [https://www.biopharmadive.com/news/fda-approval-decisions-watch-2026/808684/](https://www.biopharmadive.com/news/fda-approval-decisions-watch-2026/808684/)

The FDA has a June 30, 2026 PDUFA date to decide on Ionis's supplemental NDA for Tryngolza (olezarsen) in severe hypertriglyceridemia. The application is on priority review and was accepted after the Core and Core2 Phase 3 trials showed TG reductions up to 73% and an 85% reduction in acute pancreatitis vs. placebo. In late March 2026, Ionis revealed a planned annual list price of $40,000/year for the SHTG indication—a steep drop from the $595,000/year FCS price and well below Arrowhead's Redemplo at ~$60,000/year. Analysts at Leerink Partners described this as a pricing "sweet spot" that would allow Ionis to negotiate aggressively with payers. Ionis has raised its peak net sales guidance for Tryngolza in SHTG to over $3 billion.

**Why it matters for SHTG / lipid programs:** This week's negative coronary plaque result in Essence-TIMI 73b, published just five weeks before this PDUFA date, adds scientific complexity to the regulatory discussion—though the FDA's review will focus primarily on TG lowering and pancreatitis prevention, not atherosclerosis regression.

**Arrowhead Redemplo (plozasiran) Phase 3 SHTG data expected mid-2026**

*BioPharma Dive — context piece, updated March 30, 2026*
Source: [https://www.biopharmadive.com/news/fda-approval-decisions-watch-2026/808684/](https://www.biopharmadive.com/news/fda-approval-decisions-watch-2026/808684/)

Arrowhead's Redemplo (plozasiran, ARO-APOC3) received FDA approval for FCS in November 2025. Phase 3 SHTG trials are expected to complete primary analysis around mid-2026, with a regulatory filing to follow shortly if data are positive. Arrowhead has been pricing Redemplo at roughly $60,000/year in FCS—structured intentionally to remain consistent if approved in SHTG, where market volumes are much larger. Ionis has reported that Tryngolza's commercial momentum remains "robust" with no disruption from Redemplo's FCS launch. Sanofi has separately penned a deal for Arrowhead's metabolic assets in China.

**Why it matters for SHTG / lipid programs:** Arrowhead's SHTG Phase 3 data, arriving on roughly the same timeline as Ionis's potential SHTG approval, will define whether a true head-to-head market competition emerges by late 2026 or early 2027.

## What to Watch

The Essence-TIMI 73b plaque paper lands at an inflection point for the TG-lowering field. The new 2026 ACC/AHA dyslipidemia guideline—published the same day—likely wrestles with this exact question: if we can dramatically lower TG and remnant cholesterol but cannot show atherosclerosis regression at 12 months, how confident should we be in cardiovascular risk reduction? Nordestgaard's accompanying editorial in JACC will offer one prominent interpretation. Watch for whether the guideline codifies APOC3 inhibition as standard of care for SHTG ahead of the Tryngolza PDUFA date on June 30. Meanwhile, Arrowhead's mid-year SHTG Phase 3 readout for plozasiran will either validate or complicate the competitive picture. The next 8 weeks are among the most consequential in the history of triglyceride-lowering pharmacology.
