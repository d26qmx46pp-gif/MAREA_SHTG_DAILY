# SHTG / ANGPTL / APOC3 / LPL — Daily Digest — Friday September 18

**Bottom line:** A new JACC: Basic to Translational Science analysis shows ApoC-III inhibition with olezarsen strips triglycerides and cholesterol out of Lp(a) particles without lowering Lp(a) mass itself, while two case reports underline how hard SHTG still is to diagnose and treat at the bedside — and it was a quiet week for Arrowhead, Alnylam, and Ionis in the trade press.

## Part A — SHTG / lipid science (PubMed)

*Note: the top-tier-journal search (NEJM, Lancet, JAMA, Circulation, JACC, Eur Heart J, Nat Med, etc.) returned zero hits in the last 5 days. The query was broadened to all journals indexing SHTG/ANGPTL3/ANGPTL4/APOC3/LPL papers; results below are the most relevant of that broader set, led by two companion pieces in JACC: Basic to Translational Science, a legitimate JACC-family cardiology journal.*

**A Novel Triglyceride- and Cholesterol-Enriched Lipoprotein(a) Phenotype in Moderate Hypertriglyceridemia: Reversal by ApoC-III Inhibition**
Lizhu Lin, Maiah N. Brush, C. Bradley Nelson, et al. — *JACC: Basic to Translational Science* (2026)
[PMID: 42753678](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42753678/) | [DOI: 10.1016/j.jacbts.2026.101699](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jacbts.2026.101699)
In people with moderate hypertriglyceridemia, a meaningful chunk of circulating Lp(a) particles turn out to be riding around loaded with extra triglyceride and cholesterol — accounting for roughly 16–21% of total plasma triglycerides and 9–11% of total cholesterol. Using samples from a phase 2 trial of olezarsen (the ApoC-III-targeting antisense drug now marketed as Tryngolza), the authors show that blocking ApoC-III cuts this Lp(a)-associated triglyceride and cholesterol by 56–70%, even though it doesn't consistently lower Lp(a) particle number itself. Oxidized phospholipid levels tracked more closely with how many Lp(a) particles were around than with how much fat was stuck to them, suggesting these are two separable dimensions of Lp(a) biology. Practically, it means Lp(a)'s "badness" in someone with high triglycerides may be partly a lipid-loading problem that ApoC-III drugs can fix, separate from the harder problem of lowering Lp(a) mass.

**Lipoprotein(a) Particle Core Remodeling and Apolipoprotein C-III Inhibition: Unraveling the Complexity of Lp(a) Metabolism in Hypertriglyceridemia**
Dick C. Chan, Gerald F. Watts — *JACC: Basic to Translational Science* (2026), editorial
[PMID: 42753679](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42753679/) | [DOI: 10.1016/j.jacbts.2026.101697](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jacbts.2026.101697)
Companion editorial to the paper above. Chan and Watts frame the Lp(a) lipid-loading finding within the broader mess of overlapping lipoprotein pathways in hypertriglyceridemia, and argue it helps explain why Lp(a)-lowering and triglyceride-lowering drugs can have divergent effects on cardiovascular risk markers even when both move Lp(a)-related numbers. Useful context for interpreting the primary paper, though it doesn't add new data.

**Glucagon-like peptide-1 receptor agonists as adjunctive therapy in refractory hypertriglyceridemia following recurrent pancreatitis**
Daisuke Miura, Stephen Nicholls, Anju E. Joham, et al. — *JCEM Case Reports* (2026)
[PMID: 42750924](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42750924/) | [DOI: 10.1210/jcemcr/luag274](https://doi.org/10.1210/jcemcr/luag274)
Case report of a 39-year-old man with six episodes of HTG-induced pancreatitis over nine years despite maximal statin/fibrate/omega-3 therapy, peak triglycerides of 2,329 mg/dL, and comorbid diabetes and steatotic liver disease. Despite historical caution around GLP-1 receptor agonists in patients with prior pancreatitis, the team cautiously started semaglutide and saw triglycerides normalize to 213 mg/dL, along with weight loss, glycemic improvement, and no recurrent pancreatitis. It's one case, not a trial, but it's a data point for a question a lot of SHTG clinicians are quietly asking: does the old pancreatitis-history contraindication for GLP-1RAs still hold in carefully selected, multidisciplinary-managed patients?

**When biochemistry fails: extreme lipemia masking hypertriglyceridemia-induced acute pancreatitis treated with insulin**
Elif Unal Kaya, Mert Ali Kaya — *JCEM Case Reports* (2026)
[PMID: 42751071](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42751071/) | [DOI: 10.1210/jcemcr/luag281](https://doi.org/10.1210/jcemcr/luag281)
A 30-year-old man presented with abdominal pain so severe it triggered an ICU admission, but his triglycerides were so high (eventually confirmed at 6,300 mg/dL) that the lab couldn't run standard chemistry panels on the sample — the plasma was too lipemic to analyze. Clinicians empirically started an insulin/dextrose infusion to drive lipoprotein lipase activity before they had a confirmed diagnosis, and triglycerides fell to 243 mg/dL by day 8. A pancreatic pseudocyst was managed conservatively. A vivid illustration of how SHTG can outrun the diagnostic tools meant to catch it, and a reminder that empiric LPL-activating treatment is a reasonable move when extreme lipemia itself is blocking diagnosis.

## What to watch

The JACC: BTS work adds a mechanistic wrinkle worth watching as ApoC-III inhibitors (olezarsen/Tryngolza, and Arrowhead's plozasiran/Redemplo close behind) move deeper into broader SHTG populations: if these drugs are quietly reshaping Lp(a) particle composition as a side effect, that could complicate how trials and clinics interpret Lp(a) as a cardiovascular risk marker in treated patients. On the clinical-practice side, both case reports this cycle are reminders that real-world SHTG management still runs into contraindication gray zones (GLP-1RAs after pancreatitis) and basic lab limitations (lipemia blocking diagnosis) that no amount of drug-pipeline progress solves on its own. With Tryngolza's label now expanded into general SHTG and Redemplo's own SHTG phase 3 data expected before long, the next industry-news cycle is a reasonable bet to be less quiet.
