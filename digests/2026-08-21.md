**Clinical Benefits Associated With Evinacumab in Pediatric Patients With Homozygous Familial Hypercholesterolemia**
Reijman MD, Nigmann C, Kusters DM, Planken RN. *Journal of the American Heart Association*, Aug 20, 2026.
PMID: [42622232](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42622232/) | DOI: [10.1161/JAHA.125.048419](https://doi.org/10.1161/JAHA.125.048419)

This retrospective observational study followed 13 children with homozygous familial hypercholesterolemia (HoFH) who started evinacumab — a monoclonal antibody that blocks ANGPTL3 — on top of oral lipid-lowering therapy, with a median follow-up of two years. Before evinacumab, none of the 13 patients were at their LDL-C goal; afterward, 9 of 13 (69%) reached goal. Add-on evinacumab was also associated with a reduced need for lipoprotein apheresis and, in most patients, stabilization or improvement of coronary CT angiography findings. The cohort is small and retrospective, so causality can't be firmly established, but it's a useful real-world data point on ANGPTL3 blockade in a genetically extreme lipid population, and directly relevant to Marea's ANGPTL3 interest even though the population here is HoFH rather than SHTG per se.

**Plozasiran: a small interfering RNA therapy for hypertriglyceridemia and familial chylomicronemia syndrome**
Rodriguez-Montes J, Hale G, Benny T. *Daru* (Journal of the Faculty of Pharmacy, Tehran University of Medical Sciences), Aug 19, 2026. Review.
PMID: [42616276](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42616276/) | DOI: [10.1007/s40199-026-00638-6](https://doi.org/10.1007/s40199-026-00638-6)

A pharmacy-focused review of plozasiran (Redemplo), Arrowhead's APOC3-targeting siRNA, summarizing the mechanism (hepatic APOC3 mRNA degradation, reduced serum APOC3 and triglycerides) and the clinical data package to date. It highlights the Phase 3 PALISADE results in familial chylomicronemia syndrome — roughly 80% fasting triglyceride reduction at 10 months and a lower pancreatitis incidence versus placebo — while noting that PALISADE wasn't powered specifically for pancreatitis prevention, so that signal remains supportive rather than definitive. The review also flags an ongoing safety profile of low-grade thrombocytopenia, GI symptoms, and hyperglycemia in patients with preexisting dysglycemia, and lists the full slate of ongoing Phase 3 trials (SHASTA-3, -4, -5, -10, MUIR-3, CAPITAN) still reading out in broader SHTG and cardiovascular-outcome populations. Useful as a compact reference on where the plozasiran data package stands, and pairs naturally with the SHASTA-3/4 industry story below.

**Critical Care-Based Decision-Making and Timing of Pregnancy Termination in Hypertriglyceridemia-Induced Acute Pancreatitis Complicated by Diabetic Ketoacidosis and Intrauterine Fetal Demise: A Case Report**
Hu Y, Mao Y, Gao H. *Case Reports in Critical Care*, Aug 17, 2026.
PMID: [42609868](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42609868/) | DOI: [10.1155/crcc/4122751](https://doi.org/10.1155/crcc/4122751)

A single case report of a pregnant patient with severe hypertriglyceridemia-induced acute pancreatitis complicated by diabetic ketoacidosis and intrauterine fetal demise. Management combined continuous renal replacement therapy for refractory acidosis with therapeutic plasma exchange to rapidly lower triglyceride burden, used as a bridge before cesarean delivery once the patient's metabolic status stabilized. As a single case it can't establish generalizable treatment guidance, but it's a vivid illustration of the acute, high-stakes clinical reality that drives the case for better chronic SHTG control (and for on-label triglyceride-lowering agents) in patients who could become pregnant.

## What to watch

The science and the industry threads point to the same near-term event: Arrowhead's full SHASTA-3/4 dataset at ESC on August 30 will show whether plozasiran's topline triglyceride and pancreatitis numbers hold up in granular subgroup analysis, and how the drug's tolerability profile (including the liver-fat differentiation versus Tryngolza that analysts flagged after the topline release) compares head-to-head with Ionis's olezarsen. On the mechanism side, this cycle's ANGPTL3 and APOC3 papers are a reminder that the two approaches — antibody blockade of ANGPTL3 versus siRNA/ASO knockdown of APOC3 — are being tested in overlapping but not identical patient populations (HoFH versus SHTG/FCS), so head-to-head positioning across both classes will keep sharpening as more data lands this fall.
