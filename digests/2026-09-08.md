**Integrating 2.5D multi-modal CT imaging with serum triglycerides for early risk stratification in hypertriglyceridemia-induced acute pancreatitis: A multicenter study.** Guo, Chen, Qin, et al. *European Journal of Radiology.* [PMID: 42322722](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42322722) · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ejrad.2026.113028)
This is a solidly powered multicenter study (1,518 patients with hypertriglyceridemia-induced acute pancreatitis across three hospitals, with two external validation cohorts) that combines deep-learning features from routine dual-phase CT scans with a single triglyceride measurement to flag which patients will progress to severe acute pancreatitis. The fused model hit AUCs of 0.89–0.97 across training and validation cohorts, and adding triglyceride level alone boosted sensitivity for severe disease from around 61% to 94% in one external cohort while keeping specificity high. For a bedside-relevant readout: this is a practical triage tool built almost entirely from data clinicians already collect in the first 24 hours, which could help emergency and GI teams decide who needs aggressive early intervention.

**A prognostic model based on metabolic indicators for recurrent acute pancreatitis in hypertriglyceridemia patients: a retrospective cohort study.** Wang, Liu, Ren, et al. *Annals of Medicine.* [PMID: 41978916](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41978916) · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13017-025-00601-x)
A retrospective single-center cohort of 293 patients with hypertriglyceridemic acute pancreatitis, of whom about 1 in 5 had a recurrence within two years. The authors built and internally validated a nomogram combining visceral adipose tissue index and other metabolic markers to stratify recurrence risk, with reasonable discrimination on cross-validation. It's a modest, single-center effort, but it speaks directly to a persistent clinical gap: identifying which SHTG-pancreatitis patients need the most intensive follow-up and lipid-lowering therapy versus who can be managed more conservatively.

**Genetic predisposition of Asparaginase-induced hypertriglyceridemia in children with acute lymphoblastic leukemia: Two case reports and review of literature.** Krivosheina, Bakaleiko, Toshina, et al. *World Journal of Clinical Pediatrics.* [PMID: 42626214](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42626214) · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.5409/wjcp.120927)
A case-report-plus-review piece on two pediatric leukemia patients who developed severe HTG after pegylated asparaginase, a known but mechanistically murky toxicity of that chemotherapy. Targeted genetic panel testing turned up variants tied to inherited dyslipidemia in both children, and the authors argue this supports a model where an underlying genetic predisposition — not just the drug itself — determines who develops dangerous triglyceride spikes on asparaginase. If replicated in larger series, this could argue for pre-treatment lipid genetic screening in pediatric ALL, and it's a reminder that drug-induced SHTG often has a silent genetic first hit.

## Part B — Industry news (SHTG / Arrowhead / Alnylam / Ionis)

**Novartis, Ionis drug failure spurs questions about an emerging class of heart medicines.** BioPharma Dive, published September 5, 2026. [Source](https://www.biopharmadive.com/news/novartis-ionis-pelacarsen-lpa-horizon-study-results-fail/829736/) (also covered by [STAT News](https://www.statnews.com/2026/09/04/novartis-therapy-pelacarsen-fails-cardiovascular-trial/), paywalled)
Novartis and Ionis's pelacarsen — an antisense drug designed to sharply lower lipoprotein(a), or Lp(a) — failed to reduce cardiovascular death, heart attacks, or strokes in the Phase 3 HORIZON trial, a study of more than 8,000 patients. The drug did lower Lp(a) levels as expected (Novartis said the reduction was "in the range previously observed" in earlier trials, per analyst notes), but that biomarker drop didn't translate into a clinical benefit. Novartis hasn't released full data yet — that's expected at a future medical meeting — but analysts are already flagging "meaningful risk" for other Lp(a)-lowering drugs in development at Amgen, Eli Lilly, and Silence Therapeutics. *Why it matters for SHTG/lipid programs:* this doesn't directly implicate the APOC3 or ANGPTL3 pathways that Arrowhead's plozasiran/zodasiran and Ionis's own olezarsen (Tryngolza) work through, but it's a sobering data point for the broader premise that lowering a lipid biomarker via RNA-targeted therapy automatically translates to fewer cardiovascular events — a premise that underlies much of the sHTG drug-development thesis too.

## What to watch

The pelacarsen readout is the story to watch even though it sits just outside this show's core APOC3/ANGPTL3/LPL lane — if it turns out biomarker-lowering alone doesn't reliably predict outcomes in this drug class, it will sharpen scrutiny on the outcomes data still pending for Arrowhead's and Ionis's sHTG programs, even though those work through different, more directly triglyceride-metabolizing pathways (apoC-III and ANGPTL3, versus apo(a)). On the science side, this cycle's papers are a reminder that a lot of the actionable near-term work in SHTG is still happening at the bedside — risk-stratifying pancreatitis patients and untangling genetic susceptibility — rather than in flashy new drug targets.
