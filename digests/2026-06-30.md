### 1. Apolipoprotein C3 promotes retinal angiogenesis via the MYC pathway in hypoxia

**Chen X, Wu G, Wu X, Jin Y, Zhu H, Liu J, et al.** *Current Eye Research.* 2026 Jun 28. PMID: [42366514](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42366514/) | DOI: [10.1080/02713683.2026.2691742](https://doi.org/10.1080/02713683.2026.2691742)

This study identifies a previously underappreciated function for APOC3 in the eye. Using proteomics data and a multiplex immunoassay, the authors found APOC3 is significantly elevated in the aqueous humor and serum of patients with diabetic retinopathy, and in the retinas of diabetic db/db mice and oxygen-induced retinopathy (OIR) mice. When recombinant APOC3 was applied to human retinal microvascular endothelial cells under hypoxic conditions, it stimulated cell proliferation, migration, and tube formation — key steps in pathological angiogenesis. RNA sequencing and inhibition experiments implicated the MYC transcriptional pathway as the downstream effector. The findings suggest APOC3 acts locally in the retina under ischemic conditions to drive vascular proliferation, independent of its canonical role in triglyceride metabolism. While this does not directly address SHTG pharmacology, it adds to the growing picture of APOC3 as a pleiotropic protein with disease-relevant biology beyond the liver and bloodstream. Potential implications for anti-APOC3 therapies — whether lowering systemic APOC3 might incidentally protect the diabetic retina — remain speculative but worth watching.

### 2. Genome sequencing identifies monogenic causes in adults with metabolic diseases

**Okur V, Marcus A, Falcone J, Hurd M, Stewart S, Claudio K, et al.** *Journal of the Endocrine Society.* 2026 Jul (ahead of print). PMID: [42255514](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42255514/) | DOI: [10.1210/jendso/bvag091](https://doi.org/10.1210/jendso/bvag091)

This New York clinical cohort study asks a pragmatic question: how often does whole-genome sequencing identify an actionable monogenic cause in adults presenting with common metabolic diseases? The investigators performed clinical genome sequencing (GS) in 560 adults with hyperlipidemia/hypertriglyceridemia (HLD/HTG), pre-diabetes, T2DM, and/or metabolic fatty liver disease, screened against 90 genes associated with monogenic forms of those conditions using ACMG/ClinGen variant classification guidelines. The cohort skewed female and included substantial representation of African American (18.6%) and Latino (22.6%) participants — populations often underrepresented in genomic studies. HLD/HTG was the most common enrollment diagnosis at 25%. The study establishes diagnostic yield data that could inform clinical triage: which patients with apparent "common" dyslipidemia or HTG are actually carrying a monogenic variant in a gene like LPL, APOC3, or the ANGPTL family that would change management. Precise yield numbers are not in the PubMed abstract but the paper is available in the Journal of the Endocrine Society. Clinically, this supports a growing case for genetic testing in HTG patients who present early, have extreme TG elevations, or fail standard therapy.

### 3. Context-dependent effects of Apolipoprotein A2 on lipid metabolism and atherogenesis

**Zvintzou E, Fenyo I, Ionita R, Kakafoni G, Dumitrescu M, Alemi M, et al.** *FASEB Journal.* 2026 Jun 30. PMID: [42268249](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42268249/) | DOI: [10.1096/fj.202600765R](https://doi.org/10.1096/fj.202600765R)

A less directly disease-relevant but biologically interesting entry: this study characterizes APOA2, a structural component of multiple plasma lipoproteins whose effects on cardiovascular risk have been conflicting across decades of research. The group used adenovirus-mediated overexpression of human APOA2 in normal C57BL/6 mice and in LDL-receptor-deficient (ldlr−/−) mice to dissect how genetic background and metabolic context determine whether APOA2 raises or lowers atherogenic risk. The work is relevant to the lipid biology community as it probes how apolipoprotein context shapes lipoprotein function — a question that matters for interpreting the full lipid response to APOC3- and LPL-targeting therapies. The PubMed abstract is abbreviated; full results are in the FASEB Journal. APOA2 is not a direct therapeutic target in SHTG but the conceptual framework — that apolipoprotein function depends on the metabolic environment — has broader implications.

## Part B — Industry news (SHTG / Arrowhead / Alnylam / Ionis)

*Previously covered — update this cycle:* **Ionis enters next commercial phase after FDA approves Tryngolza for sHTG**

**FiercePharma | Kevin Dunleavy | June 25, 2026** | [Source](https://www.fiercepharma.com/pharma/fda-nod-tryngolza-ionis-enters-new-era-commercial-stage-company)

*See also (covered in prior digest):* [FiercePharma — Ionis scores landmark FDA label expansion](https://www.fiercepharma.com/pharma/ionis-scores-landmark-fda-label-expansion-tryngolza) | [BioPharma Dive — Ionis wins FDA OK](https://www.biopharmadive.com/news/ionis-wins-fda-ok-Tryngolza-broader-label-expansion/823717/)

The FDA approved Tryngolza (olezarsen) for severe hypertriglyceridemia on June 24, 2026, was the top story in the prior cycle. What is new this cycle is the commercial launch detail and the strategic pivot it represents. Ionis has spent 35 years as a pure R&D company; the sHTG approval marks its first entry into a large commercial market, with an estimated 3.6 million Americans meeting the sHTG definition (TG ≥ 500 mg/dL). Ionis is initially targeting the higher-risk subset — approximately 500,000 patients with TG ≥ 880 mg/dL — where pancreatitis risk is most acute. A 200-person field force has been deployed since February, calling on endocrinologists, cardiologists, and lipid specialists who already manage sHTG patients and who know the disease well. CEO Brett Monia said physicians are "chomping at the bit" to prescribe Tryngolza, as current generic options (fibrates, omega-3 fish oils) offer only 10–20% TG reduction versus the up to 72% reduction Tryngolza achieved in Phase 3. Ionis raised its peak net sales guidance from $2 billion to $3 billion for the sHTG indication in April, and first-year FCS launch generated $108 million — a proof point for the commercial infrastructure. The price was slashed from $595,000 to $40,000 annually in April to support broad access.

**Why it matters for SHTG / lipid programs:** This is the first ASO approved for sHTG, and it resets expectations for what an APOC3-targeting drug can achieve commercially. The launch will test whether TG-lowering can translate into covered prescriptions at scale — a market access question that will also affect how plozasiran (Arrowhead, RNAi vs. APOC3) is positioned when it pursues its own sHTG label.

## What to watch

The APOC3 retinal angiogenesis paper is a reminder that drugs like Tryngolza and plozasiran are being deployed into patients who, by definition, carry chronically elevated circulating APOC3. Whether sustained APOC3 suppression has ophthalmologic effects — protective against diabetic retinopathy, or potentially disruptive of physiological retinal repair — is a question none of the current trials are powered to answer but that long-term safety follow-up data will touch. On the industry side, all eyes are on Ionis' first prescriptions and early payer coverage decisions in sHTG, which will set the commercial blueprint for the RNA-targeting lipid space. Arrowhead's sHTG Phase 3 program (MURMUR, studying plozasiran in sHTG) is enrolling, and early commercial signals from Tryngolza will shape how aggressively that data is interpreted.
