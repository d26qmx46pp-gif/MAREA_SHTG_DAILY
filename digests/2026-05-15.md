## Part A — SHTG / lipid science (PubMed)

**Efficacy and Safety of Olezarsen in Hypertriglyceridemia: An Updated Meta-Analysis of Randomised Controlled Trials.**
Emara A, Mansour A, Aboeldahab H, et al. *Diabetes, Obesity & Metabolism*, June 2026. [PMID 41943994](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41943994/) · [DOI 10.1111/dom.70716](https://doi.org/10.1111/dom.70716)

Updated pooled analysis of six RCTs (n = 2,676) of the APOC3 ASO olezarsen. At the 50 mg dose, triglycerides fell ~47%; at 80 mg, ~54%, with ApoC-III itself dropping ~64–67%. Non-HDL-C declined meaningfully (–21% to –25%), though LDL-C ticked up modestly at 50 mg. Most importantly for the SHTG conversation, acute pancreatitis risk fell sharply (RR 0.16 at 50 mg; RR 0.33 at 80 mg). Overall AEs tracked placebo, but ≥3× ULN transaminase elevations were more common at 80 mg, a signal worth watching as the indication broadens.

**Efficacy and Safety of Apolipoprotein C-III Inhibitors in Hypertriglyceridemia: A Network Meta-Analysis of Randomised Controlled Trials.**
Elbahloul M, Gamal A, Maihoub O, et al. *Diabetes, Obesity & Metabolism*, June 2026. [PMID 41917770](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41917770/) · [DOI 10.1111/dom.70717](https://doi.org/10.1111/dom.70717)

A 15-RCT, 3,934-patient frequentist network meta-analysis pitting volanesorsen, olezarsen, and plozasiran against placebo. Almost every regimen reduced triglycerides significantly (exceptions: volanesorsen 100 mg Q1W and olezarsen 10 mg Q4W). Olezarsen 80 mg Q4W ranked highest on SUCRA for TG reduction (MD ≈ –63%). Across the class, ApoC-III inhibitors cut acute pancreatitis risk by ~84% (HR 0.16, 95% CI 0.07–0.33) without a meaningful increase in serious adverse events — the cleanest cross-agent comparison published to date and a useful framing for clinicians weighing emerging options.

**The Efficacy and Safety of Plozasiran on Lipid Profile in Dyslipidemic Disorders: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis.**
Sydhom P, Al-Quraishi B, Gohar A, et al. *Cardiovascular Drugs and Therapy*, June 2026. [PMID 41251855](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41251855/) · [DOI 10.1007/s10557-025-07798-8](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10557-025-07798-8)

Four-study, 1,514-patient meta-analysis of Arrowhead's APOC3 siRNA. Plozasiran significantly improved triglycerides, ApoC-III, non-HDL-C, HDL-C, and ApoB at 24 weeks and study end, with consistent effects across 10, 25, and 50 mg quarterly regimens. ApoB reductions were sustained at 25 mg and 50 mg but waned at 10 mg by end of study. On safety, higher overall AEs, more headache, and small dose-dependent rises in HbA1c stood out — a reminder that the metabolic signal seen in earlier plozasiran datasets remains a watch-item heading into the SHTG Phase 3 readout expected later this year.

**SPP2 as an HNF4A target gene regulating triglyceride homeostasis via LPL activation.**
Wen Y, Liu X, Wang M, et al. *Biochemical and Biophysical Research Communications*, June 25, 2026. [PMID 42030881](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42030881/) · [DOI 10.1016/j.bbrc.2026.153778](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.bbrc.2026.153778)

A nice piece of mechanism. The authors show that SPP2 (also known as SPP24), a secreted cystatin-family protein, lowers serum triglycerides — both as a recombinant protein and via AAV8 overexpression — by binding LPL directly and increasing VLDL hydrolysis. SPP2 also pushes hepatic β-oxidation and ketogenesis during fasting. Upstream, fasting-driven free fatty acids activate HNF4A, which binds the SPP2 promoter and drives expression. The HNF4A–SPP2–LPL axis is a new, fasting-responsive node in TG homeostasis and a plausible target for SHTG beyond the APOC3/ANGPTL family.

**Drug targets for lipid modification and risk of type 2 diabetes: a cis-Mendelian randomization study.**
Chen Z, Triatin R, Luo L, et al. *Cardiovascular Diabetology*, May 14, 2026. [PMID 42135799](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42135799/) · [DOI 10.1186/s12933-026-03209-w](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12933-026-03209-w)

Cis-MR across thirteen lipid drug-target loci, including ANGPTL3, ANGPTL4, APOC3, and LPA, using UK Biobank, Lifelines, and public GWAS data. HMGCR modification reduces CAD but raises T2D risk, as expected. APOC3, LDLR, LPA, MTTP, NPC1L1, and PCSK9 lower CAD without a T2D penalty. Crucially for the SHTG pipeline, ANGPTL4 modification is predicted to reduce both CAD and T2D risk, and ANGPTL3 modification shows neither benefit nor harm on either endpoint — a genetic note of caution for ANGPTL3-targeted programs that are otherwise riding on biomarker effects.

## Part B — Industry news (SHTG / Arrowhead / Alnylam / Ionis)

This was a thin cycle on direct SHTG news; covering the most relevant pieces from the past ~10 days, with dates labeled.

**Madrigal continues MASH deal spree with $1B biobucks license for Arrowhead asset rejected by J&J.**
*Fierce Biotech*, May 5, 2026 (context piece, just outside the 7-day window). [Source](https://www.fiercebiotech.com/biotech/madrigal-boosts-mash-pipeline-1b-biobucks-license-arrowhead-asset)

Madrigal paid Arrowhead $25M upfront, with up to ~$1B in development, regulatory, and sales milestones, to license ARO-PNPLA3, an RNAi therapeutic that silences hepatic PNPLA3 expression for patients with MASH. Phase 1 data previously showed a 46% drop in liver fat after a single dose in PNPLA3 I148M homozygotes — a population that represents about 30% of MASH patients with moderate-to-advanced fibrosis. J&J had returned the asset to Arrowhead during a 2023 pipeline restructure.
*Why it matters for SHTG / lipid programs:* Arrowhead is monetizing a non-core liver RNAi asset while preserving cash and focus for its lead cardiometabolic programs — plozasiran (APOC3) in SHTG and the Phase 2 ANGPTL3 program — ahead of the mid-2026 plozasiran SHTG readout that will set up its head-to-head with Tryngolza.

**Ionis' Tryngolza for severe hypertriglyceridemia — FDA decision by June 30.**
*BioPharma Dive*, "5 FDA decisions to watch in the second quarter of 2026" (published Jan 7, 2026; updated Mar 30, 2026) — context piece, but the PDUFA window is now six weeks away. [Source](https://www.biopharmadive.com/news/fda-approval-decisions-watch-2026/808684/)

The FDA is set to decide on Tryngolza (olezarsen) in severe hypertriglyceridemia by June 30, 2026. Pivotal Phase 3 data showed monthly olezarsen cut triglycerides by up to 73% in one study and 68% in the other, with significant reductions in acute pancreatitis. In late March, Ionis priced Tryngolza for sHTG at $40,000/year, undercutting Arrowhead's $60,000 Redemplo list price. Tryngolza generated $108M in 2025 net sales in FCS, and analysts have flagged sHTG as a potential multi-billion-dollar opportunity (Ionis' own guidance: $2B+ peak).
*Why it matters for SHTG / lipid programs:* This will be the first FDA approval of an APOC3-directed agent in a non-orphan triglyceride indication — the defining regulatory moment for the entire SHTG drug class and the reference point against which Arrowhead's plozasiran SHTG filing will be judged.

**Arrowhead battles Ionis over patent dispute tied to RNAi candidate that could rival Tryngolza.**
*Fierce Biotech* — ongoing dispute, latest coverage labeled as context. [Source](https://www.fiercebiotech.com/biotech/arrowhead-battles-ionis-over-patent-dispute-tied-rnai-candidate-could-rival-tryngolza)

Arrowhead has been seeking a court ruling that Ionis' patent No. 9,593,333 — "Modulation of apolipoprotein C-III (APOC3) expression in lipoprotein lipase deficient populations" — is invalid or not infringed by plozasiran's planned commercialization. Ionis and Arrowhead filed dueling lawsuits last September; Ionis alleged Arrowhead copied its patented technology to develop Redemplo and asked a California court for unspecified damages.
*Why it matters for SHTG / lipid programs:* Even with Redemplo on the market in FCS, the patent fight remains a live overhang on plozasiran's sHTG commercial trajectory — and on how aggressively Arrowhead can price and market the drug if it follows Ionis into the broader sHTG indication later this year.

## What to watch

The journal cycle and the industry cycle are pointing at the same date — June 30, when Ionis is expected to get an FDA verdict on Tryngolza in severe hypertriglyceridemia. Three meta-analyses this week independently confirmed that APOC3 inhibition delivers ~50–65% TG reductions and an ~80–85% drop in acute pancreatitis risk across volanesorsen, olezarsen, and plozasiran — making the regulatory question less about whether the class works and more about how the label is written, how the price gradient between Tryngolza and Redemplo settles, and whether Arrowhead's mid-year plozasiran SHTG data hold up well enough to bring a second APOC3 agent into the same market by year-end. On the mechanism side, the SPP2/HNF4A/LPL paper and the cis-MR analysis of ANGPTL3 vs. ANGPTL4 are quiet reminders that the field's next round of triglyceride targets may not be limited to APOC3 — and that the genetic case for ANGPTL4-directed therapy is, if anything, getting stronger.
