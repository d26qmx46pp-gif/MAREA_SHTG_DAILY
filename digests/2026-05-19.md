# SHTG / ANGPTL / APOC3 / LPL — Daily Digest — Tuesday May 19

**Bottom line:** A landmark Circulation imaging trial shows olezarsen dramatically lowers triglycerides but does not shrink coronary plaque, while two new meta-analyses confirm robust TG and pancreatitis benefits across the ApoC-III inhibitor class, and on the industry side both Arrowhead and Ionis are sprinting toward a mid-2026 collision in the sHTG market.

## Part A — SHTG / lipid science (PubMed)

One top-tier journal hit met the bar this cycle; three next-best-tier papers add meaningful context.

---

**Effect of APOC3 Inhibition With Olezarsen on Coronary Atherosclerosis: Essence-TIMI 73b Imaging Study**

Marston NA, Bergmark BA, Prohaska TA, Moura FA, Zimerman A, Alexander VJ, et al. *Circulation*. 2026 May 19;153(20):1539–1548. [PMID 41910513](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41910513/) | [DOI 10.1161/CIRCULATIONAHA.126.080012](https://doi.org/10.1161/CIRCULATIONAHA.126.080012)

Despite slashing triglycerides by 64% and remnant cholesterol by 72% relative to placebo, twelve months of olezarsen did not reduce noncalcified coronary plaque volume in the 468-participant Essence-TIMI 73b coronary CT angiography sub-study. This was a well-powered, randomized, placebo-controlled imaging trial in patients with largely moderate hypertriglyceridemia (median baseline TG 249 mg/dL) who were already on standard lipid-lowering therapy; there was no significant difference between groups for low-attenuation, calcified, or total plaque volumes either. The null plaque result should be interpreted carefully — the study was not designed to detect pancreatitis prevention, olezarsen reduced apoB by only 16% (with no LDL-C change), and the population had moderate rather than severe TG elevation — but it raises an important question about whether triglyceride-rich lipoprotein lowering alone translates to coronary plaque regression on top of statin therapy.

---

**Efficacy and Safety of Olezarsen in Hypertriglyceridemia: An Updated Meta-Analysis of Randomised Controlled Trials**

Emara A, Mansour A, Aboeldahab H, Awashra A. *Diabetes Obes Metab*. 2026 Jun [Epub ahead of print]. [PMID 41943994](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41943994/) | [DOI 10.1111/dom.70716](https://doi.org/10.1111/dom.70716)

This updated pooled analysis of six RCTs (2,676 participants) provides the most comprehensive summary of olezarsen's efficacy to date. At the 50 mg dose, olezarsen reduced triglycerides by a mean 47% and ApoC-III by 64%; at 80 mg, TG fell 54% and ApoC-III 67%, both highly significant. Notably, the 80 mg dose was associated with a significantly higher rate of liver enzyme elevations (ALT/AST ≥3× ULN: RR 2.56), a safety signal that deserves monitoring as the drug moves into a broader sHTG population. Most importantly, both doses substantially reduced acute pancreatitis risk — RR 0.16 at 50 mg and 0.33 at 80 mg — confirming the most clinically urgent benefit of ApoC-III inhibition. A modest increase in LDL-C (roughly 18%) was observed at the 50 mg dose.

---

**Efficacy and Safety of Apolipoprotein C-III Inhibitors in Hypertriglyceridemia: A Network Meta-Analysis of Randomised Controlled Trials**

Elbahloul M, Gamal A, Maihoub O, Elbaz Hassan M. *Diabetes Obes Metab*. 2026 Jun [Epub ahead of print]. [PMID 41917770](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41917770/) | [DOI 10.1111/dom.70717](https://doi.org/10.1111/dom.70717)

The first network meta-analysis to directly compare all three ApoC-III inhibitors — volanesorsen, olezarsen, and plozasiran — across 15 RCTs and 3,934 patients. Olezarsen 80 mg every four weeks ranked highest for TG reduction (mean difference −63.3%), though multiple regimens across all three agents were effective. Crucially, ApoC-III inhibitors as a class reduced pancreatitis incidence by 84% (HR 0.16, 95% CI 0.07–0.33). None of the agents significantly increased serious adverse events in this analysis, though volanesorsen's platelet liability was a previously known concern not fully adjudicated here. This head-to-head comparison positions olezarsen 80 mg as the numerically strongest TG-lowering option, relevant context as Ionis and Arrowhead head into direct market competition.

---

**SPP2 as an HNF4A Target Gene Regulating Triglyceride Homeostasis via LPL Activation**

Wen Y, Liu X, Wang M, Li C. *Biochem Biophys Res Commun*. 2026 Jun 25 [Epub ahead of print]. [PMID 42030881](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42030881/) | [DOI 10.1016/j.bbrc.2026.153778](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.bbrc.2026.153778)

A mechanistic study identifying secreted phosphoprotein 2 (SPP2) as a previously unrecognized hepatokine that lowers serum triglycerides by enhancing LPL activity. Both recombinant SPP2 protein and AAV8-mediated overexpression selectively reduced serum TG without affecting other metabolic parameters; the mechanism involves direct SPP2 binding to LPL and modulation of LPL cofactor expression. Fasting induces SPP2 expression via free fatty acid–activated HNF4A, which binds directly to the SPP2 promoter — establishing an HNF4A–SPP2–LPL axis as a physiological TG-clearing circuit. The study is preclinical and the journal tier is modest, but the discovery of a new LPL-activating hepatokine is potentially therapeutic-grade; LPL activation strategies (cf. ANGPTL3/4 inhibition) are an active area for SHTG, and a direct LPL-binding protein offers a distinct angle.

**Arrowhead Pharmaceuticals Reports Q2 FY2026 Results: plozasiran momentum builds, novel dual-gene RNAi enters clinic**

*Arrowhead Pharmaceuticals press release via SEC 8-K — context piece, May 7, 2026*
[Source: Arrowhead 8-K / May 7 2026](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0000879407/000087940726000045/arwr-202605078kex991.htm)

Arrowhead reported that REDEMPLO (plozasiran) has now reached over 400 prescriptions and 180 patients with at least one shipment since its November 2025 FDA approval for FCS; new weekly written prescriptions are averaging approximately 30. The company updated the wholesale acquisition cost of REDEMPLO to $45,000 per patient per year under a "One-REDEMPLO" unified-pricing model intended to remain consistent if the sHTG indication is approved — a deliberate move to undercut Ionis' announced $40,000 sHTG price while maintaining a premium for FCS. At the ACC scientific sessions, Arrowhead presented two-year open-label data showing an 83% median TG reduction in sHTG patients and zero adjudicated acute pancreatitis events. On the pipeline front, Arrowhead dosed the first subjects in a Phase 1/2a trial of ARO-DIMER-PA, the first dual-functional RNAi clinical candidate targeting both PCSK9 and APOC3 simultaneously in a single molecule. The company also licensed ARO-PNPLA3 (a MASH candidate) to Madrigal Pharmaceuticals for $25M upfront and up to $975M in milestones, and raised $930M in a two-part equity and convertible-note offering.

*Why it matters for SHTG / lipid programs:* With sHTG Phase 3 data (SHASTA-3/4) expected around mid-2026 and the unified pricing model already locked in, Arrowhead is engineering a direct competitive challenge to Ionis on both clinical and commercial grounds. The ARO-DIMER-PA program signals the next wave: combining LPL de-repression (via APOC3 silencing) with LDL reduction (PCSK9) in a single shot.

**Ionis Reports Q1 2026 Results: olezarsen sNDA on Priority Review, PDUFA June 30; peak sHTG sales guidance raised to >$3B**

*Ionis Pharmaceuticals press release via SEC 8-K — context piece, April 29, 2026*
[Source: Ionis 8-K / April 29 2026](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0000874015/000114036126017637/ef20071749_ex99-1.htm)

Ionis announced that the FDA accepted the supplemental NDA for olezarsen in severe hypertriglyceridemia for Priority Review with a PDUFA target action date of June 30, 2026. The company raised its peak net sales guidance for olezarsen in sHTG from >$2B to >$3B, reflecting growing confidence in the market size and in its $40,000 annual list price — an intentional undercut of Arrowhead's REDEMPLO. Tryngolza generated $27 million in net FCS sales in Q1 2026, while EU launch via Sobi is now underway. Total revenue reached $246 million (+87% YOY), supported by $95 million in milestone payments from partnerships. Also notable: positive Phase 3 results for bepirovirsen (hepatitis B) are slated for presentation at EASL, and pelacarsen's Lp(a) HORIZON outcomes trial data are expected later in 2026 — both are partnered programs that could drive additional R&D revenue and de-risk Ionis' balance sheet path toward 2028 cash-flow breakeven.

*Why it matters for SHTG / lipid programs:* The June 30 PDUFA date is now the single most important near-term catalyst in the sHTG space. An approval would make olezarsen the first drug cleared by FDA for the broad sHTG population (FCS approval covers only the genetic extreme), opening a market Ionis estimates at >$3B peak. Pricing, payer contracting, and the timing of Arrowhead's competing sHTG data package will determine market dynamics through 2027.

## What to watch

The next six weeks are critical for the sHTG field. Ionis' June 30 PDUFA date for olezarsen in sHTG is the immediate pivot point — approval would establish the first regulatory standard for the broader condition and set the clinical and commercial baseline against which Arrowhead's expected mid-2026 sHTG Phase 3 readout from SHASTA-3/4 will be judged. On the science side, today's Essence-TIMI 73b imaging null result will sharpen debate about whether TG-rich lipoprotein lowering alone moves the atherosclerosis needle: if plaque regression requires a drug that also lowers apoB more substantially (or reduces LDL-C), that could favor mechanistic strategies like ANGPTL3 inhibition (which cuts LDL as well as TG) over pure ApoC-III blockade. The network meta-analysis confirming a class-wide 84% reduction in pancreatitis risk keeps the acute clinical argument for these drugs firmly intact regardless of the plaque data. And the SPP2/LPL finding is one to track: a hepatokine that directly activates LPL without engaging ANGPTL3 or ANGPTL4 as intermediaries could open a new target class for the post-sHTG approval era.
