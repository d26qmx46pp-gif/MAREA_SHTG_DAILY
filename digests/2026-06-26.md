## Part A — SHTG / lipid science (PubMed)

**Clinical Pharmacology of Plozasiran**
Huston J, Orey D, Corona K, Ashchi A, Lachapelle A et al. *American Journal of Cardiovascular Drugs*, June 2026.
[PMID 42340592](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42340592/) | DOI: 10.1111/joim.13059

This review consolidates the pharmacokinetic, pharmacodynamic, and clinical evidence for plozasiran, an siRNA that silences hepatic APOC3 mRNA, thereby reducing apoC-III protein and its downstream inhibition of lipoprotein lipase. The result is enhanced triglyceride clearance — a mechanistically clean approach to SHTG and familial chylomicronemia syndrome. The authors walk through Phase 1-3 data supporting once-quarterly subcutaneous dosing, with durable triglyceride reductions of 60–80% observed in FCS and SHTG trials. The review arrives at a timely moment: Arrowhead's Phase 3 sHTG data are expected in Q3 2026, and payers and clinicians are now weighing plozasiran against the newly approved Tryngolza. A useful reference for anyone synthesizing the APOC3-inhibitor landscape.

---

**Stage-dependent dynamics of Apolipoprotein C3 across the spectrum of MASLD**
Messer E, Fischer J, Herta T, Boehlig A, Matz-Soja M et al. *PLoS ONE*, 2026.
[PMID 42335045](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42335045/) | DOI: 10.4254/wjh.v6.i6.435

This 401-patient cohort study measured serum ApoC3 across four stages of metabolic dysfunction-associated steatotic liver disease (MASLD) and healthy blood-donor controls. The key finding: ApoC3 is highest in early-stage MASLD and correlates strongly with triglycerides (r = 0.485) and hepatic steatosis severity, but falls sharply in cirrhosis and hepatocellular carcinoma (median 18.7 vs. 26.6 mg/dL, p < 0.05), reflecting impaired hepatic synthetic function rather than resolution of metabolic disease. For therapeutic targeting, this biphasic pattern has important implications — APOC3 inhibitors may be most clinically meaningful before advanced fibrosis develops, and ApoC3 alone has modest diagnostic performance for cirrhosis (AUC 0.642). The authors argue ApoC3 is best understood as a cardiometabolic risk marker requiring integration into multi-parameter models.

**Urinary ANGPTL3: a novel noninvasive biomarker for podocyte injury in pediatric glomerular diseases**
Wang H, Liu J, Dai R, Wang C, Chen X et al. *Journal of Translational Medicine*, June 2026.
[PMID 42337674](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42337674/) | DOI: 10.1186/s12967-026-08512-4

This study tests the urinary ANGPTL3-to-creatinine ratio as a noninvasive surrogate for podocyte injury in 496 pediatric patients with glomerular disease. Renal ANGPTL3 expression negatively correlated with established podocyte injury markers (P57Kip2, Synaptopodin), and urinary ANGPTL3/Cre was an independent risk factor for podocyte injury (OR 7.66). Diagnostic performance was impressive — AUC of 0.90 in both test and validation sets, improving to 0.95 when combined with clinical variables and outperforming serum ANGPTL3 and traditional biomarkers. Notably, the test maintained an AUC of 0.88 even in patients with normal proteinuria post-treatment, suggesting utility for detecting subclinical injury. While this is a pediatric nephrology finding rather than a lipid biology result per se, it adds a new functional dimension to ANGPTL3 biology beyond its canonical role in lipoprotein metabolism.

## Part B — Industry news (SHTG / Arrowhead / Alnylam / Ionis)

**FDA approves Ionis' Tryngolza (olezarsen) for severe hypertriglyceridemia — first drug ever indicated for sHTG pancreatitis prevention**
*Fierce Pharma / BioPharma Dive — June 24–25, 2026*
[Fierce Pharma](https://www.fiercepharma.com/pharma/ionis-scores-landmark-fda-label-expansion-tryngolza) | [BioPharma Dive](https://www.biopharmadive.com/news/ionis-wins-fda-ok-Tryngolza-broader-label-expansion/823717/)

The FDA on June 24 approved Tryngolza (olezarsen, an APOC3-targeting antisense oligonucleotide) as an adjunct to diet to reduce triglycerides and the risk of acute pancreatitis in adults with sHTG (TG ≥ 500 mg/dL). This is the first drug ever specifically approved for sHTG and the first to carry a pancreatitis risk-reduction label in this population. The approval was based on two Phase 3 trials in which Tryngolza reduced TG by 63–72% vs. placebo at six months and cut pancreatitis risk by 85% as a secondary endpoint; 86% of patients crossed the <500 mg/dL threshold. Ionis set the annual list price at $40,000 (down from $595,000 for the FCS indication) and projects peak net sales exceeding $3 billion. Analysts called the label a "best case scenario" and described the approval as a "commercial inflection point" for a company historically built on licensing its platform rather than marketing products. The competitive clock is now ticking for Arrowhead: its rival APOC3 siRNA plozasiran has Phase 3 sHTG data expected in Q3 2026.

*Why it matters for SHTG / lipid programs:* This approval formally establishes sHTG as a pharmacologically addressable indication with a regulatory-accepted pancreatitis reduction endpoint — a template Arrowhead and any other entrant must now meet or beat. It also validates the APOC3 mechanism in a broad sHTG population beyond rare FCS, with profound implications for how this entire therapeutic class is priced, positioned, and compared.

**Arrowhead's REDEMPLO (plozasiran) commercial momentum and sHTG readout approaching**
*Context — multiple sources, most recent data from Q1 2026 earnings and SEC filings*

Arrowhead's plozasiran (brand name REDEMPLO), approved by the FDA in November 2025 for FCS, had logged more than 400 total prescriptions as of the most recent update, representing over 40% growth in a four-week period. The company has also received a positive CHMP opinion in Europe and TGA approval in Australia. Phase 3 sHTG data are expected in Q3 2026, setting up a direct head-to-head market entry against the now-approved Tryngolza. Arrowhead has deprioritized its ANGPTL3 siRNA zodasiran and is seeking a partner for that asset; the company's bet is concentrated on plozasiran across FCS, sHTG, and potentially mixed dyslipidemia.

*Why it matters for SHTG / lipid programs:* The Ionis approval this week directly sets the competitive bar plozasiran must clear. With sHTG data arriving this quarter, the next few months will define whether the market supports two APOC3 inhibitors or whether one emerges as a dominant standard of care.

**What to watch**

The FDA approval of Tryngolza this week is the single most consequential near-term event for the SHTG / APOC3 field. Ionis now has a commercial product in a 3.6-million-patient U.S. market, at a price point ($40,000/year) designed to facilitate broad payer access. The immediate question is how quickly cardiologists, lipidologists, and endocrinologists who have been waiting for this approval convert their sHTG patients — and whether the 85% pancreatitis risk reduction carries the clinical weight that the mechanism predicts. On the science side, the plozasiran pharmacology review and the ApoC3/MASLD paper together reinforce that the APOC3 axis matters across a wide spectrum of metabolic liver disease, not just SHTG. And the urinary ANGPTL3 biomarker data, while a nephrology story, are a reminder that ANGPTL3 biology extends well beyond lipid metabolism into glomerular biology — a thread that may eventually connect to the ANGPTL3 inhibitor programs at Alnylam and Ionis.
