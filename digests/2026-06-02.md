**GPIHBP1 on oligodendrocytes binds lipoprotein lipase within the human brain**
Liu M, Hung M, Kozlov E, Hung M, Colaço-Gaspar M, Roy S, et al.
*Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences* | June 2026
[PMID 42224591](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42224591/) | DOI: [10.1073/pnas.2610646123](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2610646123)

In peripheral tissues — fat, muscle — GPIHBP1 is the canonical endothelial protein that captures LPL and escorts it to the luminal capillary surface for lipolysis of circulating triglyceride-rich lipoproteins. Until now, no analogous binding partner for LPL in the central nervous system had been identified, even though LPL is expressed in the brain by microglia and oligodendrocyte precursor cells. By mining single-nucleus RNA-seq databases from human brain tissue, this group found that mature oligodendrocytes robustly express GPIHBP1 — a cell type not previously associated with this protein — and show that GPIHBP1 on these cells physically binds LPL within the brain interstitium. The discovery suggests that the CNS has its own LPL-capture machinery analogous to the endothelial system peripherally, potentially governing local fatty acid delivery to myelin-forming cells. Implications for neurological disease and the CNS effects of LPL-modulating therapies deserve attention.

**Efficacy and Safety of Olezarsen for the Management of Dyslipidemia: An Updated Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis of Randomized Controlled Trials**
Burhan M, Ibrahim T, Naveed MA, Ansari Z, Ansab M, Fatima K, et al.
*American Journal of Cardiology* | June 2026
[PMID 41887560](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41887560/) | DOI: [10.1016/j.amjcard.2026.03.052](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.amjcard.2026.03.052)

This updated meta-analysis pooled seven RCTs (n = 2,121) comparing olezarsen — Ionis' GalNAc-conjugated APOC3-targeting antisense oligonucleotide, marketed as Tryngolza for FCS — against placebo in patients with various forms of dyslipidemia. Treatment produced a mean reduction of 59.2 mg/dL in triglycerides, 64.2% reduction in ApoC-III, and a 31 mg/dL increase in HDL cholesterol, with no significant LDL effect — a lipid fingerprint consistent with the drug's APOC3 mechanism. The safety analysis is important context as Ionis awaits an FDA decision for sHTG: injection site reactions were nearly four times more frequent than placebo (RR 3.93), and thrombocytopenia (platelets <140,000/µL) was increased nearly five-fold (RR 4.76), though serious adverse events and liver enzyme elevations were comparable to placebo. The evidence was rated moderate to low certainty, and the authors appropriately call for long-term cardiovascular outcomes data — the critical gap in the field. This synthesis arrives at a strategically important moment, three weeks before Ionis' June 30 PDUFA date for the sHTG indication.

**A prognostic model based on metabolic indicators for recurrent acute pancreatitis in hypertriglyceridemia patients: a retrospective cohort study**
Wang Z, Liu Y, Ren P, Zhu G, Zhang G, Zhang X, et al.
*Annals of Medicine* | June 2026
[PMID 41978916](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41978916/) | DOI: [10.1186/s13017-025-00601-x](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13017-025-00601-x)

Hypertriglyceridemia-associated acute pancreatitis (HTG-AP) carries a 21.5% two-year recurrence rate in this single-center retrospective Chinese cohort of 293 patients. Using multivariate Cox regression, the authors identified visceral adipose tissue index and several other metabolic variables as independent predictors of recurrence and built a nomogram for risk stratification. Model performance was internally validated with bootstrap resampling and showed reasonable discrimination (C-index reported). While the study is limited by its retrospective, single-center design and modest sample size, it reinforces that SHTG is a recurrent disease requiring active long-term management beyond the acute episode — a context relevant to evaluating APOC3-targeting treatments not just for single episode prevention but for chronic risk reduction.

**\*Previously covered — update this cycle:\* Ionis Tryngolza sHTG FDA decision now 28 days out**
*BioPharma Dive* | Updated March 30, 2026 (context piece; PDUFA June 30 unchanged)
Source: [https://www.biopharmadive.com/news/fda-approval-decisions-watch-2026/808684/](https://www.biopharmadive.com/news/fda-approval-decisions-watch-2026/808684/)

The FDA is expected to decide on Ionis' supplemental NDA for olezarsen (Tryngolza) in severe hypertriglyceridemia by June 30, 2026. What has crystallized since prior coverage: Ionis revealed in late March that it would price sHTG Tryngolza at $40,000 per patient annually — a 93% cut from the $595,000 FCS list price — undercutting Arrowhead's Redemplo at $60,000/year. Analysts have described the pricing as targeting the "sweet spot" for aggressive payer contracting. The two Phase 3 CORE trials showed triglyceride reductions of 68–73% and significantly reduced pancreatitis risk. Demand for the FCS indication has been described by Ionis as "robust" with "no disruption" from Arrowhead's REDEMPLO launch.

*Why it matters for SHTG / lipid programs:* If approved June 30, Tryngolza would become the first APOC3 inhibitor cleared for a broad sHTG population in the U.S., opening a market Ionis projects could reach >$3 billion in peak sales.

The MUIR-3 phase 3 trial of plozasiran in severe hypertriglyceridemia has an estimated primary completion of May 2026. Arrowhead's Fiscal Q2 2026 results (May 7) noted that the European Commission decision on REDEMPLO for FCS was anticipated in Q2 2026, and that MUIR-3 sHTG readout would come "around the middle of 2026," with an NDA submission to follow if positive. The MUIR-3 result, when it lands, will be the next major data catalyst for the plozasiran vs. olezarsen competitive narrative.

*Why it matters:* If MUIR-3 data meet their endpoints, Arrowhead could file an sNDA for sHTG within months of Ionis' expected approval, setting up a direct head-to-head commercial contest.

## What to watch

The next four weeks are the most consequential in years for the SHTG treatment landscape. The FDA's June 30 decision on Tryngolza for sHTG will either validate or complicate Ionis' pivot to a high-volume commercial strategy at a dramatically lower price point. Simultaneously, the GPIHBP1 discovery in the human brain opens a new question for LPL biology: does LPL-GPIHBP1 signaling in oligodendrocytes contribute to neurological disease, and could ANGPTL3/4 modulation — which affects LPL activity — have unexplored CNS effects? On the clinical science side, the olezarsen meta-analysis gives the field a useful efficacy and safety benchmark just as the drug approaches its broadest indication yet; the thrombocytopenia signal at five-fold excess over placebo deserves close monitoring in the post-marketing sHTG setting. Watch for MUIR-3 plozasiran data from Arrowhead and the first commercial pricing dynamics once (if) the Ionis approval lands.
