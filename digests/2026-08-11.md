## Part A — SHTG / lipid science (PubMed)

*Note: the top-tier-journal search (NEJM, Lancet, JAMA, Nature, Cell, Science, Nat Med, Circulation, JACC, Eur Heart J, JCI, Cell Metab, Nat Metab, Circ Res, Lancet Diabetes Endocrinol) returned zero hits on SHTG/ANGPTL3/ANGPTL4/APOC3/LPL over the last 5 days. The search was broadened to all indexed journals; the four papers below are the most relevant hits from that broadened pool — mostly reviews and a case report rather than primary trial data.*

**ApoC-III Antisense Oligonucleotide Therapy Used in Glycogen Storage Disease Type 1a.** Burns J, Sutton VR, Zachariah JP. *JACC: Case Reports.* [PMID: 42569988](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42569988/) | [DOI: 10.1016/j.jaccas.2026.109561](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jaccas.2026.109561)
A case report of a 22-year-old with glycogen storage disease type 1a (GSD1a) whose hypertriglyceridemia — already refractory to fibrates, niacin, and bempedoic acid, with a prior pancreatitis episode — dropped from 4,951 to 1,643 mg/dL after starting an apoC-III antisense oligonucleotide (the same drug class as Ionis's olezarsen/Tryngolza). The authors frame this as the first reported use of apoC-III-ASO therapy in GSD1a-driven HTG. It's a single case, but it's a notable proof-of-concept that APOC3-targeted therapy can move triglycerides even in a genetic disease where the driver isn't APOC3 itself.

**Size-Resolved Lipoprotein Fatty Acid Content as a Novel Nuclear Magnetic Resonance-Derived Trait Specifically Associates with Genetic Variants That Control Fatty Acid Metabolism.** Belkadi A, Thareja G, Stephan N, Halama A, Suhre K. *Journal of Proteome Research.* [PMID: 42365607](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42365607/) | [DOI: 10.1021/acs.jproteome.6c00107](https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.jproteome.6c00107)
Using NMR lipoprotein profiling on 274,303 UK Biobank participants, the authors show that a derived "fatty acid content" variable (computed from triglyceride, phospholipid, and cholesteryl ester concentrations) correlates almost perfectly with directly measured fatty acid content, and that this derived trait has stronger genetic association signals than the underlying lipid measures at eight loci — five of which (LIPC, LIPG, PLB1, LPL, and APOC3) have direct roles in fatty acid metabolism. It's a methods/population-genetics paper, but it's a useful reminder that LPL and APOC3 remain the dominant genetic levers on lipoprotein fatty acid handling at biobank scale.

**Dietary Interventions for Hypertriglyceridemia.** Kirkpatrick CF, Petersen KS, Kris-Etherton PM, Maki KC. *Current Atherosclerosis Reports.* [PMID: 42572089](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42572089/) | [DOI: 10.1016/j.amjmed.2016.02.004]
A review of dietary management of HTG: it reiterates that most HTG is polygenic (multiple TG-raising variants) with rare monogenic causes at the extreme end, and that dietary restriction should scale with the severity of TG elevation — added sugars, alcohol, and total fat intake all matter, with partial replacement of carbohydrate by unsaturated fat or protein helping lower TG. Useful clinical refresher, no new data.

**Incretin-based therapies: Effects on plasma lipids and potential mechanisms.** Syed-Abdul MM, Lewis GF. *Pharmacology & Therapeutics.* [PMID: 42250734](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42250734/) | [DOI: 10.1016/j.pharmthera.2026.109057]
A mechanistic review of how GLP-1 receptor agonists, DPP-4 inhibitors, and dual GIP/GLP-1 agonists affect lipids. The most reproducible effect is blunted postprandial lipemia (lower triglycerides and apoB48 after meals), driven by both reduced intestinal lipoprotein output and enhanced clearance; the review specifically flags the apoC-III/LPL axis as one of the pathways implicated. Relevant background for anyone tracking how incretin drugs might complement — or compete for attention with — dedicated APOC3/ANGPTL3 therapies.

## Part B — Industry news (SHTG / Arrowhead / Alnylam / Ionis)

**Arrowhead acquires an FDA priority review voucher to speed its severe hypertriglyceridemia filing.** BioPharma Dive, published Aug. 7, 2026 (news roundup: "'Significant' demand for Revolution drug; Intellia pinpoints liver toxicity culprit"). [Source](https://www.biopharmadive.com/news/daraxonrasib-compassionate-use-intellia-arrowhead-cspc-vedanta/827186/)
Arrowhead Pharmaceuticals announced it has acquired a priority review voucher — without disclosing the price or the seller — that it intends to apply to its upcoming supplemental filing for plozasiran (marketed as Redemplo in FCS) in severe hypertriglyceridemia (sHTG). A priority review voucher shortens the FDA's target review clock from 10 months to 6. Arrowhead still expects to complete its sHTG submission by the end of 2026. The move comes weeks after Ionis's Tryngolza (olezarsen) won its own FDA label expansion into sHTG, so the voucher is a direct bid by Arrowhead to close the regulatory-timeline gap with its rival.
*Why it matters for SHTG / lipid programs:* This is a tactical, not clinical, development — but it's a concrete signal of how seriously Arrowhead is racing the clock against Ionis in the much larger sHTG market (~3 million U.S. patients) versus the rare-disease FCS indication where both drugs already compete.

## What to watch

The science this cycle is thin on primary data but consistent in theme: APOC3 and LPL remain the two genetic and pharmacological levers everyone keeps returning to, whether in a GSD1a case report, a UK Biobank genetics paper, or a review of incretin drugs' side effects on lipids. On the industry side, the real story to watch is the Aug. 30 ESC Congress late-breaker in Munich, where Arrowhead will present full SHASTA-3/SHASTA-4 data for plozasiran in sHTG — that, plus Arrowhead's new priority review voucher, sets up a fall filing race against an Ionis Tryngolza franchise that already has a head start in the clinic and on the label.
