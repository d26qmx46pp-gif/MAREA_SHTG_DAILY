**Efficacy and Safety of Evinacumab in Japanese Patients with Homozygous Familial Hypercholesterolemia: Long-term Results from an Open-label, Single-arm, Phase 3 Trial**
Koseki M, Makino H, Otsubo Y, Kawashiri MA, et al. *Journal of Atherosclerosis and Thrombosis*, July 1, 2026.
[PMID: 42386604](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42386604/) | [DOI: 10.5551/jat.66077](https://doi.org/10.5551/jat.66077)

This is a long-term extension of the pivotal ELIPSE HoFH trial, looking at evinacumab — an ANGPTL3-inhibiting monoclonal antibody — in 11 Japanese patients (9 adults, 2 adolescents) with homozygous familial hypercholesterolemia, a severe genetic disorder marked by extremely high LDL cholesterol. Patients received evinacumab infusions every 4 weeks on top of lipoprotein apheresis and other lipid-lowering therapy. LDL-C dropped by roughly 43% by week 24 and the effect held out to week 168 (over three years), with parallel reductions in triglycerides, Lp(a), and other lipid measures. Side effects were common but mostly unrelated to treatment, and no new safety signals emerged. It's a small, single-arm dataset, but it's a useful long-run confirmation that ANGPTL3 blockade holds up durably in a hard-to-treat population, which is relevant background for anyone tracking the broader ANGPTL3 franchise (evinacumab, and by extension RNAi/ASO approaches against the same target).

**Triglyceride/HDL-Cholesterol Ratio as a Predictor for Treatment-Related Severe Hypertriglyceridemia in Children with Lymphoid Malignancies**
Małecka A, Irga-Jaworska N, Wieczorek-Breitzke E, et al. *Scientific Reports*, July 2, 2026.
[PMID: 42393108](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42393108/) | [DOI: 10.1038/s41598-026-59133-2](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-026-59133-2)

Small case-control study (37 pediatric patients) looking at why some kids being treated for lymphoid cancers with steroids plus PEG-asparaginase develop severe treatment-related hypertriglyceridemia while others don't. The TG/HDL-C ratio measured just seven days after the first asparaginase dose turned out to be a strong predictor (AUC 0.85) of who would go on to develop severe HTG, with each one-unit rise in the ratio associated with an 81% increase in odds. It's a modest, single-population dataset and needs larger prospective validation, but it's a simple, cheap early-warning tool that oncology teams could plausibly use now to flag kids who need closer triglyceride monitoring or earlier intervention.
