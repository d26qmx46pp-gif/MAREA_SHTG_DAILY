**The Approval of Redemplo for Familial Chylomicronemia Syndrome and the Many Flavors of GalNAc-Oligonucleotides**
Alterman JF, Gross KY, Khvorova A. *Nucleic Acid Therapeutics*, June 2026.
[PMID 41877501](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41877501/) | DOI: 10.1177/21593337261435980

Plozasiran (Redemplo), developed by Arrowhead Pharmaceuticals, became the eighth approved siRNA therapeutic and the third oligonucleotide-based medicine approved for familial chylomicronemia syndrome (FCS), targeting apolipoprotein C-III (APOC3). The authors situate Redemplo's arrival within the broader evolution of GalNAc-conjugated delivery—the liver-targeting system that routes siRNA drugs to hepatocytes and has now yielded seven approved products. This perspective dissects how plozasiran represents a third distinct "flavor" of GalNAc architecture to reach the clinic, distinguishing it from Alnylam's inclisiran and earlier siRNA chemistries. For those tracking the SHTG drug landscape, this paper is a useful primer on the chemical and pharmacological lineage of the entire class: it clarifies what differentiates GalNAc-siRNA from GalNAc-ASO platforms (such as those underpinning Ionis's olezarsen), with implications for dosing intervals, immunogenicity, and manufacturing scalability. The review also situates plozasiran as the first quarterly-dosed APOC3-targeting agent approved in the US.

**Deep Chemical Structure Graph Learning Deciphers the Lipotoxicity Code of Hypertriglyceridemic Pancreatitis**
Huang A, Yuan Q, Chen J, Wang L, Yu Y. *NPJ Digital Medicine*, May 27, 2026.
[PMID 42204286](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42204286/) | DOI: 10.1038/s41746-026-02792-2

A longstanding clinical puzzle in SHTG is that serum triglyceride levels correlate poorly with who actually develops acute pancreatitis — some patients with TG above 2,000 mg/dL never develop AP, while others with lower levels do. This paper from a Chinese group introduces DeepLipiDecipher, a graph neural network that integrates lipid chemical structures with metabolic network topology to identify latent lipotoxic features beyond raw TG mass. Trained and tested on 433 subjects (including those with hyperlipidemic acute pancreatitis, or HLAP, and those with HTG alone), the model achieved an AUC of 0.810 for distinguishing the HLAP phenotype — outperforming conventional machine learning approaches. The key interpretability finding is striking: HLAP susceptibility correlated not with total lipid burden but with a structural lipid profile enriched in polyunsaturated and ether-linked phospholipids. Causal inference further implicated an SMPD3-ceramide-PTGS2 axis, where ceramide accumulation potentiates peroxidation of vulnerable lipid species to trigger systemic inflammation. If validated prospectively, this framework could be transformative for triaging SHTG patients at admission — identifying the subset who urgently need triglyceride-lowering (or even plasmapheresis) versus those who can be managed conservatively.

**Lipidomic Profiling of Plasma Triglyceride Species Reveals Distinct Signatures and Dietary Associations in Drug-Refractory Hypertriglyceridemia**
Cheng C-N, Wang W-C, Chen P-L, Jhan S-J, Chang W-C. *Clinical Nutrition*, June 2026.
[PMID 42048979](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42048979/) | DOI: 10.1016/j.clnu.2026.106664

This Taiwanese study tackled a clinically underappreciated subset of HTG: patients whose triglycerides remain dangerously elevated despite diet and medications. Using LC-MS lipidomics in 140 participants (100 HTG patients, 40 normal controls), the authors found that the proportional composition of individual TG species — not total TG — distinguished drug-refractory HTG (r-HTG) from non-refractory HTG. Of 251 TG species profiled, 117 differed significantly between groups, with r-HTG enriched in shorter-chain, more saturated species (fewer double bonds, lower carbon number). A four-species TG panel achieved an AUROC of 0.953 for identifying r-HTG, rising to 0.963 when genetic data were added, far outperforming a drug-use-only model (AUROC 0.717). Dietary analysis showed r-HTG patients consumed fried breakfasts more frequently and less vegetarian food — consistent with higher saturated fatty acid intake driving the distinct lipidomic signature. These findings have practical implications: total TG monitoring may miss patients with pathological TG species profiles, and lipidomics could eventually guide dietary counseling and drug selection in the SHTG clinic, particularly for patients who appear stable by conventional lipid panels but remain at pancreatitis risk.

**Assessment of Pediatric Hypertriglyceridemia Etiology: Insights from Next-Generation Sequencing Panels and Identification of Novel Variants**
Anlas O, Bulut FD. *Biochemical Genetics*, June 2026.
[PMID 40773003](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40773003/) | DOI: 10.1542/pir.2016-0138

This Turkish single-center study screened 26 pediatric patients with primary hypertriglyceridemia (12 of them severe) using a 25-gene NGS panel encompassing LPL, APOC2, APOA5, LMF1, APOE, GPIHBP1, ANGPTL3, APOC3, and a range of other lipid regulators. Pathogenic or likely pathogenic variants were found in 58% of severe HTG cases, with novel mutations in LPL, GPD1, GPIHBP1, APOC2, and LIPC. Notably, 16 of the 20 total variants detected were novel, expanding the known mutation landscape for these genes. The yield was lower in less-severe HTG (3 of 14), reinforcing the recommendation to prioritize genetic testing in children presenting with TG above 500 mg/dL, a family history, or complications such as pancreatitis. For SHTG researchers, this paper underscores the molecular heterogeneity of the condition even within the pediatric SHTG gene set — and highlights that diagnostic panels now need to capture rare ANGPTL3 and APOC3 variants alongside the classic LPL/APOA5 targets. Personalized treatment and family cascade screening depend on identifying the causative gene.

## Part B — Industry news (SHTG / Arrowhead / Alnylam / Ionis)

**Ionis and GSK Unveil Landmark Hepatitis B Phase 3 Data — ASO Platform Validated Again**
*BioPharma Dive* — May 28, 2026
Source: [https://www.biopharmadive.com/news/gsk-ionis-bepirovirsen-hepatitis-b-nejm-study-results/821341/](https://www.biopharmadive.com/news/gsk-ionis-bepirovirsen-hepatitis-b-nejm-study-results/821341/)

Ionis and its partner GSK announced Phase 3 results for bepirovirsen, an investigational antisense oligonucleotide for chronic hepatitis B (CHB), on May 28. Two trials (B-Well 1 and B-Well 2) simultaneously published in the New England Journal of Medicine showed a 19% functional cure rate in the overall population and 26% in patients with lower baseline viral burden — compared to 0% on placebo. Functional cure — defined as undetectable hepatitis B surface antigen and viral DNA for at least 24 weeks after stopping all treatment — currently occurs in under 1% of patients on standard-of-care antivirals. The FDA is reviewing a bepirovirsen application with breakthrough designation; a decision is expected by October 26. Analysts called the data a "historic moment" for hepatitis B and projected blockbuster commercial potential.

*Why it matters for SHTG / lipid programs:* Bepirovirsen is not a lipid drug, but it is the same GalNAc-ASO delivery chemistry that underpins Tryngolza (olezarsen). This landmark Phase 3 validation—published in the NEJM—demonstrates the manufacturing and clinical reliability of Ionis's ASO platform across therapeutic areas, providing additional confidence for the company's lipid franchise as the Tryngolza sHTG FDA decision deadline of June 30 approaches.

## What to Watch

The most significant near-term binary event in this space remains the Ionis Tryngolza sHTG FDA decision, due by June 30, 2026. If approved, Tryngolza will become the first drug labeled specifically for non-FCS severe hypertriglyceridemia in the US — a market orders of magnitude larger than FCS. Ionis has already pre-emptively cut Tryngolza's price from $595,000 to $40,000 per year for this indication, undercutting Arrowhead's Redemplo ($60,000) and signaling an aggressive formulary access strategy. Arrowhead, meanwhile, expects to report its own sHTG Phase 3 (PALISADE) data around mid-2026 and, if positive, file promptly. On the science side, the DeepLipiDecipher paper this week opens a new question: should SHTG trials stratify patients by lipidomic risk profile rather than TG level alone? If individual TG species — not total TG — predict pancreatitis, the field may need to rethink both how clinical endpoints are defined and how responders are identified in future drug trials.
