## Part A — SHTG / lipid science (PubMed)

*Note: no papers in the target journal tier (NEJM, Lancet, JAMA, Nature/Nat Med, Circulation, JACC, Eur Heart J, JCI, Cell Metab, Nat Metab, Circ Res, Lancet Diabetes Endocrinol) matched this cycle's search. The filter was broadened to all journals within the 5-day window; the three papers below are the most relevant of that broader pull.*

**Clinical Pharmacology of Plozasiran.** Huston J, Orey D, Corona K, et al. *American Journal of Cardiovascular Drugs.* [PMID: 42340592](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42340592/) | [DOI: 10.1007/s40256-026-00808-6](https://doi.org/10.1007/s40256-026-00808-6)

A pharmacology review walking through what's known about plozasiran, Arrowhead's siRNA drug that knocks down hepatic APOC3 production to lower triglycerides. It lays out the pharmacokinetic and pharmacodynamic data generated so far, reiterates the mechanism (apoC-III suppression relieves inhibition of lipoprotein lipase-mediated triglyceride clearance), and frames the drug's relevance for both familial chylomicronemia syndrome and the broader severe hypertriglyceridemia population. No new trial data — this is a synthesis of the existing plozasiran evidence base, including the SHASTA and PALISADE programs. Useful as a single-source refresher on where the plozasiran clinical package stands.

**Olezarsen (Tryngolza) for severe hypertriglyceridemia.** *The Medical Letter on Drugs and Therapeutics.* [PMID: 42628981](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42628981/) | [DOI: 10.58347/tml.2026.1762c](https://doi.org/10.58347/tml.2026.1762c)

The Medical Letter's independent drug review of Tryngolza (olezarsen) now that it's approved for the broader sHTG indication (not just FCS). These reviews typically summarize the pivotal trial data, dosing, adverse-event profile, and cost relative to alternatives, and carry weight with prescribers because Medical Letter reviews are seen as free of promotional influence. No abstract is indexed on PubMed (Medical Letter items don't carry one), so the annotation here is based on the title and publication context.

**Discovery of functional variants regulating lipid metabolism in an East Asian population.** Moon S, Park YJ, Choi J, et al. *Metabolism.* [PMID: 42636954](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42636954/) | [DOI: 10.1016/j.metabol.2026.156751](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.metabol.2026.156751)

A GWAS of triglycerides, LDL-C, and HDL-C in 72,298 Korean participants that turned up 182 independent lipid-associated signals (64 previously unreported). Fine-mapping and variant-to-gene mapping prioritized 24 regulatory variants across 40 genes, and functional follow-up with luciferase reporter assays in HepG2 cells confirmed allele-specific activity for two of them. Most notably for this audience: the study identified a Korean-enriched ANGPTL3 missense variant (rs753849210, p.W338C) among the low-frequency, protein-altering signals implicating established lipid pathways — a population-specific addition to the ANGPTL3 loss-of-function literature that's been a major source of therapeutic rationale in this space.
