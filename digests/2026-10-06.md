**Deficiency of the Lipoprotein Lipase Pathway Modifies the Phenotypic Expression of Genetically Determined Lipoprotein(a)**
Tsimikas S, Yang X, Waldman E, Lyu F, Alexander VJ, Marcovina SM. *Journal of Lipid Research*, Oct 5, 2026.
PMID: [42833459](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42833459/) · DOI: [10.1016/j.jlr.2026.101164](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jlr.2026.101164)

Using 66 genetically confirmed FCS patients from the Phase 3 Balance trial of olezarsen (triglycerides ≥880 mg/dL), the authors compared Lp(a), apo(a) isoforms and oxidized phospholipid (OxPL) biomarkers with 66 age- and sex-matched moderate-HTG patients from Essence-TIMI 73b. Median Lp(a) didn't differ significantly (9.8 vs 11.8 nmol/L), but the FCS distribution was shifted down, with fewer people at high Lp(a), even though apo(a) isoform sizes were similar. Within the same isoform-size bands, Lp(a) was much lower in FCS (e.g., <17 KIV repeats: 67 vs 175 nmol/L). OxPL-apoB was lower in FCS and the OxPL-apo(a)/OxPL-apoB ratio was about 70% lower, which points to a shift in how OxPL is carried across apoB lipoproteins. Olezarsen had no consistent effect on Lp(a) or OxPL markers. The conclusion is that losing LPL-pathway function changes how genetically set Lp(a) shows up in plasma. That's a new link between TRL metabolism and Lp(a) biology. Note: the senior author, Sam Tsimikas, leads global cardiovascular development at Ionis.

**LPL-Positive Endothelial Cells Control T-cell Homing in Liver Metastasis**
Zhang X, Kamiyama M, Tulessin M, … Mogler C, Singhal M, Augustin HG. *Cancer Discovery*, Oct 1, 2026.
PMID: [42474324](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42474324/) · DOI: [10.1158/2159-8290.CD-25-1411](https://doi.org/10.1158/2159-8290.CD-25-1411)

This is a mechanism paper from outside the lipid field. In liver metastasis models, the authors found a tumor endothelial cell subpopulation that strongly expresses lipoprotein lipase and helps activated CD8+ T cells home into tumors, which drives metastatic regression. They show that LPL increases MHC-I–dependent cross-presentation of tumor antigens on endothelial cells, which makes those vessels "visible" to T cells. In human liver metastasis samples, intratumoral LPL+ vessels correlated with T-cell accumulation. It's oncology, not triglycerides, but it adds a non-lipolytic, immunological job for endothelial LPL. That's worth knowing for anyone thinking about the vascular biology of the LPL complex.
