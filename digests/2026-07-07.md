## Part A — SHTG / lipid science (PubMed)

**Residual lipid risk in atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease.**
Nordestgaard BG, Hegele RA. *European Heart Journal* (July 7, 2026). [PMID: 41701506](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41701506/) | [DOI: 10.1093/eurheartj/ehag087](https://doi.org/10.1093/eurheartj/ehag087)

This is a review, not a primary study, so it's the lone top-tier hit this cycle — flagged here rather than padded with lower-tier papers. Nordestgaard and Hegele lay out the case that LDL-lowering alone leaves meaningful cardiovascular risk on the table, and that remnant cholesterol (the cholesterol carried in triglyceride-rich particles) and Lp(a) are the other two independent drivers of atherosclerosis. They argue apoB and non-HDL cholesterol are useful summary numbers but can obscure which of the three pathways is actually driving a given patient's risk, since remnants, Lp(a), and LDL cause disease through different mechanisms. The triglyceride content of remnant particles is highlighted as a plausible contributor to arterial inflammation and plaque instability, independent of straightforward cholesterol deposition. Practically, the review frames this as the rationale for the current wave of ANGPTL3, ANGPTL4, and apoC-III-directed therapies: they're aimed squarely at the remnant/triglyceride axis that LDL-focused drugs don't touch. Keywords list ANGPTL proteins, apoB, apoC-III, FGF21, pancreatitis, and triglyceride-rich lipoproteins, though the abstract itself stays at the level of a clinical overview rather than new mechanistic data.

## What to watch

The Nordestgaard/Hegele review is a useful marker of where the field's head is at: LDL is increasingly seen as necessary-but-not-sufficient, and the triglyceride/remnant axis — exactly what olezarsen, plozasiran, and the ANGPTL3 programs are built to hit — is being framed as the next major lever. On the industry side, the real test now is commercial: Tryngolza's sHTG launch is underway following the June approval, and the next things worth watching are early prescription/uptake data, any competitive response from Arrowhead's Redemplo, and the pelacarsen Lp(a) HORIZON cardiovascular outcomes readout, which is still tracking for a 2026 release and would be a landmark event for the broader non-LDL lipid space once it lands.
