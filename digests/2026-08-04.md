**Angiopoietin-like protein 3 complete and partial deficiency markedly accelerates apolipoprotein B48 and B100 metabolism in triglyceride-rich lipoproteins in humans**
Arca M, Björnson E, D'Erasmo L, et al. *Journal of Internal Medicine*, 2026.
[PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42273920/) | [DOI: 10.1111/joim.70124](https://doi.org/10.1111/joim.70124)

This is a kinetic-tracer study in humans with natural ANGPTL3 loss-of-function mutations — three people with essentially no circulating ANGPTL3, four with roughly half-normal levels, and ten matched controls. The headline finding: complete ANGPTL3 deficiency doesn't slow down production of chylomicrons or VLDL, it massively speeds up their clearance — particles that normally circulate for hours are gone in minutes, because unrestrained lipoprotein lipase chews through them so fast. That accelerated lipolysis also reshapes the LDL that eventually forms: it ends up triglyceride-enriched, cholesterol-depleted, and cleared faster than normal. Partial (heterozygous) deficiency showed a milder version of the same effect on chylomicrons and VLDL, with LDL kinetics staying normal. For anyone tracking ANGPTL3-directed drugs (evinacumab and the various antisense/siRNA candidates), this is useful reassurance and useful caution in the same package: it mechanistically explains why blocking ANGPTL3 lowers triglycerides so effectively, but also flags that the downstream LDL particles in deep-deficiency states are compositionally unusual, which is worth keeping an eye on in long-term safety follow-up.
