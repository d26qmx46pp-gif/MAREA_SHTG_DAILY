## Part A — SHTG / lipid science (PubMed)

Three substantive review articles appeared today (June 5) in *Circulation Research* as part of what appears to be a focused collection on lipoprotein biology and emerging therapies. All are new to this digest.

---

**New and Emerging Therapeutic Targets for ApoB-Containing Particles Lowering**
Gaudet D, Larouche M, Raal FJ, Catapano AL. *Circulation Research.* 2026 Jun 5.
[PMID 42241517](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42241517/) | DOI: [10.1161/CIRCRESAHA.126.327270](https://doi.org/10.1161/CIRCRESAHA.126.327270)

This review argues for a shift from an LDL-centric to an apoB-centric framework for managing atherogenic lipoproteins. ApoB is the structural scaffold shared by all atherogenic particles — from VLDL and LDL to chylomicron remnants and Lp(a) — and plasma apoB concentration tracks the total particle burden more accurately than LDL-C alone. The authors give particular attention to the apo B48-containing chylomicron axis and its role in severe hypertriglyceridemia and pancreatitis risk, then survey the emerging armamentarium: ANGPTL3 inhibition (evinacumab, zodasiran), APOC3 targeting, MTP inhibition, and gene-editing approaches. The case is made that RNA-based and gene-targeted strategies targeting the angiopoietin-like protein axis and apo CIII simultaneously shrink both atherosclerotic cardiovascular risk and triglyceride-mediated pancreatitis risk, arguing these should be viewed as a unified therapeutic opportunity rather than separate problems.

---

**Triglyceride-Rich Lipoprotein Metabolism and Cardiovascular Risk in Diabetes: Bedside to Bench to Bedside**
Ginsberg HN, Bornfeldt KE. *Circulation Research.* 2026 Jun 5.
[PMID 42241511](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42241511/) | DOI: [10.1161/CIRCRESAHA.125.327267](https://doi.org/10.1161/CIRCRESAHA.125.327267)

A clinician-scientist duo walks through six annotated patient cases to illustrate how dysregulated TRL (triglyceride-rich lipoprotein) metabolism amplifies cardiovascular risk in both type 1 and type 2 diabetes, above and beyond traditional risk factors. Type 2 diabetes drives higher hepatic VLDL secretion, increased intestinal chylomicron output, and impaired remnant clearance — and when these defects stack on top of genetic variants in LPL, APOC3, ANGPTL3, ANGPTL4, or ANGPTL8, the result is compounded TRL accumulation and markedly elevated ASCVD risk. The authors note that cardiovascular outcome trials targeting triglyceride levels alone have been "mostly futile" and pivot to the argument that the field must now focus on trials targeting the proteins governing TRL clearance. The careful design of inclusion/exclusion criteria for such trials — to enrich for patients with genuine TRL-driven risk — is flagged as the central methodological challenge ahead.

---

**Translating Lipoprotein Genetics Into New Therapies**
Hegele RA. *Circulation Research.* 2026 Jun 5.
[PMID 42241509](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42241509/) | DOI: [10.1161/CIRCRESAHA.126.328828](https://doi.org/10.1161/CIRCRESAHA.126.328828)

Robert Hegele surveys how human genetics became the primary validation engine for lipid drug targets. The "human knockout paradigm" — individuals with lifelong loss-of-function variants who are inadvertently running the proof-of-concept trial — has now yielded approvals for PCSK9 inhibitors (LDL-C lowering with CV benefit), APOC3-targeted agents (olezarsen and plozasiran, both now approved for familial chylomicronemia syndrome/FCS), and ANGPTL3 inhibition (evinacumab in homozygous FH). Notably, Mendelian randomization prospectively identified HDL-C as a noncausal biomarker, correctly predicting the failure of HDL-raising therapy in outcomes trials before those trials ran. GalNAc-conjugated siRNA and ASO platforms are highlighted as the pragmatic translation layer for converting validated genetic targets into infrequently dosed therapies. Cardiovascular outcomes trials for APOC3- and ANGPTL3-targeted RNA agents are pending or unannounced, and gene-editing approaches toward permanent intervention are presented as the next frontier.

## Part B — Industry news (SHTG / Arrowhead / Alnylam / Ionis)

This was a quiet cycle for breaking news on the allowed publications. No stories published in the last 5 days specifically featured SHTG, Arrowhead, Alnylam, or Ionis. Two relevant context pieces provide useful background:

**Context piece (May 7, 2026) — Arrowhead bets on plozasiran, drops zodasiran**
[Fierce Biotech](https://www.fiercebiotech.com/biotech/arrowhead-axes-1-heart-disease-drug-goes-all-another-pipeline-progress-drives-costs)

Alongside its fiscal Q2 2026 results (quarter ended March 31), Arrowhead officially discontinued zodasiran — its ANGPTL3-targeted siRNA — and consolidated resources behind plozasiran (APOC3-targeted, branded REDEMPLO). CMO Bruce Given attributed the decision partly to the crowded LDL space: "There's actually a lot of drug availability over on the LDL side now." By contrast, he said, the treatment landscape for high triglycerides remains "a relative desert." Arrowhead's One-REDEMPLO unified pricing model pegs the annual list price at $60,000 across both FCS and — if approved — sHTG. Phase 3 SHASTA-3 and SHASTA-4 results in sHTG are due around mid-2026.

**Context piece (April 29, 2026) — Ionis raises Tryngolza peak sales guidance to >$3B, PDUFA date set**
[Ionis Q1 2026 earnings / SEC filing](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0000874015/000114036126017637/ef20071749_ex99-1.htm)

Ionis reported that its olezarsen sNDA for sHTG was accepted by the FDA for Priority Review, with a PDUFA target action date of **June 30, 2026**. The company raised peak net sales guidance for Tryngolza in sHTG from >$2B to >$3B and confirmed $27M in Q1 Tryngolza net product sales in FCS (up from $6M in Q1 2025). The EMA also accepted an indication extension application for sHTG in March. Ionis has set the annual list price at $40,000 for the sHTG indication — below Arrowhead's $60,000 REDEMPLO price.

## What to watch

The Circulation Research trio published today reads as a coordinated argument: ApoB thinking should replace LDL thinking, TRL dysregulation is an underappreciated ASCVD risk driver (especially in diabetes), and genetics-to-RNA-therapy translation has worked for APOC3 and ANGPTL3 — the question now is whether it will produce cardiovascular outcomes benefit, not just biomarker-level TG lowering. These themes converge directly on the next critical industry milestone: the FDA's June 30 PDUFA date for Tryngolza in sHTG, which would mark the first approval of an RNA-targeted drug for a broad (non-rare) cardiometabolic indication. Arrowhead's SHASTA-3 and SHASTA-4 readouts — also expected in mid-June — will determine whether plozasiran can mount a competitive NDA filing for sHTG later this year. The next two to three weeks may be the most eventful stretch this field has seen.
