## Part A — SHTG / lipid science (PubMed)

Only one hit from the top-tier journal list (NEJM, Lancet, JAMA, Nature, Cell, Science, Nat Med, Circulation, JACC, Eur Heart J, JCI, Cell Metab, Nat Metab, Circ Res, Lancet Diabetes Endocrinol) in the last 5 days — but it's a notable one and not a padding pick.

**Durability of CRISPR-Cas9 Gene Editing Targeting ANGPTL3 with CTX310.**
Luke J. Laffin, Stephen J. Nicholls, Russell S. Scott, et al. *New England Journal of Medicine*. [PMID: 42663980](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42663980/) | DOI: [10.1056/NEJMc2609825](https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMc2609825)

This is a correspondence-format follow-up (not a full original article) from the CRISPR Therapeutics/Cleveland Clinic team reporting longer-term durability of CTX310, their in vivo CRISPR-Cas9 base/gene-editing therapy targeting ANGPTL3 in hepatocytes. It builds on the Phase 1a data the company has been presenting through 2026: a single IV dose produced deep, sustained reductions in ANGPTL3 protein along with durable lowering of both triglycerides and LDL cholesterol out to one year, with no new dose-limiting toxicities reported. PubMed doesn't carry a structured abstract for this letter format, so exact per-dose numbers aren't in the indexed record, but the accompanying press materials describe mean one-year ANGPTL3 reductions in the roughly 80% range at the highest dose tested, with parallel triglyceride and LDL drops. For anyone tracking ANGPTL3 as a target, this is the first NEJM-level evidence that a one-time in vivo edit can hold up for a full year — directly relevant to whether gene editing becomes a viable "one-and-done" alternative to chronic antisense/siRNA dosing (Arrowhead's zodasiran, Alnylam's ANGPTL3 programs) discussed in Part B below.

## Part B — Industry news (SHTG / Arrowhead / Alnylam / Ionis)

**At ESC Congress, Arrowhead makes its case that plozasiran has "an edge" over Ionis's Tryngolza.**
BioPharma Dive, published August 31, 2026. Byline: Ben Fidler. [Source](https://www.biopharmadive.com/news/esc-congress-2026-results-eplontersen-alnylam-cytokinetics/829139/)

At the European Society of Cardiology Congress in Munich, Arrowhead presented fuller details from its Phase 3 SHASTA-3 and SHASTA-4 trials of plozasiran (Redemplo) in severe hypertriglyceridemia. To multiple Wall Street analysts, the data described a drug with efficacy comparable to Ionis's Tryngolza but a potentially better safety profile — specifically fewer treatment discontinuations and smaller liver-fat/liver-enzyme increases. Among patients who underwent specialized liver imaging, plozasiran recipients showed a roughly 1.5% placebo-adjusted increase in liver fat, versus 2–4% for Tryngolza recipients in Ionis's own studies, though the two drugs haven't been tested head-to-head. Arrowhead intends to file for FDA approval in sHTG by year-end, setting up a possible 2027 launch; one analyst estimated plozasiran could capture 60% of a market opportunity reaching $6 billion by 2035. The same article also covered a separate, non-lipid story involving Alnylam and Ionis at the same conference — a failed Phase 3 study of AstraZeneca/Ionis's eplontersen in transthyretin amyloid cardiomyopathy, which is flagged here only because it names two of the tracked companies, not because it touches SHTG, APOC3, or ANGPTL3 work.
*Why it matters for SHTG / lipid programs:* This is the direct follow-through on last cycle's "what to watch" — the first substantive analyst read on how plozasiran stacks up against Tryngolza now that both have late-stage sHTG data in hand, right as Arrowhead heads toward an FDA filing.

## What to watch

The throughline this cycle is durability and differentiation. On the science side, CTX310's one-year NEJM data is an early signal that ANGPTL3-directed gene editing could eventually compete with chronic dosing regimens — though it's still Phase 1a and far from an approved therapy. On the industry side, Arrowhead and Ionis are now both sitting on Phase 3 sHTG data and the fight is shifting from "does it work" to "which is safer and more convenient," with liver fat/liver enzyme changes emerging as the key differentiator analysts are watching. Expect more granular head-to-head framing as Arrowhead's FDA filing approaches by year-end.
