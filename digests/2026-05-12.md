## Part A — SHTG / lipid science (PubMed)

*Note: no top-tier journal hits (NEJM, Lancet, JAMA, Nature, Cell, Science, Circulation, Cell Metab, Eur Heart J, Nat Med, Nat Cardiovasc Res, Circ Res, Lancet Diabetes Endocrinol, JACC, JCI, Nat Metab) met the bar this cycle. Showing the strongest next-tier hits.*

**Efficacy and Safety of Olezarsen in Hypertriglyceridemia: An Updated Meta-Analysis of Randomised Controlled Trials.**
Emara A, Mansour A, Aboeldahab H, Awashra A, et al. *Diabetes, Obesity & Metabolism*, June 2026. [PubMed 41943994](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41943994/) · [DOI 10.1111/dom.70716](https://doi.org/10.1111/dom.70716)
An updated meta-analysis pooled six RCTs and 2,676 patients to put numbers on what olezarsen does in hypertriglyceridemia. The 50 mg and 80 mg doses cut triglycerides by 47% and 54%, respectively, with ApoC-III suppression in the 63–67% range and non-HDL-C reductions of 21–25%. Most importantly for the SHTG audience, the pancreatitis signal held up: relative risks of 0.16 and 0.33 across the two doses. Toxicology was reassuring overall, but ALT/AST elevations ≥3× ULN were ~2.5× more frequent at the 80 mg dose, which is something to keep an eye on as broader-population use approaches.

**Efficacy and Safety of Apolipoprotein C-III Inhibitors in Hypertriglyceridemia: A Network Meta-Analysis of Randomised Controlled Trials.**
Elbahloul MA, Gamal A, Maihoub O, Elbaz Hassan MM, et al. *Diabetes, Obesity & Metabolism*, June 2026. [PubMed 41917770](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41917770/) · [DOI 10.1111/dom.70717](https://doi.org/10.1111/dom.70717)
A frequentist network meta-analysis of 15 RCTs (3,934 patients) lined volanesorsen, olezarsen, and plozasiran up head-to-head against placebo on TG-lowering and pancreatitis. Olezarsen 80 mg Q4W came out on top by SUCRA ranking (MD −63%) for TG reduction; volanesorsen 100 mg Q1W and olezarsen 10 mg Q4W were the only doses that failed to significantly cut TGs. Across agents, the pancreatitis HR was 0.16, and no agent significantly increased serious adverse events. This is the cleanest indirect-comparison evidence yet that the class effect on pancreatitis is real and large.

**Promise of ANGPTL3 as a therapeutic target for controlling cholesterol levels.**
Hegele RA. *Expert Opinion on Therapeutic Targets*, 7 May 2026. [PubMed 42095752](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42095752/) · [DOI 10.1080/14728222.2026.2671686](https://doi.org/10.1080/14728222.2026.2671686)
Rob Hegele's expert review pushes back, gently, on the framing of ANGPTL3 as primarily a cholesterol target. He walks through the genetic phenotype, the four pharmacologic modalities now in the clinic (evinacumab, vupanorsen, zodasiran/solbinsiran, and the VERVE-201 / CTX310 base-editing programs), and argues that the strongest signal across trials is on triglyceride-rich lipoproteins and remnants, with LDL-C effects being context-dependent and tied to baseline triglyceride burden. The implicit message — pick your patients carefully — is directly relevant to anyone modeling ANGPTL3 inhibition in mixed dyslipidemia or SHTG-leaning populations.

**Associations of ANGPTL3/4/8 proteins and complexes with heart failure and heart failure mortality.**
Silbernagel G, Maas AC, Li H, Lemen D, et al. *Lipids in Health and Disease*, 7 May 2026. [PubMed 42098711](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42098711/) · [DOI 10.1186/s12944-026-02973-8](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12944-026-02973-8)
In 2,394 LURIC participants followed a median of ~10 years, ANGPTL3 and CD-ANGPTL4 were positively associated with NT-proBNP, HFrEF and HFpEF, NYHA class, and HF mortality (101 deaths); ANGPTL4/8 complex tracked with HF phenotype but not mortality. CD-ANGPTL4 showed the strongest mortality signal. The headline for lipid-target programs is that ANGPTL inhibition may need to be evaluated in HF subpopulations not just for safety but as a potentially meaningful efficacy axis — though this is an observational, calorically-sensitive biomarker story and causality is unclear.

**SPP2 as an HNF4A target gene regulating triglyceride homeostasis via LPL activation.**
Wen Y, Liu X, Wang M, Li C, et al. *Biochemical and Biophysical Research Communications*, 25 June 2026 (early). [PubMed 42030881](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42030881/) · [DOI 10.1016/j.bbrc.2026.153778](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.bbrc.2026.153778)
A mechanism paper worth flagging because it identifies a new endogenous LPL activator. Recombinant SPP2 protein and AAV8-mediated hepatic SPP2 overexpression selectively lower serum triglycerides in mice; mechanistically, SPP2 binds LPL directly, enhances VLDL hydrolysis, and modulates LPL cofactor expression while also driving hepatic β-oxidation and ketogenesis. The regulatory loop is interesting too: fasting-induced free fatty acids activate HNF4A, which in turn drives SPP2 transcription, defining an HNF4A–SPP2–LPL fasting axis. Conceptually similar real estate to ANGPTL3/4/8 modulation, but with an LPL-activating rather than LPL-disinhibiting logic.

**RNA interference for lipid disorders: is this the future?**
Nassif M, Fazoli RTJ, Generoso G, et al. *Current Opinion in Lipidology*, 1 June 2026. [PubMed 41762752](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41762752/) · [DOI 10.1097/MOL.0000000000001033](https://doi.org/10.1097/MOL.0000000000001033)
A compact review covering the state of siRNA in lipidology — inclisiran's durable ~50% LDL-C reduction, plus the wave of siRNAs aimed at Lp(a), ApoC-III, and ANGPTL3 that are now in late-stage development. Useful as a primer or one-pager for non-specialist colleagues; the authors land where the field has been landing for a year: outcomes data and cost-effectiveness analyses are the rate-limiting step from here.

## What to watch

The single biggest forward-looking variable this cycle remains Tryngolza's sHTG PDUFA on June 30 and the matching Arrowhead Redemplo Phase 3 SHTG readout, both due in the next ~7 weeks. On the science side, watch whether the network-meta-analytic pancreatitis signal — class HR ~0.16 — survives once the first real-world post-approval pancreatitis cohorts in sHTG (versus the FCS-only experience to date) start hitting the literature, and whether HNF4A–SPP2–LPL-style endogenous LPL activators surface as serious tractable targets. The ANGPTL3-as-HF-biomarker story is also worth tracking as the ANGPTL3 modality wave (siRNA, ASO, base editing) reaches outcomes-trial maturity.
