## Part A — SHTG / lipid science (PubMed)

**Heterozygosity for pathogenic variants in familial chylomicronemia syndrome genes: from carrier state to complex trait.** Hegele RA. *Current Opinion in Lipidology*, 2026. [PubMed 42308165](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42308165/) | [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1097/MOL.0000000000001048)
This review from Robert Hegele synthesizes 18 months of evidence on carriers of a single pathogenic variant in the five canonical FCS genes — LPL, GPIHBP1, APOA5, APOC2, and LMF1 — as opposed to the biallelic carriers who develop classic FCS. Longitudinal data on LPL and APOA5 heterozygotes show triglyceride phenotypes that vary enormously, both between people and within the same person over time, ranging from entirely normal to severely elevated. Heterozygosity for these variants accounts for an estimated 15–25% of "multifactorial chylomicronemia syndrome" cases, where a single rare variant combines with polygenic and environmental triglyceride-raising factors to produce a chylomicronemia phenotype. The practical takeaway is that genetic testing panels built only to find biallelic FCS cases will miss a meaningful chunk of patients whose severe hypertriglyceridemia has an identifiable, single-variant genetic contributor.

**The Angiopoietin-like Protein (ANGPTL) Axis in Dyslipidemia: Mechanisms, Cardiovascular Risk, and Emerging Therapies.** Bitencourt CS, Zimerman A, Marston N. *Current Atherosclerosis Reports*, 2026. [PubMed 42435249](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42435249/) | [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1177/2047487319871733)
A mechanism-to-clinic review of the ANGPTL3-4-8 axis as a regulator of lipoprotein lipase activity. The authors summarize the human genetics case for ANGPTL3 and ANGPTL4 loss-of-function variants lowering both triglycerides and coronary artery disease risk, and walk through how monoclonal antibody and RNA-based ANGPTL3 inhibitors lower triglycerides, remnant cholesterol, LDL-C, and apoB largely independent of the LDL receptor pathway — relevant to why these drugs work in patients whose LDL receptors are otherwise compromised. Useful as a current-state map of where the ANGPTL3/4 drug class stands mechanistically relative to the APOC3-targeted agents (olezarsen, plozasiran) covered in past cycles.

**Hypoxia drives ANGPTL4 expression in tumor-associated macrophages and promotes intrahepatic cholangiocarcinoma intrahepatic metastasis.** Lin J, Hua Y, Huang W, et al. *Cancer Letters*, 2026. [PubMed 42214805](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42214805/) | [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.canlet.2026.218614)
Not a lipid-disease paper, but a mechanistic ANGPTL4 study worth flagging for anyone tracking the target's biology outside metabolism. Using single-cell sequencing of 15 tumor samples plus a 255-patient transcriptomic cohort, the authors show that hypoxic tumor-associated macrophages upregulate ANGPTL4 via the transcription factor CEBPB, and that macrophage-derived ANGPTL4 then signals through Integrin α5β1 on cancer cells to promote metastasis in intrahepatic cholangiocarcinoma. It's a reminder that ANGPTL4 has tissue-specific, context-dependent roles well beyond lipoprotein lipase inhibition — relevant background for anyone thinking about on-target safety signals for ANGPTL4-directed lipid drugs.

## What to watch

The next real catalysts are Arrowhead's SHASTA-3/4/5 Phase 3 sHTG data for Redemplo (plozasiran), expected later this year, and any read-through from Ionis on early Tryngolza sHTG-indication uptake now that the label expansion has had a few weeks in the market. On the science side, Hegele's carrier-state review is a useful reminder that genetic testing strategy for severe hypertriglyceridemia needs to account for monoallelic FCS-gene carriers, not just classic biallelic FCS — a population that could grow as a target for both approved and pipeline apoC-III/ANGPTL3 therapies.
