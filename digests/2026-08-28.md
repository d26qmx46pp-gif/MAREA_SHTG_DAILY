**Angiopoietin-like protein 3 complete and partial deficiency markedly accelerates apolipoprotein B48 and B100 metabolism in triglyceride-rich lipoproteins in humans.**
Marcello Arca, Elias Björnson, Laura D'Erasmo, et al. *Journal of Internal Medicine*. [PMID: 42273920](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42273920/) | DOI: [10.1111/joim.70124](https://doi.org/10.1111/joim.70124)

This is a kinetic-labeling study in humans with inherited ANGPTL3 loss-of-function — 3 complete-deficiency homozygotes, 4 heterozygotes, and 10 matched controls — tracing apoB48 and apoB100 through chylomicrons, VLDL, IDL, and LDL under post-prandial conditions. Chylomicron and VLDL production rates were unchanged, but complete ANGPTL3 deficiency caused dramatically faster lipolysis: circulating residence times dropped from hours to minutes, with accelerated conversion of VLDL straight through to IDL and LDL. The resulting LDL particles were triglyceride-enriched, cholesterol-depleted, and cleared unusually fast. Heterozygotes showed a milder version of the same pattern with otherwise normal IDL/LDL kinetics. For anyone tracking ANGPTL3-directed drugs (evinacumab, and the antisense/siRNA candidates behind it), this is useful mechanistic grounding for what "full pathway inhibition" actually looks like in lipoprotein flux, and for anticipating on-treatment LDL compositional shifts.

**Macrophage TREM2 is a triglyceride-rich lipoprotein receptor that undergoes shedding in prediabetic hepatic steatosis.**
Jingjing Tang, Jenny Kanter, Baohai Shao, et al. *JCI Insight*. [PMID: 42658581](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42658581/) | DOI: [10.1172/jci.insight.202675](https://doi.org/10.1172/jci.insight.202675)

Working across human cohorts with prediabetes/hepatic steatosis and mouse models, the authors show that plasma soluble TREM2 tracks with APOC3-rich triglyceride-rich lipoproteins (TRLs), and that macrophage ADAM17 sheds full-length TREM2 off the cell surface in the setting of hepatic steatosis. Losing that shedding (preserving full-length TREM2) protected mice against elevated APOC3, dyslipidemia, and atherosclerosis, while TREM2 deficiency made things worse. Mechanistically, intact macrophage TREM2 appears to help clear TRLs directly, limiting hepatic APOC3-rich particle output. It's a new node connecting hepatic steatosis, macrophage biology, and APOC3-driven dyslipidemia — worth watching as a potential biomarker (sTREM2) or target adjacent to existing APOC3-lowering approaches.

**Dual-functional poly(disulfide) enables "Once-and-for-all" atherosclerosis therapy via precision hepatocyte base editing.**
Xiaoping Zhang, Shijuan Gao, Danyang Chen, et al. *Biomaterials*. [PMID: 42214211](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42214211/) | DOI: [10.1016/j.biomaterials.2026.124333](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.biomaterials.2026.124333)

A materials-science/gene-editing paper rather than a clinical one, but directly relevant to the ANGPTL3 target: the authors built a hepatocyte-targeted, cytosol-penetrating polymer that delivers adenine base editors to durably edit the ANGPTL3 gene in vivo after a single dose. In a mouse atherosclerosis model, one treatment produced sustained LDL-cholesterol lowering and reduced plaque burden. It's early (mouse-only, LDL-C readout rather than triglycerides), but it's a preview of where in vivo base editing of ANGPTL3 could head as a potential one-and-done alternative to chronic antisense/siRNA dosing — a space Verve and others are already pursuing for PCSK9 and Lp(a).
