### 1. Olezarsen for Hypertriglyceridemia: A New Therapy Update with Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis

**Khan N, Shahid H, Choudhry HHA, Irshad M, et al.**
*Cardiology in Review* | Published June 16, 2026
[PMID 42298338](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42298338/) | [DOI: 10.1097/CRD.0000000000001341](https://doi.org/10.1097/CRD.0000000000001341)

This meta-analysis of eight randomized controlled trials evaluated the safety and efficacy of olezarsen — Ionis' antisense oligonucleotide targeting apolipoprotein C-III — in hypertriglyceridemia. Across trials, olezarsen produced a placebo-adjusted mean triglyceride reduction of roughly 58.6% (95% CI: −63.1 to −54.0; I² = 13%), a remarkably consistent effect for a lipid-lowering agent. Reductions in apoC-III itself were even larger (mean −69.8%, 95% CI: −75.8 to −63.8%). Secondary lipid parameters including apolipoprotein B and non-HDL cholesterol also improved significantly. The authors used the RoB 2.0 tool for bias assessment and the GRADE framework for evidence quality, lending methodological rigor to what is now the most comprehensive efficacy synthesis of olezarsen. Safety was not flagged as a concern across trials.

**Why it matters:** This study arrives two weeks before the FDA's June 30 PDUFA date for Tryngolza (olezarsen) in severe hypertriglyceridemia and provides a pooled evidence anchor that clinicians and payers will likely cite. The near-zero heterogeneity in TG reduction (I² = 13%) is unusually tight for a lipid-lowering meta-analysis, strengthening confidence in the effect estimate.

### 2. Angiopoietin-like Protein 3 Complete and Partial Deficiency Markedly Accelerates ApoB48 and ApoB100 Metabolism in Triglyceride-Rich Lipoproteins in Humans

**Arca M, Björnson E, D'Erasmo L, Di Costanzo A, et al.**
*Journal of Internal Medicine* | Published June 11, 2026
[PMID 42273920](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42273920/) | [DOI: 10.1111/joim.70124](https://doi.org/10.1111/joim.70124)

This elegant kinetic study examined apoB48, apoB100, and TG metabolism across chylomicrons, VLDL1, VLDL2, IDL, and LDL in three ANGPTL3 loss-of-function (LOF) homozygotes, four LOF heterozygotes, and ten matched controls — all under post-prandial conditions. The defining abnormality was not altered production rates but dramatically accelerated lipolysis: chylomicrons and VLDL had circulating residence times of minutes rather than hours in LOF homozygotes, with nearly complete ANGPTL3 absence (undetectable plasma levels vs. ~110 ng/mL in controls). LOF heterozygotes (~45 ng/mL ANGPTL3) showed intermediate effects. LDL particles in LOF homozygotes were TG-enriched and cholesterol-depleted, and VLDL-to-IDL-to-LDL conversion was markedly accelerated. Production rates of chylomicrons and VLDL were similar across groups, firmly placing the locus of action on catabolism, not secretion.

**Why it matters:** ANGPTL3 is the key LPL inhibitor that pharmacological strategies — evinacumab, zodasiran, and others — aim to neutralize. This is one of the most detailed human kinetic portraits of ANGPTL3 deficiency ever published, confirming that the molecule's primary physiological role is to brake post-prandial TRL clearance. The graded effect between homozygotes and heterozygotes informs dose-response expectations for partial pharmacological ANGPTL3 inhibition.

### 3. Severe Hypertriglyceridemia Managed in a Pregnant Patient Using Nonstatin Therapies

**Roy R, Altenberg M, Faulkner S, Dickens L, et al.**
*JACC: Case Reports* | Published June 12, 2026
[PMID 42283676](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42283676/) | [DOI: 10.1016/j.jaccas.2026.108753](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jaccas.2026.108753)

This case report from a cardio-obstetrics multidisciplinary team describes a 34-year-old woman with type 2 diabetes and severe hypertriglyceridemia (HTG) who was referred at 16 weeks of gestation. Most modern lipid-lowering agents — including fibrates and, critically, emerging APOC3 and ANGPTL3 inhibitors — cannot be used during pregnancy due to fetal safety concerns. The team managed her with omega-3 acid ethyl esters, insulin (which lowers TG via LPL activation), and intensive lifestyle modification, achieving meaningful TG reduction over four months. The paper emphasizes preconception risk stratification, selective use of nonstatin agents, and the central role of a multidisciplinary care model.

**Why it matters:** As the SHTG drug landscape expands rapidly, the pregnancy data gap for new-generation TG-lowering agents (siRNA, ASO) becomes more clinically pressing. This case underscores that for the foreseeable future, pregnant patients with severe HTG face a treatment-option desert — a gap that future label development and pregnancy registries for olezarsen and plozasiran will need to address.

### Context: FDA Decision on Tryngolza (Olezarsen) in sHTG — June 30 PDUFA Date

**BioPharma Dive** | *context piece — published January 7, 2026, updated March 30, 2026*
[Source: 5 FDA decisions to watch in Q2 2026](https://www.biopharmadive.com/news/fda-approval-decisions-watch-2026/808684/)

The FDA is scheduled to decide by June 30 whether to approve Ionis' Tryngolza (olezarsen) for severe hypertriglyceridemia — the broader, more commercially significant indication beyond its initial FCS label. Ionis' Phase 3 CORE and CORE2 studies showed placebo-adjusted TG reductions of 55–73% over six months, alongside significant reductions in acute pancreatitis events. In late March, Ionis revealed its planned annual list price for sHTG: $40,000 per patient — a 93% cut from the FCS price of ~$595,000 and a strategic undercut of Arrowhead's Redemplo at $60,000 per year. The launch is positioned as Ionis' first medicine targeting a broad patient population and a critical step toward the company's 2028 break-even target.

**Why it matters for SHTG / lipid programs:** If approved on schedule, June 30 marks the first time a dedicated RNA-based therapy is commercially available for the large, underserved sHTG population (TG ≥500 mg/dL). Arrowhead's competing SHASTA-3/4 phase 3 data are also expected this month, setting up a head-to-head pricing and access battle before the end of Q2.

### Context: Arrowhead's SHASTA-3/4 Phase 3 Data — Imminent Readout

**Arrowhead Pharmaceuticals** | *context piece — expected mid-2026*

Arrowhead's two pivotal sHTG trials, SHASTA-3 (NCT06347003) and SHASTA-4 (NCT06347016), both had estimated primary completion dates of June 2026. Earlier Phase 2b open-label extension data showed 83% median TG reduction with plozasiran in sHTG, with 96% of patients achieving TG below 500 mg/dL and no adjudicated acute pancreatitis events over two years. Phase 3 results, if positive, would trigger an NDA submission and potentially set up Arrowhead to compete head-to-head with Tryngolza in sHTG before year-end. Pricing is already set at $60,000/year for Redemplo (established from the FCS launch), creating the unusual scenario of the newer entrant being the pricier option in sHTG.

**Why it matters for SHTG / lipid programs:** The SHASTA-3/4 readout is among the most anticipated lipid trial results of 2026. A strong showing would confirm that the APOC3 siRNA platform can match or exceed the ASO benchmark, create competitive pressure on both Ionis and payers, and establish plozasiran's position in a nascent but rapidly growing rare metabolic disease market.

## What to watch

The next two weeks are unusually loaded for the SHTG field. The science this cycle reinforces two converging narratives: that APOC3 inhibition has now earned robust meta-analytic validation (the olezarsen pooled data), and that ANGPTL3's primary physiological contribution is throttling post-prandial TRL clearance — a mechanism now under pharmacological exploitation across several programs. On the industry side, the June 30 FDA clock is ticking for Tryngolza in sHTG, and Arrowhead's Phase 3 data could drop any day. When those SHASTA results land, watch whether plozasiran's safety and efficacy profile diverges meaningfully from the Ionis benchmark — particularly on thrombocytopenia, LDL effects, and duration of TG lowering. The pregnancy management gap highlighted in the JACC case report is also worth tracking as both companies build out post-marketing commitments.
