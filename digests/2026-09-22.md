**Hypertriglyceridemia and atherosclerosis in Apolipoprotein A5 mutant rabbits generated using CRISPR/Cas9**
Zha Y, Feng L, Zhang T, Yan K, Wang X, Cheng Y, et al. *Journal of Lipid Research*, Sept 21, 2026.
[PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42767503/) · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jlr.2026.101151)

APOA5 is a key regulator of plasma triglycerides, but nearly all in vivo work has relied on mice, whose lipoprotein handling differs substantially from humans. This group used CRISPR/Cas9 to knock out APOA5 in rabbit zygotes, producing founders with roughly 90% loss of plasma APOA5 protein. On a normal chow diet, the mutant rabbits developed a stable, atherogenic lipid profile — high triglycerides, low HDL cholesterol, an expanded VLDL pool, and reduced lipoprotein lipase and hepatic lipase activity — worse in males than females, alongside signs of low-grade systemic inflammation (elevated IL-1β, TNF-α, CCL2). After eight weeks on a high-fat, cholesterol-enriched diet, the animals developed severe dyslipidemia, extensive aortic plaque, and fatty liver. The upshot is a new large-animal model that more faithfully recapitulates human APOA5 deficiency than existing mouse models, giving the field a better platform for testing triglyceride-lowering therapeutics.

**Hypoxia drives ANGPTL4 expression in tumor-associated macrophages and promotes intrahepatic cholangiocarcinoma metastasis**
Lin Z, Hua H, Huang Y, Li X, Liang Y, Yi T, et al. *Cancer Letters*, Sept 2026.
[PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42214805/) · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.canlet.2026.218614)

This one is outside the usual cardiometabolic lane but worth flagging for anyone tracking ANGPTL4 biology broadly. Using single-cell sequencing across 15 tumor samples plus a public cohort of 255 patients, the authors show that hypoxic regions of intrahepatic cholangiocarcinoma tumors induce macrophages to make ANGPTL4 via the transcription factor CEBPB. Macrophage-derived ANGPTL4 then acts on tumor cells through an integrin/FAK/Src/β-catenin pathway to promote invasion and metastasis, and blocking ANGPTL4 reduced metastasis in animal models. It's a reminder that ANGPTL4 signaling extends well beyond lipoprotein lipase inhibition — the same protein is being explored as a cancer biomarker and target in a completely different context.
