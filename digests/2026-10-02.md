**1. Durability of CRISPR-Cas9 Gene Editing Targeting ANGPTL3 with CTX310**
Laffin LJ, Nicholls SJ, Scott RS, Clifton PM, Koops R, Sarraju A, et al. (14 authors; Nissen SE senior author per sponsor). *N Engl J Med*, Oct 2026 (Correspondence).
PMID: [42663980](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42663980/) · DOI: [10.1056/NEJMc2609825](https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMc2609825)
This letter reports one-year follow-up on all 15 people in the Phase 1a dose-escalation trial of CTX310, an LNP-delivered CRISPR-Cas9 therapy that knocks out hepatic *ANGPTL3* in a single IV course. The cohorts covered HoFH, HeFH, sHTG and mixed dyslipidemia. At the top dose (0.8 mg/kg lean body weight), the mean reduction from baseline at one year was 79% for circulating ANGPTL3 (max 89%), 48% for triglycerides (max 78%) and 53% for LDL (max 84%). The sponsor reported no new treatment-related adverse events during extended follow-up and no ≥Grade 3 transaminase changes. The earlier safety findings still stand: one allergic reaction, three Grade 2 infusion reactions and one transient aminotransferase rise. Note that the letter was released online Aug 28 alongside ESC 2026 and is indexed this cycle with the October print issue. PubMed has no abstract, so the figures above come from CRISPR Therapeutics' [Aug 28 press release](https://ir.crisprtx.com/news-releases/news-release-details/crispr-therapeutics-presents-phase-1a-data-ctx310r-demonstrating/). Phase 1b is testing a flat dose equivalent to 0.8 mg/kg, and the company expects an sHTG-focused update in 2H 2026.

**2. A prognostic model based on metabolic indicators for recurrent acute pancreatitis in hypertriglyceridemia patients: a retrospective cohort study** *(next-tier journal)*
Wang Z, Liu Y, Ren P, Zhu G, Zhang G, Zhang X, et al. *Ann Med*, 2026.
PMID: [41978916](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41978916/) · DOI: [10.1080/07853890.2026.2653904](https://doi.org/10.1080/07853890.2026.2653904)
This single-center Chinese cohort followed 293 patients with HTG-associated acute pancreatitis (2019–2023), and 21.5% had a recurrence within two years. The independent predictors were visceral adipose tissue index, triglyceride-glucose index, smoking and drinking. The combined nomogram had an AUC of 0.927, but it was validated only internally (bootstrap and cross-validation). The model is hypothesis-generating. If it holds up, it points to visceral adiposity and insulin resistance, not just peak TG, as drivers of who comes back.

**3. Hypertriglyceridemia: Causes, Consequences, Diagnosis, and Management** *(next-tier journal; review)*
Qi S, Chen W, Chen K, Yang S, Wang S, Ni T, et al. *MedComm (2020)*, Oct 2026.
PMID: [42820004](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42820004/) · DOI: [10.1002/mco2.70956](https://doi.org/10.1002/mco2.70956)
This review proposes a two-axis framework for managing HTG: residual ASCVD risk, driven by apoB, TG-rich lipoproteins and remnants, and acute pancreatitis risk, driven by chylomicronemia. It argues that the TG value alone doesn't define risk or tell you which drug to use. It also surveys the APOC3- and ANGPTL3-targeted agents. It's useful for orientation but has no new data.

## What to watch

The CTX310 one-year data put a one-time option next to the repeat-dose ANGPTL3 and APOC3 drugs. A 48% mean TG reduction at the top dose is well below the roughly 80% median reductions plozasiran posted in SHASTA-3/4. So the open question for SHTG is whether gene editing's appeal (no adherence burden) can make up for a shallower TG effect, and the sHTG cohort readout from CTX310 Phase 1b later this year should be the first real test. On the commercial side, the next catalysts are Arrowhead's sHTG sNDA submission and the Q3 earnings season (late October to early November), which will give the first full quarter of Tryngolza sHTG launch numbers.
