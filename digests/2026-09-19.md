## Part A — SHTG / lipid science (PubMed)

**CRISPR therapy for dyslipidemia.** Nicholls SJ. *Current Opinion in Lipidology*, Sept 18, 2026. [PMID 42755348](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42755348/) · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1097/MOL.0000000000001062)

This narrative review surveys where gene- and base-editing therapies for dyslipidemia stand in early clinical testing. The author notes that in-human editing of ANGPTL3 and PCSK9 has already produced meaningful, durable reductions in both LDL cholesterol and triglycerides, with tolerability so far limited to mild-to-moderate infusion reactions. The appeal is durability: a one-time (or infrequent) edit could sidestep the adherence problems that limit chronic injectable or oral lipid therapies. The review is appropriately cautious, framing this as early-phase promise rather than a near-term practice change, and flags that these approaches will need real outcomes data before they can move beyond boutique use. Useful as a bird's-eye view of where editing-based ANGPTL3 suppression sits relative to RNAi and antisense approaches already in the clinic.

**Efficacy of plasma exchange and exchange transfusion in the clinical management of severe hypertriglyceridemia in emergency.** Stefanutti C, Zeppa G, Perrone G, et al. (MIGHTY MEDIC collaborative). *Molecular Genetics and Metabolism Reports*, Sept 12, 2026. [PMID 42756739](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42756739/) · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ymgmr.2026.101353)

A retrospective, multinational case series of 21 SHTG patients — adults, children, pregnant women, and one neonate — treated with therapeutic plasma exchange (TPE) or exchange transfusion (ET) during acute presentations, most with acute pancreatitis. Sixteen of the pancreatitis patients received TPE/ET and had rapid, substantial drops in triglycerides and lipoprotein levels alongside symptomatic improvement, with the authors arguing these apheresis techniques outperform standard TG-lowering drugs for the acute, rapid-lowering phase of a crisis. The authors are appropriately modest about the evidence tier here: this is an uncontrolled, retrospective series, so it confirms feasibility and a plausible role for apheresis in acute SHTG crises rather than establishing superiority over medical therapy.

**Declines in Plasma Erythritol and Improvements in Cardiovascular Risk and Atherogenic Lipid Profiles: A 2-Year Randomized Weight-Loss Dietary Intervention Trial.** Heianza Y, Rood JC, Champagne CM, et al. *American Journal of Clinical Nutrition*, Sept 18, 2026. [PMID 42759771](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42759771/) · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ajcnut.2026.101528)

A secondary analysis of the POUNDS Lost weight-loss RCT (n≈800) finds that higher baseline plasma erythritol (a sugar-alcohol sweetener increasingly linked to cardiovascular risk) tracked with higher estimated 10-year ASCVD risk, and that people whose erythritol levels fell most over the first 6 months had the largest improvements in ASCVD risk score at 2 years. The secondary lipid finding most relevant here: erythritol declines were specifically associated with reductions in apoC-III-containing VLDL/LDL cholesterol — tying a dietary/metabolic exposure to the same apoC-III axis that olezarsen and plozasiran target pharmacologically. Interesting hypothesis-generating signal on diet-driven apoC-III modulation, though it's observational within an RCT rather than a randomized test of erythritol reduction itself.
