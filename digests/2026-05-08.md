**Plozasiran prevents recurrent acute pancreatitis in very severe hypertriglyceridemia — post hoc analysis of PALISADE (Phase 3)**
Loomba R, de-Madaria E, Afghani E, Singh VK, Leeper NJ. *Aliment Pharmacol Ther.* 2026 Apr.
[PubMed PMID: 41923352](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41923352/) · [DOI: 10.1111/apt.70623](https://doi.org/10.1111/apt.70623)

In 67 PALISADE participants with median baseline triglycerides above 2,000 mg/dL and at least one prior episode of acute pancreatitis, quarterly subcutaneous plozasiran (an APOC3-directed siRNA) cut median TG by more than 75%, bringing them below 500 mg/dL. Recurrent acute pancreatitis occurred in 5/22 placebo participants vs. 2/45 plozasiran-treated participants — an 83% relative risk reduction (HR 0.17, 95% CI 0.03–0.87, p = 0.017). Severity, abdominal-pain hospitalizations, and length of stay also trended lower with plozasiran. This is the first prospective randomized evidence that an APOC3-silencing therapy reduces recurrent pancreatitis in familial chylomicronemia syndrome and adjacent extreme HTG phenotypes — a meaningful clinical signal beyond the surrogate of TG.

**Promise of ANGPTL3 as a therapeutic target for controlling cholesterol levels**
Hegele RA. *Expert Opin Ther Targets.* 2026 May 7. (Review)
[PubMed PMID: 42095752](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42095752/) · [DOI: 10.1080/14728222.2026.2671686](https://doi.org/10.1080/14728222.2026.2671686)

Hegele synthesizes a decade of ANGPTL3 work — antibody (evinacumab), antisense (vupanorsen), siRNA (zodasiran, solbinsiran), and now in vivo genome editing (VERVE-201, CTX310) — and pushes back on framing ANGPTL3 as primarily an LDL-C target. He argues that, outside homozygous familial hypercholesterolemia, the meaningful effect of ANGPTL3 inhibition is on triglyceride-rich lipoproteins and remnant cholesterol, with LDL-C lowering as a secondary, context-dependent consequence whose magnitude tracks with baseline hypertriglyceridemia. The framing matters for trial design and for how upcoming gene-editing readouts should be interpreted.

**Associations of ANGPTL3/4/8 proteins and complexes with heart failure and heart failure mortality**
Silbernagel G, Maas AC, Li H, Lemen D, Chen YQ, Zhen EY, et al. *Lipids Health Dis.* 2026 May 7.
[PubMed PMID: 42098711](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42098711/) · [DOI: 10.1186/s12944-026-02973-8](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12944-026-02973-8)

In 2,394 LURIC participants followed nearly 10 years with dedicated immunoassays for individual ANGPTLs and their complexes, ANGPTL3 and especially C-terminal-domain ANGPTL4 (CD-ANGPTL4) were positively associated with both HFrEF and HFpEF, with NT-proBNP and NYHA class, and inversely with LVEF. CD-ANGPTL4 also showed the strongest association with heart-failure death; ANGPTL3/8 and ANGPTL4/8 complexes did not predict HF mortality. This is one of the cleanest human datasets to date suggesting ANGPTL biology — particularly the C-terminal fragment of ANGPTL4 — extends beyond LPL inhibition into heart-failure pathophysiology, raising both biomarker and target-validation questions for an already crowded ANGPTL pipeline.

**ApoC-III inhibitors in hypertriglyceridemia — network meta-analysis of RCTs**
Elbahloul MA, Gamal A, Maihoub O, Elbaz Hassan MM, Attia MK, Salah AM, et al. *Diabetes Obes Metab.* 2026 Mar 31.
[PubMed PMID: 41917770](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41917770/) · [DOI: 10.1111/dom.70717](https://doi.org/10.1111/dom.70717)

Frequentist network meta-analysis of 15 RCTs with 3,934 patients comparing volanesorsen, olezarsen, and plozasiran. Olezarsen 80 mg every 4 weeks ranked highest for TG lowering (MD −63.3%), but the headline finding is class-level: APOC3 inhibition reduced acute pancreatitis with a pooled HR of 0.16 (95% CI 0.07–0.33), without a significant increase in serious adverse events. Cardiovascular outcome trials remain the open question, but the pancreatitis signal is now consistent across compounds and platforms. Read together with the PALISADE post hoc above, the APOC3 class story for FCS and very severe HTG is becoming hard to argue with.

**ApoC-III inhibition: from regulatory approval to equitable implementation**
Gaudet D, Larouche M, Stroes ESG. *Nat Rev Cardiol.* 2026 May. (Perspective)
[PubMed PMID: 41876702](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41876702/)

A perspective from three of the field's most experienced FCS clinicians on what comes after FDA/EMA approval of APOC3-targeted therapies: diagnostic gaps in identifying patients with FCS and persistent chylomicronemia, payer pathways, and the gulf between approved-on-paper and reach-the-patient. Useful framing for anyone thinking about real-world implementation of olezarsen and (anticipated) plozasiran.

---

**What to watch.** The week's signal is that APOC3-directed therapy now has prospective randomized evidence — not just biomarker evidence — for the outcome that matters most in severe HTG: pancreatitis. Expect the full PALISADE pancreatitis story to anchor regulatory and reimbursement discussions for plozasiran, and expect ANGPTL3 framing to shift further toward remnant cholesterol as evinacumab non-FH data and gene-editing readouts (VERVE-201, CTX310) accumulate. The CD-ANGPTL4/heart-failure signal in LURIC deserves a replication cohort — if it holds, it complicates the "inhibit ANGPTL4 freely" narrative coming out of LPL biology.
